Doulos: Bondservant For Christ

“Though I am free — I do not want to go away from you”

Dr. Annie Smith

Titus 1:1 • Harvest Intercontinental Church, Olney • August 24, 2026

The Text

"Paul, a servant of God, and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of God's elect, and the acknowledging of the truth which is after godliness."

Titus 1:1 (KJV)

“A simple verse — but very profound in its content.” The word translated servant is doulos.

The Rare Passage

“I love this passage because it’s one of the rare passages where Paul calls himself a servant. All over his epistles he brings the revelation of our sonship.”

When he encountered the glorified Christ he received the mind of Christ post-resurrection, which introduced us to the closest relationship with the Father — He is our Father, we are His sons, we have direct access, we speak to Him as a Father.

And Yet

Here he calls himself a servant. In Ephesians 4:1 he calls himself a prisoner. The son does not stop being a son — he adds something by his own choosing.

From “Do to Be” to Simply Being a Son

“Before I met my husband I was caught up in a ‘do to be’ mentality, serving the Lord because I was trying to obtain something from Him.”

The Turn

“When I met him, I understood that God just wanted me to be the son that He has always desired. I understood that He has a particular focus on me. He gazes on me. I don’t need to try to be something, to serve in order to have something. I just need to be the son that God loves. And I grew from that foundation.”

Part 1

What a Doulos Actually Was

The bond that was chosen, not imposed

The Servant Who Would Not Leave

Long before racial slavery there was indentured servanthood: a man served a fixed term of six or seven years, and at the end of it he was free to walk away.

And some would not. At the moment of release they offered their master their loyalty and their love:

“Though I am free, I want to continue to be under you. I serve you now with my family, with all that I have. I do not want to go away from you — though I am free.”

Two Servants, Two Hearts

Onesimus — who ran

He fled before his debt was paid, before the years were finished, “because that slavery for him was unbearable.” (Philemon 1:10-11)

Paul — who stayed

“Though You made me free — praise God — I willingly, voluntarily offer my service, my family. I don’t want to go.”

This is total commitment to the Father — and it is servanthood without the notion of bondage.

Chosen, Separated — and Now Persuaded

"But when it pleased God, who separated me from my mother's womb, and called me by his grace, to reveal his Son in me..."

Galatians 1:15-16 (KJV)

Paul understands his apprehension and his separation — and then goes above it: “I was chosen, I was separated, but now I have to respond.”

More Than Intention and Status

“Though I have Your intention, though I have Your status as a son and Your function — by persuasion I commit myself to be Your bondservant, and it is without the notion of bondage. I do it with all my life, willingly, because I love You.”

The Paradox

There Is No Other Freedom

“There is no other freedom than the freedom to serve willingly.”

This kind of service — “that the world would call slavery or bondage, but that I call freedom” — can only be done by the faith of the Son of God.

The Double Movement of Grace

1. We receive

“I am a bondservant through the faith of the Son of God that has been given to me” — not by my own performance, not by my own qualification.

2. We participate

“Now I, through my own participation in the faith of God, choose to acknowledge what leads to transformation — and give my life to that.”

“I have received the gift of God — but now I have become the gift of grace to other men. I not just receive the calling; I become the call for the sake of the salvation of others.”

Part 2

Saved by His Life

Why service is God’s method of saving us

Reconciled by His Death, Saved by His Life

"For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life."

Romans 5:10 (KJV)

Two Movements Again

“I received from God when I was still a sinner — He died for me. That is the first movement of grace. But now I participate by His life. I have been saved by His life.”

The Central Truth

Service Is Not Something You Give God

“The truth about service is that it is God’s method of salvation for us. Service is not us giving something to God — it’s God’s method of saving us alive.”

And when a believer’s understanding grasps that truth, it leads to transformation — and the result of transformation is commitment to others.

The System That Steals Your Focus

“At the fall of Adam there was a system introduced to the world that works against our soul. And the purpose of that system is to take away our ability to focus and to be saved by His life.”

It pulls your focus, your attention and your soul toward “things that don’t really matter and don’t really lead to our salvation.”

God’s Counter-Method

“The way God saves us by His life is by keeping our focus — in acknowledging His truth, His message — and by keeping our commitment in His service.”

The Days I Am Most Distracted

“I’ve often said — there is not a day where I found myself with my mind completely distracted like those days where I find myself idle, without service.”

The alternative is exhausting: days “so much occupied by the reasonings, the rationales, trying to figure out everything, trying to understand how things can work out for your best.”

The Verdict

“Unless God has worked it out by His life in us, the conclusion of what we are aiming at will not be salvific for us.”

What You Minister, Ministers Back

“When you minister to someone, or when you give, or whenever you commit yourself intentionally — an intentional ministration, an intentional phone call to someone — it is, at the end of the day, ministration for you. Those who know, know.”

  • “I give a call to someone and I say, how are you? What can I pray for? — and the life of God that I minister to that person begins to minister to me.”
  • “I intentionally go for evangelism — not as a request or a church duty — and therein I’m working on my own transformation.”
  • “The best way I begin my day, or end my day, or keep my soul renewed and strengthened, is to go towards someone.”

How Do You Say That From Prison?

“We’re talking about someone that was in prison. Why do you say you’re saved by His life when you’re in prison — in a condition that doesn’t permit you to really enjoy the proper life in the flesh?”

The Answer

Ministration to others. “I have no choice in this context but the giving away of myself for the life.”

And there service becomes free — without constraint, and with joy.

Serving to Be Seen, or Serving to Live

The old mentality

Serving because someone is looking at us — because someone is going to gratify us, or appreciate us.

Now we are serving to live

“Now we are serving to live. Praise God — because we have understood what Paul understood, and it’s a way out of that which works around us and against us.”

The Secret of the Inner Man

"For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day."

2 Corinthians 4:16 (KJV)

“The more you get busy with yourself, the more you’re wearing out. The secret of not allowing the life of this body to wear out is to be renewing the inner man — and I am renewing the inner man as I go towards someone, allowing that life to be actively working in me.”

Lay Hold of That for Which You Were Apprehended

“I was working before I was called to full-time ministry, and I didn’t want to live that slave mentality where you work and — as the word says — in vain do you go to bed in the night, and in vain you eat the bread of sorrow.” (Psalm 127:2)

“You run for the next miracle, the next promotion, the next thing that’s going to complete your bank account — and then you go back home and you sleep in vain.”

God Does Not Constrain

“God gives us opportunity every day to be renewed, and every day is a free opportunity. God’s method is a choice — He doesn’t constrain. He calls, He apprehends; but it is our own responsibility to lay hold of that for which we were apprehended.” (Philippians 3:12)

Part 3

You Are Always Participating in Something

The science of grace: we receive, we participate

How Do You Participate in the Christ in You?

“It’s not by saying ‘Christ is in me, thank you.’ Yeah, it is good — but that’s called communion. That’s called fellowship.”

When Paul thanks the Philippians for their fellowship, he is naming their giving as participation. “By giving, you participate in the fellowship.” (Philippians 1:5; 4:15-16)

Daily, Not Occasional

“So that participation should be our daily life. That’s what Paul said: I am a doulos. I am a bondservant. This I cannot escape.”

Nothing in This World Is Empty

“From the moment I quit that mindset of voluntarily allowing my mind to be used by God for the sake of others, from that moment I am exposed to decay. And decay begins in the mind.”

“Because there is nothing empty in this world. If there’s nothing in your mind, something else will be in your mind. So if you don’t have this idea of participating constantly in Christ, you participate in something else. You always participate in something.”

The Warning

“God forbid that we should participate in death. God forbid that our daily participation should be in those things that are corrupted, that have no salvific purpose, that will not lead to our transformation. A transformed life is one who accepts to consistently participate.”

The Clarification

What Are We Actually Participating In?

“We are not participating in Bishop Johnson’s vision. We are participating in the life of Christ. We are not participating in a church — we are participating in the life of Christ in us.”

“This is how that life is actively operational in us. This is how we are being saved — because this true, intentional participation will continue to fill us with the Holy Spirit.”

The Same Spirit That Raised Him

"But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you."

Romans 8:11 (KJV)

What That Spirit Does

“If that same Spirit that raised Jesus Christ from among the dead abides in us and continues to fill us — He will heal. He will transform. He will restore. He will strengthen. He will transfigure this mortal body. Glory be to God.”

He Has No Other Mind but Yours

"Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus."

Philippians 2:5 (KJV)

“Christ can only powerfully, greatly operate in you if He has your mind — because your mind is the only mind that Christ has. He doesn’t have another mind apart from yours.”

So Recognize the Pull

Money. A situation at work. A family situation. A situation with the children. “I know the secret — the purpose is to take me away from my participation. And I will not be an Onesimus. I will not run.”

Onesimus Goes Back — Differently

Paul writes to Philemon: receive him as a son — no longer as a servant. Take away his debt. He bears witness of his transformation.

“So when Onesimus went back to Philemon after he met Paul, he understood: this time I’m not forcefully constrained. I’m going to experience my freedom — but in that freedom it’s going to be a bond. It’s not going to be servanthood; it’s not going to be bondage. It’s going to be a sonship service.”

The Difference

“A son serves with joy in the house of his father, while a slave serves to receive compensation.” A son continues to grow in joy because he knows — he knows he has God’s full attention. He doesn’t seek God’s full attention.

Bishop Johnson’s Response

The Pipe Is Always Wet

“The pipe through which water flows is always wet. If the faucet is closed, the pipe will be dry. But if the faucet is open and water is flowing through that pipe in order to reach someone, that pipe will always itself be wet.”

“As the life of Christ flows through us to minister to others, we ourselves are being renewed, refreshed, rekindled, being saved — our mind, will and emotions. So you’re not only giving; you’re receiving. You should never, ever again need to have me say to you: serve.”

It Becomes Experiential

  • “When you’re praying for the salvation of others, His life is being ministered to you.”
  • “When you’re giving to support the work of God, you’re not only being a channel to bring salvation to the world — that very act is allowing salvation to come to you.”
  • “It’s no longer just potential — it becomes experiential as we minister.”

“Somebody needed to hear this — because somebody was complaining, somebody was getting tired, somebody saw service as a burden.”

Prayer Points

  • Father, deliver me from the “do to be” mentality — let me stop serving to obtain from You, and rest in being the son You have always desired (Titus 1:1)
  • Thank You that You gaze on me — I do not have to seek Your attention, because as a son I already have it (Galatians 4:6-7)
  • Take me beyond intention, status and function into persuasion: by choice and by love, I commit myself to You as a bondservant (Titus 1:1)
  • Teach me the freedom that the world calls bondage — for there is no other freedom than the freedom to serve willingly (1 Corinthians 9:19)
  • Let my service flow from the faith of the Son of God and never from my own performance or qualification (Galatians 2:20)
  • Having received the gift, make me the gift — let me become the call for the sake of the salvation of others (2 Corinthians 5:18-19)

Prayer Points

  • Save me by Your life, Lord — not only reconciled by Your death, but daily saved as Your life works through me toward someone (Romans 5:10)
  • Guard my focus from the system that pulls my soul toward what does not matter and cannot save (Colossians 3:2)
  • Keep me from idleness — the days I serve no one are the days my mind is most distracted (Hebrews 6:11-12)
  • Make my ministration to others become ministration to me: as I call, pray, evangelize and give, renew my own inner man (2 Corinthians 4:16)
  • Free me from serving to be seen, gratified or appreciated — teach me to serve to live (Galatians 1:10)
  • Since nothing in me stays empty, fill my mind with constant participation in Christ, that decay finds no place (Philippians 2:5)
  • Let me participate in the life of Christ — not merely in a vision, a program, or a church (Philippians 1:5)
  • Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead, keep filling me — heal, transform, restore, strengthen, and quicken this mortal body (Romans 8:11)
  • When money, work, or family pressures pull at my attention, hold me fast: I will not be an Onesimus; I will not run (Philippians 3:12)
Declaration

I Am a Son — and Willingly a Bondservant

Father, I am Your son by Your initiative, and today, by persuasion, I choose to be Your bondservant. Though I am free, I do not want to go away from You. I serve You with all that I have — not to obtain, not to be seen, not to be compensated, but because I love You and because there is no greater freedom than the freedom to serve willingly. I will not be idle; I will go towards someone. As Your life flows through me to others, renew my inner man, fill me again with the Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead, and let my participation be in the life of Christ and nothing less. I refuse decay. I refuse to run. I am serving to live, and I am being saved by His life. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

Doulos: Bondservant For Christ — Dr. Annie Smith

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Occasion — Preached at Harvest Intercontinental Church, Olney, during the church's 36th anniversary, by Dr. Annie Smith of Cameroon, ministering alongside her husband Dr. Shawn Smith. She opens by honoring Bishop Darlingston Johnson and First Lady Chrys: "God's love is measured by His grace gifts — how I know God loves me is because He gives gifted men to me."

The Central Message — From Titus 1:1, one of the rare places where Paul, the great apostle of sonship, calls himself a doulos. Behind that word lies indentured servanthood: a man who had served his term and was legally free, who then chose to stay. "Though I am free, I want to continue to be under you." That is servanthood without the notion of bondage — and there is no other freedom than the freedom to serve willingly.

The Turn — Service is not something we give God. Service is God's method of saving us alive (Romans 5:10): reconciled by His death, saved by His life. What you minister to another ministers back to you; the pipe through which water flows is always wet.

The Warning — Nothing in this world stays empty. The moment you stop letting your mind be used by God for others, you are exposed to decay, and decay begins in the mind. You are always participating in something.

The Testimony — Her own release came from leaving a "do to be" mentality: "I don't need to try to be something, to serve in order to have something. I just need to be the son that God loves."

Summary 1 of 5

Key Scriptures

Titus 1:1 — "Paul, a servant of God, and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of God's elect, and the acknowledging of the truth which is after godliness." The text: doulos, chosen freely, exercised by the faith of the Son and not by personal performance.

Ephesians 4:1 — Paul calls himself "the prisoner of the Lord": another self-description of a son who has voluntarily bound himself.

Galatians 1:15-16 — Separated from his mother's womb and called by grace. Paul understands his apprehension and separation, then goes beyond both into persuasion: "by persuasion I commit myself to be Your bondservant."

Philemon 1:10-16 — Onesimus ran because slavery was unbearable; after meeting Paul he returns transformed, received "no longer as a servant" but as a son. Freedom that becomes a willing bond — sonship service.

Romans 5:10 — "Reconciled to God by the death of his Son… saved by his life." The double movement of grace: we receive, we participate.

Philippians 1:5; 4:15-16 — Fellowship in the gospel: Paul names their giving as participation, not merely communion.

Philippians 2:5 — "Let this mind be in you" — Christ operates greatly in you only if He has your mind, "because your mind is the only mind that Christ has."

Philippians 3:12 — Lay hold of that for which you were apprehended: God calls and apprehends, but never constrains.

2 Corinthians 4:16 — The outward man perishes, the inward man is renewed day by day — and the renewal happens "as I go towards someone."

Romans 8:11 — The Spirit who raised Jesus dwells in us and quickens our mortal bodies: He will heal, transform, restore, strengthen, transfigure.

Psalm 127:2 — The vain rising and the bread of sorrow: the slave mentality the believer is not called to live in.

Summary 2 of 5

Main Teachings

1. Doulos is a chosen bond, not an imposed one — the indentured servant who, at the very moment of his legal release, offers his master his loyalty and love: "though I am free, I do not want to go away from you."

2. Sonship comes first, servanthood is added by choice — Paul, the apostle of sonship, calls himself a servant and a prisoner. The son does not lose his standing by binding himself; he expresses it.

3. Three stages: intention, status/function, persuasion — it is not enough to receive God's intention or the status and function of a son. Persuasion is the further step where the son commits himself.

4. It is done by the faith of the Son of God — this service cannot be produced by personal performance or qualification. "According to the faith of God's elect" removes every performance from his part.

5. The double movement of grace — we receive, we participate — having received the gift of God, "now I have become the gift of grace to other men." Not just receiving the calling, but becoming the call.

6. Service is God's method of saving us alive — not something we give God. Reconciled by His death, we are being saved by His life — and the mechanism of that saving is committed, focused service.

7. What you minister, ministers back to you — the intentional phone call, the prayer for a specific need, the evangelism that is not a church duty: "the life of God that I minister to that person begins to minister to me."

8. The inner man is renewed by going towards someone — "the more you get busy with yourself, the more you're wearing out."

9. You are always participating in something — nothing in this world is empty. If you are not participating in Christ, you are participating in something else, and decay begins in the mind.

10. A son serves with joy; a slave serves for compensation — the son grows in joy because he knows he already has God's full attention; he does not serve to seek it.

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

There is no other freedom than the freedom to serve willingly — what the world calls slavery, the bondservant calls freedom.

God's method is a choice — He calls, He apprehends, but He does not constrain. Laying hold of the calling remains our own responsibility.

Christ has no mind but yours — He can only operate greatly in you if He has your mind; that is why the fight is always for your attention.

Transformation follows acknowledgement of the truth — and the fruit of transformation is commitment to others.

Warning — the "do to be" mentality: serving in order to obtain something from God. It exhausts, because it seeks an attention already given.

Warning — idleness: "there is not a day where I found myself with my mind completely distracted like those days where I find myself idle, without service."

Warning — the fallen system: introduced at the fall of Adam, its purpose is to take away your ability to focus and to be saved by His life, pulling your soul toward what does not matter.

Warning — endless reasoning: days occupied with rationales, trying to work everything out for your own best. "Unless God has worked it out by His life in us, the conclusion of what we are aiming at will not be salvific for us."

Warning — decay begins in the mind: the moment you stop letting your mind be used by God for others, decay has an opening — because nothing stays empty.

Warning — serving to be seen: serving because someone is watching, will gratify you, or will appreciate you. Replace it with serving to live.

Warning — the running of Onesimus: money, work, and family pressures all aim to pull you out of participation. "I will not be an Onesimus. I will not run."

Summary 4 of 5

Practical Takeaways

Begin or end your day by going towards someone — her own practice: "the best way I keep my soul renewed and strengthened is to go towards someone."

Make the intentional phone call — ask how they are, ask what you can pray for, then actually pray. The life you minister will minister back.

Evangelize as intention, not as duty — not as a request from the church, but to bring someone to the truth that leads to transformation — "and therein I'm working on my own transformation."

Give as participation — giving is not a transaction with the church; it is fellowship in the life of Christ.

Audit what your mind is participating in — since it will never be empty, ask what currently fills it.

Name the distraction when it comes — money, job, family, children: "I know the secret — the purpose is to take me away from my participation."

Stop serving for compensation — a son already has the Father's full attention and does not need to earn a glance.

Do not wait to be told to serve — Bishop Johnson's charge on the response: "You should never, ever, ever again need to have me say to you: serve."

Declaration made over the house: "We declare that the enemy will not steal this word. We declare this word is taking root inside of us — renewing our minds, renewing our attitudes towards service, renewing our behavior. Thank You for the impartation and the transfer of life." And to the one who came in weary: "Somebody was complaining, somebody was getting tired, somebody saw service as a burden — and didn't understand that as you allow the Lord to work through you, you yourself are experiencing His life, and it's bringing salvation to you as well."

I did a little bit of research, and I discovered that Paul calls himself a bondservant because, before racial slavery, there was something that was called indentured servanthood — indentured slavery, if you want — where a man would willingly, after six or seven years of constrained service, willingly offer his loyalty and his love to his master. So he's speaking about a voluntary service where he gives his total commitment to the Father, and this servanthood is without the notion of bondage. As he said: I am not in bondage to serve. I choose. It's by choice — praise God — by choice and by love.

The Great Commission • August Focus

God Is Looking For Someone

“Whom Shall I Send, and Who Will Go for Us?”

Bishop Darlingston Johnson • Harvest Intercontinental Church-Olney

Isaiah 6:1-10 • August 10, 2026

In the Year King Uzziah Died

"In the year that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up, and the train of His robe filled the temple. Above it stood seraphim; each one had six wings: with two he covered his face, with two he covered his feet, and with two he flew."

Isaiah 6:1-2 (NKJV)

Holy, Holy, Holy

"And one cried to another and said: Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts; the whole earth is full of His glory!"

Isaiah 6:3 (NKJV)

Three Times — The Strongest Emphasis

In Hebrew, repetition maximizes the point. Jesus would say “Truly, truly” — but when you go three times, you cannot say it any stronger. What God wants to be known for in this passage — what He wants Isaiah to see and grasp — is that He is holy. The Lord of hosts: the Lord of angel armies.

The Place Shook

"And the posts of the door were shaken by the voice of him who cried out, and the house was filled with smoke."

Isaiah 6:4 (NKJV)

Every time a seraph cried out, the place shook. And the seraphim — who themselves had never sinned — covered their faces. Not because they were sinful, but because God is that holy.

Isaiah's Response

"So I said: Woe is me, for I am undone! Because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips; for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts."

Isaiah 6:5 (NKJV)

He sees the glory of God, the throne of God, the seraphim — and his response is: woe is me. I am undone.

Why the Lips?

Why didn't Isaiah say “my mind is unclean” or “my heart is unclean”? The focus on the lips suggests they are very important. And what makes lips unclean? Not the food you eat — the words we speak.

Pay Attention to What You Say

The Bible says if a man can control his tongue, he can literally control his life (James 3:2). Jesus said, “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34). Beautify your lips if you like — but one of the primary ways we make ourselves unclean is with our words.

The Coal from the Altar

"Then one of the seraphim flew to me, having in his hand a live coal which he had taken with the tongs from the altar. And he touched my mouth with it, and said: Behold, this has touched your lips; your iniquity is taken away, and your sin purged."

Isaiah 6:6-7 (NKJV)

The Question

God Is Looking For Someone

"Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying: Whom shall I send, and who will go for Us? Then I said: Here am I! Send me."

Isaiah 6:8 (NKJV)

Here Am I — Send Me

  • No argument. Here am I — send me.
  • No hesitation. Here am I — send me.
  • No list of questions. Here am I — send me.
  • No request for more time to think and pray about it.
  • No demand to know where he would be sent.
  • No inquiry — “What will it cost?”

Just: Here am I — send me.

What Made Him So Eager?

Why is Isaiah so eager to say yes when we struggle so often? We will interrogate God before we say yes. We will negotiate with God to make sure we have a good deal before we say yes.

But this man says yes with no questions and no negotiations. This is not a reluctant goer — this is someone eager to do what God wants done.

The Question for Us

What happened in Isaiah's life that needs to happen in ours, so we can respond the way he responded?

The Turning Point

Something Had to Die

The throne Isaiah saw was already there — always. The glory was present. The holiness was always there.

The problem was not that God was absent from His throne. The problem was: Uzziah was in the way.

The Only King He Knew

52 years on the throne

Isaiah was perhaps 20 to 25 years old. From the time he was born, the only king he knew was Uzziah — a great king.

Their savior, deliverer, protector

Isaiah looked to Uzziah for inspiration and security. And the fact that he was looking at Uzziah prevented him from seeing God.

What Are You Looking At?

“What are you looking at that is keeping you from looking up?”

The thing blocking your vision doesn't have to be sin. It could be a relationship you depend on more than you depend on God. Or a dream — not evil in itself — that occupies such a place in your life that it has greater influence on you than God does.

As long as that thing occupies the throne, your vision of God will be limited — and if you serve Him at all, you'll serve reluctantly, in a limited way.

The Throne Above Every Throne

Uzziah can die — and he does die

The earthly throne becomes empty. Everything you depend on down here can be here today and gone tomorrow.

But there is a throne above

Above Uzziah's throne is another throne, and on it sits the King who rules over kings — the God who cannot die, who governs and overrules in the affairs of men, whose kingdom is forever and ever and ever.

Let Uzziah Die

Church — take your eyes off Uzziah. In fact: let Uzziah die, so that you can see God — so you can come to know who God really is.

"Unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it produces much grain."

John 12:24 (NKJV)

The Revelation God Chose

The seraphim didn't cry “Powerful, powerful, powerful.” They didn't cry “Love, love, love.” What God wanted Isaiah to grasp was His holiness.

We rightly hold on to the love of God and the grace of God — never let them go. But there is a need for a greater revelation of the holiness of God: perfect in all His ways, free of sin, pure, holy — so holy that sinless seraphim cover their faces.

Conviction: The White Shirt

For five chapters Isaiah had prophesied “Woe are these people” — naming their sins. But when he saw God, it became “Woe is me.”

A white shirt in a dim room looks clean. Step into the sunlight, and spots and stains you never knew were there begin to show. Now you're in the light.

Compared to God, our own righteousness is as filthy rags (Isaiah 64:6). Seeing His holiness humbles you — and gives you a realistic perspective of how much you need grace.

Grace Shows Up

Thank God it doesn't end with “woe is me.” The seraph brings the coal: your guilt is removed, your iniquity forgiven. And just as the coal cleansed Isaiah, Jesus washed away our sins with His precious blood. Isaiah couldn't cleanse himself. You cannot cleanse yourself. But there is One who can — and He has.

"For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him."

2 Corinthians 5:21 (NKJV)

The Exchange

Grace Changes “Woe Is Me”
to “Here Am I”

If you've experienced the grace of God, what you ought to be saying now is “Here am I” — not “woe is me.” Christ has cleansed you. The blood of Jesus has washed you.

Amazing grace, how sweet the sound — I once was lost, but now I'm found.

Cleansed From — Cleansed For

There was conviction. Then there was cleansing. And then there was a calling.

God does not simply cleanse us from sin — He cleanses us for a purpose. Not just to sing songs in the temple: “I have a work that needs to be done. Of all those I have cleansed — whom can I send?”

"For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them."

Ephesians 2:10 (NKJV)

An Invitation, Not a Draft

God did not say, “Isaiah, this work is assigned to you — go do it.” He asked a question anyone who had been cleansed could answer: Whom can I send? His name wasn't on it. It just so happened Isaiah was the one eager to do it.

God doesn't draft you. You're not conscripts. “I could force you, but I'm not going to. I'm going to give you an invitation. It's up to you to say: I'm willing.” The only people God uses are those who are willing.

Yes — Without Knowing

  • Yes, without knowing where God would send him
  • Yes, without knowing what the message would be
  • Yes, without knowing how hard it would be
  • Yes, without knowing whether he would succeed
  • Yes, without knowing whether the people would listen or appreciate him
  • Yes, without knowing whether it would be comfortable, or how long the commitment would last

Many of us want all these questions answered first — and then we say, “Okay, let me pray about it.” Some of you have been praying about it for years. This isn't something to pray about — it's something to say yes to.

Stop Waiting for a Special Call

"The harvest truly is plentiful, but the laborers are few. Therefore pray the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into His harvest."

Matthew 9:37-38 (NKJV)

Billions don't know Christ — and you say, “Well, if You need me”? God doesn't care who you witness to, as long as you witness. You've got hands, a brain, a heart, strength, health, some money — and you're waiting, while there's work to be done?

Doing God a Favor?

Many feel they are doing God a favor — so they have to be begged, cajoled, persuaded, asked over and over again. But think about it: should the redeemed of the Lord have to be begged to say, “There's work that needs to be done — here am I, use me”?

A Culture of “Here Am I”

“I pray, Harvest, that the day will come soon when the culture of this church will be men and women always saying: Here am I. I present myself. Use me — people transformed by the revelation of God's holiness.”

A Privilege, Not a Punishment

The more you see God in His holiness, the more self-motivated you become to say, “God, here am I.” Unwillingness points to an insufficient revelation of how holy God is.

I, who was unclean — “woe is me” — now actually get to serve God, work with God, go on assignment for this holy God. Doing the work of God is not punishment. It's a privilege.

The Assignment Nobody Wanted

"Go, and tell this people: Keep on hearing, but do not understand; keep on seeing, but do not perceive."

Isaiah 6:9 (NKJV)

Isaiah may have hoped for a mission crowned with great success. Instead God says: don't expect a huge response. The majority will not listen, will not heed the warning, will not celebrate you. “Lord, how long?” — “Keep preaching until the assignment is done.”

The Doctor and the Smoker

Year after year the doctor warns: “Stop smoking, or this cough leads to cancer.” Year after year, nothing changes. Finally he says: “God bless you. Keep smoking, keep coughing — see you next year.”

The doctor isn't causing it. He's recognizing you don't plan to change. God is not making the willing unwilling — He had spoken to Israel for years: repent, worship, put away the idols. They heard, and never did anything about it.

Faithful Regardless of Results

“I've been preaching, and I will keep preaching — some of you didn't change five years ago, four years ago, three, two. I'm going to keep preaching, because this is what God called me to do.”

You're serving and serving, and it looks like nothing is happening. Hear me: you are not responsible for them — you're responsible for you. Remain faithful, whether or not anyone responds, until God tells you to do something else.

The Conclusion

Two Decisions

1. Let King Uzziah die

Surrender it, release it, let it go. There can only be one King in your life, one throne at the center. Let everything competing with God for preeminence go.

2. Stop waiting for an assignment with your name on it

There are needs in the kingdom and in the church. Find a need you know God has. Find a job you know God wants done, that you can do — and do it, in Jesus' name.

Prayer Points

  • Father, give me a fresh revelation of Your holiness, like Isaiah — Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts (Isaiah 6:3)
  • Show me every “Uzziah” in my life — every relationship, dream, or dependence blocking my vision of You — and give me grace to let it die (Isaiah 6:1; John 12:24)
  • Thank You that grace showed up: the blood of Jesus has cleansed me from all sin, and my “woe is me” has become “here am I” (Isaiah 6:7; 2 Corinthians 5:21)
  • Set a guard over my lips — let my words be clean, for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks (Isaiah 6:5; Matthew 12:34)
  • Make me eager, not reluctant — to answer “Here am I, send me” without argument, hesitation, or negotiation (Isaiah 6:8)

Prayer Points

  • Lord of the harvest, send out laborers — and let me be one of them; the harvest is plentiful, the laborers are few (Matthew 9:37-38)
  • Make our church a culture of willing servants — men and women who say “Here am I, use me” without being begged (Ephesians 2:10)
  • Keep me faithful when results are not encouraging — steadfast, immovable, always abounding in Your work (Isaiah 6:11; 1 Corinthians 15:58)
  • Father, save the billions who do not yet know Christ — You desire all to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth (1 Timothy 2:4)
  • Make me a doer of the word, and not a hearer only (James 1:22)
Declaration

Here Am I — Send Me

Today, by the grace of God and the power of the Holy Spirit, I choose to let Uzziah die, so I can see God and serve Him fully. Lord, I don't know what You want done — but here am I. I present myself. I am willing. Serving You is not a punishment — it is a privilege. Whom shall You send? Here am I — send me! In Jesus' name, Amen.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

God Is Looking For Someone — Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Central Message — From Isaiah's throne-room encounter (Isaiah 6:1-10), Bishop Johnson traces how a fresh revelation of God's holiness — not merely His love or grace — is what transformed a hesitant prophet into a man who answered God's call with “Here am I! Send me” and no negotiation.

The Diagnosis — Our reluctance to serve traces back to a “King Uzziah” — anything (a person, a dream, a comfort) we look at instead of looking up — that blocks our vision of God. Letting that Uzziah die is the doorway to eager, joyful obedience.

The Context — Preached during Harvest's August focus on the Great Commission, the message closes with a direct challenge: stop waiting for a personalized assignment and simply meet a need you can already see.

The Movement of the Text — The whole sermon follows Isaiah 6's structure: conviction (“Woe is me”) → cleansing (the live coal) → calling (“Whom shall I send?”) → commission (“Go, and tell this people”).

Summary 1 of 5

Key Scriptures

Isaiah 6:1-10 — The anchor text: the Lord high and lifted up; “Holy, holy, holy”; Isaiah's conviction and cleansing; God's question and Isaiah's answer; the hard commission.

Matthew 12:34 — “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” Why Isaiah's uncleanness is located in his lips: our words betray the true condition of the heart.

James 3:2 — If a man can control his tongue, he can literally control his life. The lips are a primary battleground of holiness.

John 12:24 — Unless a grain of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it abides alone. Applied to “let Uzziah die”: something must be surrendered before new fruit can come.

2 Corinthians 5:21 — He who knew no sin became sin for us. Grace in Jesus accomplishes what Isaiah's coal only pictured — full cleansing, so “woe is me” becomes “here am I.”

2 Corinthians 5:19 — “God was in Christ, reconciling the world to Himself, not imputing our trespasses.” Grace, not our effort, removes the guilt exposed by God's holiness.

Isaiah 64:6 — Our own righteousness is as filthy rags. Compared to God's holiness, even our best only produces “woe is me.”

Ephesians 2:10 — Created in Christ Jesus for good works. God cleanses us from sin so He can commission us for good works.

Matthew 9:37-38 — The harvest is plentiful, the laborers few. Exposes the gap between the size of the need (billions unsaved) and the smallness of the response.

James 1:22 — Doers of the word, not hearers only. Turns the whole message into an actionable commitment (closing prayer).

Summary 2 of 5

Main Points & Teachings

Holiness is the missing revelation — The seraphim cried “Holy, holy, holy” — not “Love” or “Powerful.” The triple repetition (Hebrew's strongest emphasis) shows holiness is what God most wanted Isaiah — and us — to grasp.

“What are you looking at that is keeping you from looking up?” — Uzziah reigned 52 years; Isaiah (20-25) had never known another king. His gaze was fixed so low he never saw the greater throne above Uzziah's.

“Let Uzziah die so you can see God” — God's throne was always there; what changed was that Uzziah's throne emptied. Every earthly Uzziah can and will die; God's throne never empties.

Conviction precedes cleansing — For five chapters Isaiah pronounced “woe are they” on others; seeing God turned the mirror: “woe is me.” Like a white shirt that looks clean in dim light until sunlight reveals the stains.

“Grace changes the 'woe is me' to the 'here am I'” — The live coal pictures what Christ's blood accomplishes: full removal of guilt, liberating a convicted sinner into a willing servant.

“He cleanses you FROM sin, but He cleanses you FOR something” — Cleansing opens into calling (Ephesians 2:10); it was never meant to end in worship alone.

“God doesn't draft you — He invites you” — God never conscripts. “Whom shall I send?” is an open invitation requiring a free, willing yes. Many kingdom needs have no name on them — they belong to whoever is willing.

Eagerness without pre-negotiation — Isaiah said yes without knowing destination, message, difficulty, outcome, cost, or timeline — a rebuke to interrogating or negotiating with God before obeying.

Serving God is a privilege, not a punishment — The clearer the vision of holiness, the greater the honor of merely being invited.

Faithfulness is not measured by visible results — Isaiah's commission was to preach to people who would not listen. “You are not responsible for them — you're responsible for you.”

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

Principle — A personal revelation of God's holiness — not merely doctrinal belief in it — is the primary catalyst for eager, unhesitating obedience.

Principle — Whatever competes with God for the throne of your life will, by definition, keep your vision of God limited and your obedience reluctant.

Principle — True conviction of sin comes by comparison with God's holiness, not by comparison with other people.

Principle — Every cleansing carries a corresponding calling: being made clean is preparation for being sent, not merely for feeling forgiven.

Principle — Obedience is evaluated by faithfulness to the assignment, not by visible fruit or audience response.

Warning — Beware an “Uzziah” occupying God's throne: a relationship depended on more than God, or a dream so large it dictates your pursuit of God rather than the reverse.

Warning — Beware turning obedience into years of “praying about it”: “This isn't something to pray about. This is something you need to say yes to the Lord about.”

Warning — Beware the “if You need me” posture — treating service as a favor done for God. “Should the redeemed of the Lord have to be cajoled, persuaded, pleaded with?”

Warning — The doctor and the smoker: persistent refusal to change in response to repeated warning eventually produces a settled, unresponsive heart. God's hard word in Isaiah 6:9-10 describes that pattern — it is not an arbitrary judgment.

Warning — Beware measuring obedience by results; disappointment with outcomes is not license to quit.

Summary 4 of 5

Takeaways & Declarations

1. Let your Uzziah die — Identify whatever relationship, dream, comfort, or dependency competes with God for the throne of your life; consciously surrender it so your vision of God is unobstructed.

2. Stop waiting for a name-tagged assignment — Find a need you can already see in the church or the world, and simply meet it.

3. Examine your speech — Take inventory of your words as a measure of your heart's condition (Matthew 12:34).

4. Receive the cleansing — If you have never received Christ, receive the cleansing pictured by the altar coal — through the blood of Jesus — before you can be commissioned.

5. Consecrate yourself — If you're a believer who has been hesitant to say “Yes, use me,” identify your personal Uzziah and consecrate yourself fully.

6. Stay faithful without applause — Keep serving even when the visible response is discouraging, “until God tells you to do something else.”

Prophetic prayer for the church — “I really pray, Harvest, that the day will come soon when the culture of this church will be the culture of men and women who are always saying, 'Here am I. I present myself. Use me' — people transformed by the revelation of God's holiness.”

Closing prayer — “We thank You that You have changed our 'woe is me' to 'here am I.' Like Isaiah, we pray that You give us a fuller revelation of Your holiness — that we might become humble before You, and consider serving You a privilege, and not a problem.”

Altar-call declaration — “Today, by the grace of God and the power of the Holy Spirit, I choose to let Uzziah die, so I can serve God fully.”

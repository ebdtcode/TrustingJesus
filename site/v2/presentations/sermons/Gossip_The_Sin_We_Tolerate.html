Rubber Meets the Road

Gossip: The Sin We Tolerate

“We must stop treating casually what God treats seriously.”

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Proverbs 6:16-19 • Romans 1:28-32 • September 2026

The Sins We Recognize

When Christians talk about sin, certain things come to mind: adultery, fornication, stealing, drunkenness, dishonesty. Those we treat as serious.

But gossip? We have become so desensitized to it that many times we don't even consider it a sin. It's under the radar. We think it's normal.

“Let's be truthful. If I was talking about adultery, you wouldn't be laughing. But we laugh about gossip.”

When Was the Last Time?

Three Questions

When was the last time you repented of gossip and confessed it to the Lord? When was the last time you asked the Lord to be merciful because of your gossip? When was the last time you said, “Lord, I did that. I never want to do it again”?

“Did you hear?” • “I'm only telling you so you can pray.” • “Don't tell anybody I told you.” • “I probably shouldn't say this, but...” • “I'd like to share this prayer request.”

We excuse it. We rename it. We even spiritualize it. But even though we have made small of this sin, God doesn't.

Part 1

How Seriously God Takes Gossip

Gossip is a lot more serious to God than we realize

Seven Things God Hates

"These six things the Lord hates, yes, seven are an abomination to Him: a proud look, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that devises wicked plans, feet that are swift in running to evil, a false witness who speaks lies, and one who sows discord among brethren."

Proverbs 6:16-19 (NKJV)

That's the Gossip

God puts sowing discord in the same category as shedding innocent blood and calls it an abomination. If something is an abomination to God, it ought to be an abomination to us. It shouldn't be treated small.

Why It Is an Abomination

God values unity among His people so much that anything we say or do that unnecessarily divides His people and breaks their unity, God considers an abomination.

Gossip is just one example. There are other ways to divide the church. Take this into your heart and mind: anytime you are dividing God's people, whether by gossip or some other way, you are doing something that God hates.

The Company Gossip Keeps

"God gave them over to a debased mind... being filled with all unrighteousness, sexual immorality, wickedness, covetousness, maliciousness; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, evil-mindedness; they are whisperers."

Romans 1:28-29 (NKJV)

Read the Whole List

We rightly connect this passage to the sins the world has embraced. But read it completely. “Whisperers”: some translations simply say “they are gossips.” That's what gossip is. It's whispering: “Don't let her know I told you.” Notice the company that gossip keeps.

God Holds Gossipers Accountable

"Whoever secretly slanders his neighbor, him I will destroy."

Psalm 101:5 (NKJV)

Thank God for the Blood of Jesus

The only reason we don't get destroyed is the blood of Jesus. But God is letting us know that gossip itself deserves that kind of judgment. We don't want to be connected with, engaged in, or guilty of something so serious in the eyes of God.

“God, help me to view gossip the way You do. Help me to hate it like You do. Help me to treat it as the serious sin it is.”

Stop Treating Casually What God Treats Seriously

Not merely a personality weakness
Not merely careless words
Not merely a bad habit

When something damages another person, or divides God's people, God treats it seriously. “Lord, help me to treat seriously that which You treat seriously.”

Part 2

What Gossip Is

A working definition, and the exceptions

A Definition

Gossip Is...

Sharing private or negative information about someone else, true or false, to someone else, without a legitimate reason, and without being reasonably certain that the person would be comfortable with you sharing it.

Usually behind their back, though it doesn't have to be. The test is simple: if they were to learn that you shared it, would they be okay with it?

Truth Is Not a License

The information doesn't have to be false to be gossip. Everything you say can be true, and it's still gossip. Just because something is true doesn't automatically give you the right to share it.

You learn about someone's medical condition, and it's accurate. Your motive isn't evil; you genuinely want prayer for them. But it is their private information. Before you announce it to the prayer meeting, ask: would this person be comfortable knowing I shared this?

If you're not sure, don't share it. Call them and ask: “I want to present this for prayer with the group. Is that okay?” If you don't know, don't assume.

Motive Is Important

“Did you hear? Sister Brown has a serious drug problem.” • “Don't tell anybody, but Brother So-and-so lost his job and his car was repossessed.” • “Don't ever lend her money. She borrowed $100 from me and hasn't paid me one penny.”

All of That Is True. But...

If I'm sharing it simply for conversation, entertainment, retaliation, or to make a person look bad, I am gossiping. “I just took 90% of the things that you talk about often.”

When the information is unverified or false and the intent is to damage a reputation, gossip crosses into slander.

Possession Is Not Ownership

A friend lends you his car. It's in your possession, but it doesn't belong to you. Would you lend it to someone else? No. Just because it's in your possession doesn't mean it belongs to you.

Treat someone's information the same way. Just because you possess information about a person doesn't mean you own it. And since you don't own it, you don't have the right to give it to somebody else without their permission, or without knowing it will be okay.

When Silence Is Too Costly

Now change the scenario. Someone nearby has a medical emergency and may die. The only vehicle is your friend's car; the only driver is somebody else. The general rule says don't lend it. But somebody's life depends on it. The cost of not allowing it is too high.

Sometimes information is like that. A married man posing as single is courting your friend. Someone ministering in the church is selling drugs, or living a sexually loose life, or spreading untrue rumors about leadership. If I don't act, the harm caused is too high.

Responsible Disclosure

The Only Reason

Not for information. Not for entertainment. Not to get back at the person. Not for any malicious reason. The only reason I share this is to prevent serious harm to somebody else, or to the group I'm part of.

Who? Only the person who has the ability, the responsibility, or the authority to do something about it. Not everybody.

How much? Only what relates to the problem. Not everything you know about the person.

Five Questions Before You Speak

  • Is it true?
  • Does it need to be said?
  • Does it need to be said now?
  • Does it need to be said by me?
  • Is this the person it needs to be said to?

If the answer is yes, then do it.

The Simple Test

The General Principle

Responsible disclosure may be necessary to protect someone from harm. But in general, don't share negative or private information about someone behind their back unless there is a legitimate reason, and the person you are speaking to can do something about it.

“If they're not part of the problem or part of the solution, they probably are not the person you should be talking to.”

Part 3

Why God Takes Gossip So Seriously

Because gossip is so destructive

Gossip Destroys Unity

"Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!" • "That they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You."

Psalm 133:1; John 17:21 (NKJV)

The Very Thing Christ Is Building

Unity is precious to God. Jesus prayed that His followers would be one. Christ builds unity, and gossip destroys the very thing He is building. You can't be one with someone you're gossiping about.

Gossip Is the Opposite of Love

Love is always seeking someone else's good (1 Corinthians 13). Love doesn't eagerly, unnecessarily expose someone's failures, weaknesses, and pain.

Love says

“How can I help?”

Gossip says

“Who else can I tell?”

When you gossip you are not walking in love. You are siding with the enemy, allowing him to bring discord and confusion into the work of God.

Words Are Small, Consequences Are Not

"Death and life are in the power of the tongue." • "See how great a forest a little fire kindles! And the tongue is a fire."

Proverbs 18:21; James 3:5-6 (NKJV)

"A whisperer separates the best of friends."

Proverbs 16:28 (NKJV)

Not casual friends. People who were tight for years, whose friendship survived a lot of pressure. Until one day a gossiper gossiped: “She was talking behind your back.” And while she says it, she is talking behind the back of the friend.

Gossip Destroys a Good Name

"A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches."

Proverbs 22:1 (NKJV)

“I'm turning 68 in a few days. What I've tried to do all my life is protect my name and my reputation.” Yet one gossiper can share something totally untrue, and a reputation built over years is taken away just like that. And people are so quick to believe.

Someone recently published our history on social media without ever asking us, and people thanked him as though he had the facts. Opinions are formed and attitudes developed because someone spread lies, or spread the truth in a way that was not helpful.

Verify Before You Speak

Since you know how precious your name is, don't do it to anybody. Isn't there something inherently wrong, in fact evil, about entertaining ourselves at the expense of somebody else?

Before sharing something as fact, make sure it's true. It's not true just because somebody told you. Many of you hold attitudes toward people based on things you heard and never verified.

Or you saw something real, then assigned a motive that had nothing to do with it. “The Bishop walked past me and didn't speak. He's proud now.” You don't know what kind of day I was having.

“That is something I never want done to me. Therefore, by the grace of God, I will not do it to somebody else.”

Part 4

Why Do We Gossip?

And what to do when it reaches you

Four Reasons We Gossip

1. It's tasty

“Gossip is so tasty. How we love to swallow it” (Proverbs 18:8). The flesh has an appetite for it, so you must intentionally resist it.

2. It's easy

It takes no effort and almost no courage. Throw the bomb and walk away. No confrontation, no consequence.

3. It's effective

You get back at someone and escape the price. You don't pay it; I do, or the other person does.

4. We're envious

It's a tongue problem because it's a heart problem. Out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks (Matthew 12:34).

Examine Your Heart, Then Repent

If there are particular people you always enjoy putting down, ask: What's wrong with my heart? Why am I enjoying this? People who gossip often compare themselves with others, and something in them wants to bring the other person down.

Repentance Is Not Just Feeling Bad

Repentance means I take responsibility, I acknowledge it's wrong, and by the grace of God I put a stop to it. And if you gossiped about somebody to someone who now has a bad attitude toward them, go to the person you gossiped to and correct it. That's repentance.

It Takes Two

For gossip to live, grow, and spread, it takes two: someone who likes to talk, and someone who likes to hear. If you started it, stop and say, “I'm sorry, I shouldn't have brought that up.” If you are the listener, you don't have to receive it.

If somebody brought garbage from their house and emptied it on your living room floor, how long would you permit it? “Come on in! Can I give you some tea while you pour your garbage on my floor?” No. “You can come in, but that stays out.” You don't have to receive garbage. That's what gossip is.

Redirect It or Remove Yourself

"Let no corrupt word proceed out of your mouth, but what is good for necessary edification, that it may impart grace to the hearers."

Ephesians 4:29 (NKJV)

One Question

Is this conversation building somebody up, or tearing down somebody who isn't here to defend themselves? Once you answer that, you know whether it needs to stop. Act before you become an accomplice.

“That person isn't here, so I don't think we should talk about them.” • “Have you spoken to them? Maybe that's what you need to do. Let's pray for them.” • “I'm not comfortable with this. I don't know the facts, and nothing positive will come of it.”

The Charge

Gossip Stops With Me

Gossip has been the sin we've tolerated for too long. Today, let's decide we tolerate it no more. Let's have a culture where people know: in this church, gossip is not permitted. The minute a conversation takes on the character of gossip, we redirect it or we remove ourselves.

You don't have to be rude, but you do need courage. Unity and love are too important to God for us to allow it to continue. If it reaches you, say: “This is where it stops. This is where it dies.”

Prayer Points

  • Lord, help me to view gossip the way You do: to hate it like You hate it, and to treat it as the serious sin it is, not a personality weakness or a bad habit (Proverbs 6:16-19)
  • Thank You for the blood of Jesus that keeps me from the judgment gossip deserves; I refuse to keep company with the whisperers (Psalm 101:5; Romans 1:29)
  • Forgive me for every time I shared private or negative information about someone without a legitimate reason, and for excusing, renaming, and spiritualizing it as a prayer request (Ephesians 4:29)
  • Teach me that possession is not ownership: I hold other people's information as a trust, and I will not give away what does not belong to me
  • Give me wisdom for responsible disclosure: to speak only to prevent serious harm, only to the person who can act, and only what needs to be said

Prayer Points

  • Protect the unity Christ is building in Harvest; let no whisperer separate the best of friends, and let me never side with the enemy against Your family (Psalm 133:1; John 17:21; Proverbs 16:28)
  • Search my heart and expose the envy and comparison beneath my tongue; heal the heart problem so the tongue problem stops (Matthew 12:34)
  • Give me the courage to go directly to a person with my questions rather than throwing a bomb behind their back, and the humility to correct what I have already spread
  • Guard the good name of every member of this church, and guard mine; let me never entertain myself at someone else's expense (Proverbs 22:1)
  • Like David, set a guard over my mouth and a guard over my ears, so that I neither speak gossip nor receive it; let it stop with me and die with me (Psalm 141:3)
Declaration

Gossip Stops With Me

Father, I will no longer treat casually what You treat seriously. I take responsibility for what I have done with my mouth and for what I have permitted to enter my ears, and I repent. From today I will not share private or negative information about anyone without a legitimate reason, because possession is not ownership and truth is not a license. When a conversation begins to tear down someone who is not there, I will redirect it or remove myself, courteously but with courage. I will not be an accomplice in something You hate. I choose love that asks “How can I help?” over gossip that asks “Who else can I tell?” Set a guard over my lips and over my ears. Gossip stops with me. This is where it dies. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sermon Summary

Gossip: The Sin We Tolerate - Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Central Message - Part of a series on the rubber-meets-the-road issues that affect relationships and the church's mission, the sermon confronts the one sin Christians have become desensitized to. We recognize adultery, stealing, and drunkenness as serious, but we excuse, rename, and even spiritualize gossip. God does not. He calls sowing discord among brethren an abomination (Proverbs 6:16-19) and lists whisperers alongside murder, strife, and deceit (Romans 1:28-32).

The Definition - Gossip is sharing private or negative information about someone, true or false, to someone else, without a legitimate reason and without being reasonably certain the person would be comfortable with it. Truth is not a license. Possession is not ownership. The only exception is responsible disclosure: speaking to prevent serious harm, only to the person who can do something about it, and only what needs to be said.

The Damage - God takes gossip seriously because it is so destructive. It breaks the unity Christ is building, it is the opposite of love, it separates the best of friends, and it destroys a good name that took a lifetime to build.

The Charge - We gossip because it is tasty, easy, effective, and rooted in envy. The answer is honest repentance, a guard on the mouth and the ears, and the courage to redirect or leave a conversation. “Gossip stops with me.”

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

Principle - What God treats seriously, His people must treat seriously. Familiarity with a sin does not make it small.

Principle - Truth does not automatically give you the right to share it. Consent and legitimate reason matter as much as accuracy.

Principle - Possession is not ownership. Information about another person is held in trust, not owned.

Principle - Disclosure is justified only to prevent serious harm, and only to the one who can act on it.

Principle - Gossip and love cannot coexist. Love seeks to help; gossip seeks an audience.

Principle - The tongue reveals the heart. A chronic gossip problem is a heart problem, usually envy.

Principle - Gossip needs a listener as much as a speaker. Refusing to hear it kills it.

Warning - Beware spiritualizing gossip as a prayer request. Concern for someone does not entitle you to broadcast their private matters.

Warning - Beware sharing true things for conversation, entertainment, retaliation, or to make someone look bad. That is gossip, and when unverified, slander.

Warning - Beware forming attitudes toward people from unverified hearsay or from motives you assigned to them.

Warning - Beware becoming an accomplice by staying in a conversation that tears down someone who is not there to defend themselves.

Warning - Beware listening to this message on behalf of someone else. Think about your own mouth and your own ears.

Summary 4 of 5

Takeaways & Declarations

1. Repent specifically - Name your gossip before the Lord as sin, not as a slip. Take responsibility and decide, with His help, to stop.

2. Ask before you share a prayer request - If you are not certain the person would be comfortable, call and ask. If you don't know, don't assume.

3. Check your motive - Before repeating something true, ask whether it is for conversation, entertainment, retaliation, or to make someone look bad. If so, keep it.

4. Use the five questions - Is it true? Does it need to be said? Now? By me? To this person? Speak only when every answer is yes.

5. Go direct - If you have a question about someone, have the courage to ask them rather than throw a bomb behind their back.

6. Correct what you spread - Where your gossip poisoned someone's attitude, go back to that listener and set it right.

7. Refuse the garbage - When a conversation turns to tearing down someone absent, redirect it gently or remove yourself. Not rude, but courageous.

8. Examine your heart - If there are one or two people you always enjoy putting down, ask why, and deal with the envy underneath.

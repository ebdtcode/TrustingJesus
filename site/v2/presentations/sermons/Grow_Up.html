Grow Up

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

1 Corinthians 13:11

A Word From the Lord

Following "Overcoming the Flesh by the Spirit"

Last week was about overcoming sin by the Spirit, not by our own strength. This week the word is simple: "I'm coming to you this day with the word of the Lord to say to you, it's time to grow up. You've been saved long enough."

"I want you to hear this not as my words but as the word of the Lord."

The Principal Text

"When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child; but when I became a man, I put away childish things."

1 Corinthians 13:11 (NKJV)

A Decision, Not a Drift

"The childish things didn't just leave Paul. When he became a man, he decided spiritually he would put away things that belong to his infancy."

Part 1

Baby Shoes

Why is a grown man wearing baby shoes?

Something Is Not Right There

  • Baby shoes are cute on babies. "But now I want you to imagine these shoes on your feet." A grown man in a suit, and we look down, and that is what he is wearing.
  • "Instinctively, nobody had to tell you anything. You would wonder if something went wrong, because as old as you are, you shouldn't be wearing baby shoes."
  • "Many of us in the Harvest family have been saved two years, three years, four years, ten years, and we look real funny, because spiritually we've got baby shoes on."
  • The grace period is one year. After one year of hearing, understanding, speaking, and reasoning by the Word, "you shouldn't be able to fit in those shoes anymore."

Say, "Baby Shoes"

"If I don't get my way, I'm leaving."

Baby shoes. You should have outgrown that by now.

"If nobody recognizes me, I'm not serving."

Baby shoes. "Nobody's calling me. Nobody's checking on me." Baby shoes.

"I don't feel like doing it."

If you are reasoning in alignment with the Word, you are not saying, I won't do it because I don't feel like it.

Feelings Versus Responsibility

  • "Babies are the ones who are controlled by their feelings. Babies are the ones who let their feelings determine what they will do."
  • "A mature adult can feel one way and still do what's right." He doesn't deny the feelings, "but recognizes there are things far more important than my feelings."
  • "Can you imagine, as a parent, if you were raising those kids based upon your feelings? You would be in jail."
  • You can feel unappreciated and still say, "That's my responsibility, and I'm going to take responsibility."
Part 2

Children Are Meant to Grow Up

Childhood is great, but it was meant to last only for a season

The Way It Works in Nature

1. We love children

"They're beautiful. They're wonderful. But we don't want them to remain children forever."

2. We want them to take responsibility for themselves

"You don't have to always be making them take a shower."

3. Then we want them to raise their own children

"Now you can spoil the grandchildren, because you know their parents will take care of them."

All of Us Are Spiritual Parents

  • "Having been in Christ for as long as I have been in Christ, I should now have raised up, reproduced, and nurtured children spiritually" who can take care of themselves and reproduce their own.
  • "That's the natural process, and that's the spiritual process." It is the way it is designed to work in nature, in the church, and in the kingdom of God.
  • "If you are not at that place where you are reproducing and nurturing the next generation of believers, you are wearing baby shoes."
  • "Where are you in this process? How long have you been a spiritual infant? Can you point to people that you are reproducing spiritually?"

A Season, Not a Lifetime

Childhood Is Not Permitted to Be Permanent

"There's a time when it's right, when you need to be the one being nurtured and the one being cared for, but that's for a season. It's not forever."

"By now most of us should be functioning as, thinking as, adults spiritually: responding to the church, responding to needs, responding to things in the kingdom as a spiritual adult."

Part 3

By This Time

You ought to be teachers

You Have Come to Need Milk

"For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the first principles of the oracles of God; and you have come to need milk and not solid food."

Hebrews 5:12 (NKJV)

Read It as Though He Is Talking to You

"You've attended enough church services. You've heard enough preaching and teaching. You've sung enough and worshiped enough. You have prayed enough. By this time, you ought to be the one teaching and training and equipping the next generation."

That Season Is Over

  • He is not even talking about the deep things, just "the first principles, the foundational principles." Yet "we've got to keep going over the same thing over and over and over."
  • "What I believe the Spirit of God is saying to Harvest Church is that the time for that has come to an end."
  • Make it personal: "The time for being a spiritual baby, wanting to be cared for, wanting to be encouraged, wanting to be prayed for, complaining if somebody doesn't call me, that season in my life is over."
  • "I'm going to change the way I think, the way I reason, the way I talk. I'm going to change my habits."
Part 4

Marks of a Spiritual Adult

Responsibility, sacrifice, perseverance, reproduction

Responsibility for the Mission

  • "One of the main differences between someone who's still an infant spiritually and one who is mature is that he is able and willing to accept responsibility for the mission of Christ."
  • "Children do not take responsibility for themselves. They don't take responsibility for others. They are a responsibility."
  • A grown believer has taken responsibility for the Lord's work, the Lord's church, and the Great Commission: "Go into all the world and make disciples."
  • "It is not Bishop Johnson's responsibility alone. I'm trying to be an adult. I refuse to wear baby shoes."

Friday's Training Meeting

"I Felt Sorry for Myself"

He looked at the attendance and thought, "What more can you do? They're not listening." Evangelist Floret came to shake his hand. "Brother, right now I need more than a handshake. I need a hug."

  • "If I was a spiritual child, I wouldn't be here today, because I would still be meditating on it, rehearsing it in my mind, and getting more and more upset."
  • "In spite of how I felt, I'm here this morning. And I still love you. And I still will spend time preparing, so that when you come, you can get fed."

Don't Leave It for Someone Else

  • "Don't be like the children who eat and then don't wash up, who don't clean behind themselves, because they're leaving it for their mother to come behind them."
  • "If I just let you not take responsibility for the church, for the Lord's work, and I don't say anything, I'm not doing you a favor. I'm failing as a parent."
  • "Whomever the Lord loves He disciplines. Every time I come with a word of correction, it won't be because I hate you. It'll be because I love you." (Hebrews 12:6)
  • "You show up, the lights are on. The place is clean. The staff is in place. Don't take that for granted. This is your house too."

Sacrifice Comes With the Job

"If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me."

Matthew 16:24 (NKJV)

Parents Know This

The father who didn't sleep well but gets up Monday and goes to work. The mother up at three in the morning with a sick child. "No parent can be a good parent if he or she is not willing to sacrifice for the sake of the child."

Time, Talent, and Treasure

Time

"My time belongs to me, I will give only the time I can spare" is the reasoning of a child. The adult says, "I don't feel like it, but I have a responsibility."

Talent

"We're doing the Lord's work together. We're taking care of His mission together, and all of us have our role to play."

Treasure

"I was planning for Hawaii, but right now this child needs to go to school." The same attitude belongs in the kingdom: "This work is ours. You're not doing me a favor."

"I'm Doing It Every Day"

  • "I don't know how much longer I'm going to be the pastor of the church. The sooner you guys grow up, the quicker I can, because I don't want to put someone else in and give them a lot of infants."
  • "When I step away, I'm not taking anything here. My name is not on the board of trustees. My name is not on any of these things."
  • "What I'm asking you to do, I'm doing it every day. Because I want to be faithful. I want to not be a child in the kingdom."
  • "No more sitting back and just letting things happen and feeling no sense of responsibility."

You Can't Quit

"And let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart."

Galatians 6:9 (NKJV)

  • "Just because you talk to them once doesn't mean they line up." Conversation after conversation, and they are still doing the same thing. "But you can't quit because of that."
  • "Making disciples requires perseverance. You can change your method, but you can't quit." Your labor is not in vain in the Lord. (1 Corinthians 15:58)

Commit These to Faithful Men

"And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also."

2 Timothy 2:2 (NKJV)

Four Generations in One Verse

"Timothy, I reproduced and nurtured you. Now it's your responsibility to reproduce and nurture the following generation," and that generation the next. "That's how the work of God will get done and the kingdom of God will expand."

Make a Disciple

  • "The Great Commission is not just become a disciple. The Great Commission is make a disciple." (Matthew 28:19)
  • The assignment is not that we become mature believers, though we should, but "that we become mature believers who are capable of and who are actually doing this reproducing and nurturing."
  • "We are responsible to make sure that when we are not here, there are a bunch of other folks who we have reproduced and nurtured to the point where they are now able to do the same for others."
  • "Have you taken responsibility for that? How are you going about it? It's going to cost us."
Part 5

Spend and Be Spent

Paul's attitude after he put away childish things

Gladly Spend and Be Spent

"And I will very gladly spend and be spent for your souls; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I am loved."

2 Corinthians 12:15 (NKJV)

Beyond the Money

"Spend" is money, resources, treasure. "Be spent" goes further: "I myself am willing personally to be spent. I'm willing to literally invest myself, sacrifice myself, give myself for you."

What God Calls Us to Give Ourselves For

  • "I know we give ourselves for so many other things. But what does God call us to give ourselves for? Souls. That's how you lay up treasure in heaven."
  • "Let's get beyond where it's all me and how I feel and what I want and what I should get and what I deserve, which all belongs to childish things."
  • "We're going to leave it all behind. So don't just be rich in this world. Make sure that as God blesses you, you understand that what matters to Him are souls."
  • Hand on your chest: "Lord, let this be true of me, that I might gladly spend and be spent for the souls of those Christ died for."

The Less I Am Loved

"Man, I'm a True Apostle Now"

Paul refused to let how people treated him, responded to him, or interpreted his behavior stop him. "I will gladly continue to be spent, if at the end of the day it's going to result in the salvation of your souls, and in you becoming a mature believer and not remaining a spiritual infant all your days."

"I don't want you to hear this and say, that's what the pastor does. I want you to hear this as God's vision for you, because you are also to be a spiritual parent."

The Charge

"I don't want to go to church today, but I'm going, because I've got a responsibility."
"I don't feel like giving, because I've got some needs, but I'm going to give, because I have a responsibility for souls."
"I'm not going to let the way people look at me impact my commitment to the Great Commission. That is how a mature believer speaks."

If You Just Got Saved

  • "Keep them on for now. You need to grow. You need someone to nurture you, encourage you, pray for you. That's one of the reasons God created the church, so that babies are not left on their own."
  • "Just refuse to let them stay on. Decide: my feet are going to get too big for these, because I don't intend to be a child for more than a year."
  • Listen to the Word again and again. Attend prayer meetings to learn to pray. Struggling with sin? "Ask someone to disciple me, to nurture me, until I'm able to do it for someone else."
  • "If you've been cared for enough that you can care for others, taught enough that you can teach others, now is the time to put on these shoes."

It Takes the Church

  • "We have a responsibility that requires sacrifice, steadfastness, and unity. We've got to do this together."
  • "Whether you like that statement or not, when it comes to discipleship, it takes the church. That's how God designed it."
  • "Don't talk about the church as though the church is separate or different from you. No, we are the church."
  • "Don't depend just on your willpower. Always learn to say, Holy Spirit, help me."

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

Grow Up - Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

Following last week's message on overcoming the flesh by the Spirit, Bishop Johnson brings what he calls "the word of the Lord" to Harvest: it is time to grow up. Holding up a pair of baby shoes, he shows how instinctively wrong it looks for a grown man to wear them, and says that many believers saved for two, four, or ten years are spiritually still wearing them. From 1 Corinthians 13:11 he argues that Paul's maturity was a decision, not a drift, and that every believer past a one-year grace period must make the same decision.

The diagnosis - Spiritual infancy shows in the way we speak, understand, and reason: "If I don't get my way, I'm leaving." "If nobody recognizes me, I'm not serving." "I don't feel like doing it." Babies are governed by feelings; adults by responsibility.

The standard - Hebrews 5:12: by this time you ought to be teachers. The mark of maturity is accepting responsibility for the mission of Christ, which brings with it sacrifice of time, talent, and treasure, perseverance, and the reproduction and nurture of the next generation (2 Timothy 2:2).

The goal - Paul's posture in 2 Corinthians 12:15: "I will very gladly spend and be spent for your souls; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I am loved." The sermon ends with a confession repeated three times, a call to unity, the Bishop's own story of sharing the gospel at IHOP, and a prayer of agreement.

Summary 1 of 9

Key Scriptures (1)

1 Corinthians 13:11 - "When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child; but when I became a man, I put away childish things." The anchor text. Childish things did not just leave Paul; he decided to put them away. Stop speaking, interpreting life, and reasoning like a spiritual baby.

Hebrews 5:12 - "For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the first principles of the oracles of God; and you have come to need milk and not solid food." Read twice aloud, the second time "as though he's talking to you." You have heard, sung, worshiped, and prayed enough to be the one teaching the first principles.

Matthew 16:24 - "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me." Deny yourself what? Time. You cannot be a spiritual parent if your time belongs only to you.

Matthew 28:19 - "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations." The Great Commission is not just become a disciple; it is make a disciple. Taking personal responsibility for it is the mark of adulthood.

Hebrews 12:6 - "For whom the Lord loves He chastens." Why the pastor keeps bringing correction: not because he hates you, but because he loves you and will not leave you a spiritual baby.

Summary 2 of 9

Key Scriptures (2)

Galatians 6:9 - "And let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart." Keep sowing into people who show no change. Parents who persevered for years finally saw the fruit.

1 Corinthians 15:58 - "Your labor is not in vain in the Lord." The promise behind perseverance in discipleship and soul winning.

2 Timothy 2:2 - "And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also." Paul reproduced and nurtured Timothy; Timothy must do the same for the next generation, and they for the next. That is how the kingdom expands.

2 Corinthians 12:15 - "And I will very gladly spend and be spent for your souls; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I am loved." The closing text and the goal for every Harvester: invest treasure, then invest yourself, regardless of how you are treated.

John 4:31-34 - Jesus, hungry at the well, told the disciples "I have food to eat of which you do not know." Sometimes the best way to be refreshed is to let the Lord use you to minister to someone else.

John 17:21 - "That they all may be one." The closing prayer of agreement asks for the unity Jesus prayed for, so the church can do this work together.

Summary 3 of 9

Main Teachings (1)

Baby shoes on a grown man - Nobody has to explain why it looks wrong. Spiritually, believers saved for years "look real funny" because they still have baby shoes on. One year is the grace period; after that, "those shoes don't fit the stage you should be in by now."

Maturity is a decision - Paul became a man and then decided to put away what belonged to his infancy. "Many of us are still wearing these shoes spiritually because we have not made the decision that Paul made to put them away."

Feelings versus responsibility - Babies are controlled by their feelings. "A mature adult can feel one way and still do what's right." He does not deny the feelings but recognizes there are things far more important. A parent who raised children by feelings "would be in jail."

Nature's pattern is the kingdom's pattern - We love children but want them to grow, to take care of themselves, and then to raise their own. Spiritually, after years in Christ you should have reproduced and nurtured believers who are now reproducing others. If not, "you are wearing baby shoes."

Childhood is for a season - It is right for a time to be nurtured and cared for, "but that's for a season. It's not forever." It is not permitted to be permanent.

Summary 4 of 9

Main Teachings (2)

By this time you ought to be teachers - The apostle in Hebrews 5 faced the same problem: believers who still needed the first principles. "What I believe the Spirit of God is saying to Harvest Church is that the time for that has come to an end." Change the way you think, reason, talk, and your habits.

The defining mark: responsibility for the mission - Children do not take responsibility; "they are a responsibility." A spiritual adult has taken personal responsibility for the Lord's work, the Lord's church, and the Great Commission. A forty-year-old should not be living off his parents; he should be providing for the next generation and caring for the old.

The pastor's example - Discouraged by Friday's training-meeting attendance, needing a hug from Evangelist Floret, he still prepared and preached. "If I was a spiritual child, I wouldn't be here today." That should be true of everyone saved more than a year, not only of the pastor.

Correction is love - Letting you avoid responsibility would be failing as a parent. "Every time I come with a word of correction, it won't be because I hate you. It'll be because I love you."

Don't take the house for granted - The lights are on, the place is clean, the staff is in place. "This is your house too. This is your work too. This is your responsibility too."

Summary 5 of 9

Main Teachings (3)

Sacrifice comes with the job - Parents sacrifice sleep, time, and money they do not have. "No parent can be a good parent if he or she is not willing to sacrifice." Jesus said deny yourself: deny yourself time, talent, and treasure. Hawaii waits because the tuition is due.

The next generation is not about age - "Somebody can be fifty years old and just got born again. He is part of the next generation."

This work is ours - Minister Trokon's offering reminder: this is not Bishop Johnson's. "You're not doing me a favor. We're doing the Lord's work together." The Bishop's name is not on the board of trustees or on any of the assets; what he asks, he does every day.

Perseverance - One conversation rarely works with children, and it rarely works in discipleship. People reject you the first time; the one you disciple has to be pushed to show up. "You can change your method, but you can't quit." In due season you will reap.

Reproduce and nurture - 2 Timothy 2:2 lays out generations: Paul, Timothy, faithful men, others also. "The Great Commission is not just become a disciple. The Great Commission is make a disciple." The assignment is to become mature believers who are actually reproducing and nurturing.

Spend and be spent - Beyond giving money, give yourself for souls. "That's how you lay up treasure in heaven." Paul kept going even as the more he loved, the less he was loved; so should every spiritual parent.

Summary 6 of 9

Principles

Growth is expected and measurable - Time saved should correspond to maturity. Childhood is a season, not a status.

Maturity is a decision - Childish things are put away deliberately, as Paul did; they do not fall off on their own.

Responsibility, not feelings, governs the adult - Feelings are real and need not be denied, but they are never the final authority.

Maturity is measured by what you take responsibility for - Not by how much you know, but by whether you own the mission of Christ.

Every believer is called to be a spiritual parent - The design in nature and in the kingdom is reproduction and nurture, generation after generation.

Responsibility costs sacrifice - Time, talent, and treasure. What is true of natural parenting is true of spiritual parenting.

Perseverance produces fruit - Methods may change, but quitting is not an option; labor in the Lord is not in vain.

Discipleship takes the church - The Great Commission is corporate, requiring unity, not a task carried by leadership alone.

Correction is an act of love - A shepherd who never challenges immaturity is failing as a parent.

Intentional prayer opens doors - Ask the Holy Spirit for opportunities before you leave the house, and expect them.

Summary 7 of 9

Warnings and Cautions

Against permanent infancy - Remaining a spiritual baby past the one-year grace period is a problem to be corrected, not a preference to be tolerated. "That is not good for you. That is not right before God."

Against feeling-driven behavior - "If I don't get my way, I'm leaving." "If nobody recognizes me, I'm not serving." "I don't feel like it." All baby shoes.

Against leaving the work to someone else - Like children who eat and leave the mess for their mother. The lights, the cleaning, the staff are not someone else's problem.

Against self-pity and discouragement - Poor attendance and feeling unappreciated are real; "maybe the devil was helping too." Rehearsing it in your mind only makes you more upset. Look past it to your responsibility.

Against quitting on people - Because they did not respond the first, second, or tenth time. Change your method, not your commitment.

Against being rich only in this world - "We're going to leave it all behind." Be rich in heaven by investing in souls.

Against relying on willpower - "Don't depend just on your willpower. Always learn to say, Holy Spirit, help me."

Against leaders who give the next pastor "a lot of infants" - The Bishop does not expect to pastor much longer and wants a grown church to hand over.

Summary 8 of 9

Takeaways and Declarations

Takeaways - Name the baby shoes you are still wearing and decide to put them away. Ask whether you can point to anyone you are discipling; if not, start, or get discipled until you can. Commit time, talent, and treasure, not just what is convenient. Persevere with the people who are not changing. Pray before you leave the house for a chance to share the gospel and watch for the ordinary opening. New believers: keep the shoes on for now, but listen to the Word repeatedly, attend prayer meetings, and ask someone to disciple you. Get up Sunday and come, call the sister, invest the time, because it is a God responsibility. Let past divisions stay in the past and work together.

Opening declaration - "I therefore declare today that I hear Your voice, Jesus, and I follow. I declare that every member of this church hears Your voice and follows."

Congregational responses - "Lord, I'm ready to take off those baby shoes." "Lord, I put away childish things." Hand on chest: "Lord, let this be true of me, that I might gladly spend and be willing to be spent for the souls of those Christ died for."

Confession, said three times - "Father God, I thank You. I am not called merely to be a disciple. I am called to become a mature disciple who is capable of and responsible for making other disciples, in Jesus' name. Amen."

Prayer of agreement - That we might be one as Jesus prayed; that what has divided us will be part of our past; repentance for letting feelings keep us from our responsibility; grace to be what He has called us to be. Then: hug one or two people and say, "Let's work together. Let's be one. Let's get this job done."

Forgiveness & Reconciliation

I Forgave, But Must I Reconcile?

The Wound Has Healed — But the Scar Remains

Bishop Darlingston Johnson • Harvest Intercontinental Church-Olney

Numbers 12:1-3 • Ephesians 4:32 • Matthew 6:12 • July 19, 2026

A Facilitator of Learning

At the Frontline Ministers Alliance conference, Bishop Johnson was reminded: "Your role is not just to be a teacher — you are called to be a facilitator of learning."

An Interactive Sermon

Sometimes preaching is the right medium. Other times, a discussion is a better way to facilitate learning. Today — still a sermon, still teaching — but we interact, ask questions, and hear one another.

Recap: Meekness Is Not Weakness

The Fireplace — fire let loose burns the house down; fire under control does great good. Meekness is strength under control.
The War Horse — a powerful animal that submits to the gentle direction of its rider.
The Surrender — "Pride always has another argument; meekness always has another surrender." I bring my emotions under the authority of God's Word.

Moses: The First Mention of Meekness

"Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth."

Numbers 12:3 (KJV)

An Explanation, Not a Compliment

Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses behind his back over his Ethiopian wife. Moses had the power to retaliate — but his meekness explains why he overlooked it, forgave them before they even asked, and let God defend him.

God Hears What Is Said Behind the Back

  • Miriam and Aaron were leaders too — yet they talked behind Moses' back, "just like some of you talk behind my back"
  • The question is not that we talk — it is what we are saying: is it something you want God to hear?
  • God heard, God did not approve, and God considered it serious enough to confront them
  • Whether before the face or behind the face — God is hearing
The Question

A Member's Text

"It truly felt like a mirror, but also a loving reminder that there is still business I need to take care of in my own heart…"

"Does forgiveness always have to lead to reconciliation?"

"There are people I have genuinely forgiven… But I can't see myself ever sitting across from them for a meal or inviting them back into my life."

When the Wound Heals but the Scar Remains

The scar means healing has taken place — the wound is no longer open, no longer bleeding. The body has done its work.

But the scar is also a reminder that something did happen. You don't have amnesia.

The Real Question

"The scar no longer hurts the way it once did, but it still tells the story of what happened. Is it possible to forgive someone and still maintain healthy distance and boundaries?"

What Is Forgiveness?

"And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."

Ephesians 4:32 (KJV)

The Definition

To forgive is a decision I make to no longer require you to repay me for what you took from me. I cancel the debt. I take the case out of court. I turn it into my gift to you.

The $1,000 Debt

You lend $1,000. The promised day passes. No payment, no explanation. Every time you see the person, you see the money — until you don't even want to see their face.

Now you are the one feeling all the anger, all the pain.

Then you decide: "I'm going to release it. I'm going to cancel that debt. I was thinking about suing you — I'm not going to sue. In fact, I'm going to let it become a gift to you. Take it, and God will bless me."

Three Truths About Forgiveness

1. It Is a Decision

An act of the will — not a feeling. You release the debt and take it out of court.

2. It Takes Only One Person

You alone, without any help from the offender, can forgive. They don't even have to acknowledge the wrong.

3. It Is Commanded

As Christians we don't have the option not to forgive — "even as God in Christ has forgiven us." God said it. That's enough.

Unforgiveness Blocks the Flow of Grace

Electricity flows through copper — but rubber blocks it.

There are things that are conduits for grace, and things grace cannot flow through. Unforgiveness is one of them.

Good Ground for the Devil

Unforgiveness blocks grace from where it needs to go — and gives the devil room to do what he wants to do. It can really hinder your prayers.

Stop Victimizing Yourself

A pastor told of a colleague who hurt him so badly he planned revenge. At a social event, the offender was enjoying himself, sipping his wine — while the pastor stood there raging, angry, unable to enjoy anything.

"It is not that person anymore that's victimizing you. You are victimizing yourself" — by nursing the memory until you feel all the pain over and over again.

Give yourself the gift of forgiveness by giving that person the gift of forgiveness.

Forgiveness Does Not Deny the Facts

"But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good…"

Genesis 50:20 (KJV)

  • Joseph forgave his brothers — but he still said, "You did me wrong"
  • You don't have to pretend it didn't happen or call wrong right
  • Acknowledge the fact and the hurt — then, as an act of your will, release them

Then Pray for Them

"But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you…"

Matthew 5:44 (KJV)

Feelings Don't Negate the Decision

You may not feel like it — go that way anyway. When you can genuinely pray for those who hurt you, that's a good sign you have forgiven. It doesn't mean the feelings have caught up yet.

The Distinction

Reconciliation Takes Two

Forgiveness

One person • A decision • Commanded • Unconditional • Done for God and for yourself

Reconciliation

Two persons • A process • Requires repentance • Requires rebuilt trust • Takes time — and may never happen

Restoration cannot be done by one person. Both have to be willing to fix the relationship.

Some Relationships Require Reconciliation

  • Husband and wife: you can't say "I forgive you, but I'll never sit at the table with you again" — that marriage requires reconciliation to survive
  • If you can't fix it between the two of you, bring in a third person to help — the marriage must be fought for
  • But someone ten states away you rarely deal with? Forgiveness may be all the relationship requires
  • It depends on the nature of the relationship — not one size fits all

The Knife, the Wound, and the Boundary

If someone cuts you with a knife, you can forgive — take it out of God's court, refuse to press charges, genuinely pray for them.

But while the wound is still there, you will be most protective around the person who cut you. How do you know they won't do it again?

If there has been no repentance and no rebuilding of trust, it is wise — not unforgiving — to keep reasonable boundaries.

Three Boundary Cautions

1. Don't Punish the Wrong Person

When someone accidentally brushes the unhealed wound, don't treat them like the one who wielded the knife. Old wounds can make innocent friends pay for someone else's offense.

2. Don't Build 100-Foot Walls

If three feet will protect you, don't make it a hundred — a boundary so big there is no hope of ever being reconciled.

3. Don't Make Distance a Policy

Too many people will hurt you unintentionally. If every hurt ends a friendship, you'll have boundaries all over — and miss people who really love you.

It's Okay to Say Hello

  • Saying hello does not re-establish the relationship — and they can't hurt you because you said hello
  • If you're praying for someone, you're wishing them well — so you can talk to them
  • Set the boundaries that are reasonable and necessary — but keep loving people
  • "Let's not include more things in the boundaries than we need to"

The Best Time to Forgive

"And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors."

Matthew 6:12 (KJV)

Pre-Forgive

"The best time to forgive is to forgive in advance. The next best time is to forgive now." Jesus taught us to proclaim at the start of the day: I have already forgiven — because in the next 24 hours, somebody is going to say something.

Never Say Never

John Mark and Paul broke up so badly that Paul refused to have him on the team (Acts 15:37-39). That same John Mark later became "profitable to me for the ministry" (2 Timothy 4:11).

Moses was not naturally meek — he killed an Egyptian. Yet God testified he became the meekest man on earth. God can change anybody.

"For now, no. But don't say never. Since God doesn't give up on people, we should not." The one who hurt you can become your most loyal, most faithful friend.

God's Perfect Will Is Unity

"Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace."

Ephesians 4:3 (KJV)

Boundaries Are Not the Goal

It is not God's will for us to have boundaries all over the place. They are necessary at times — but they don't reflect His perfect will, which is that we dwell together in love and unity. Stay open to what God may do in your heart, and in theirs.

Prayer

Prayer Points

  • Lord, search my heart — show me anyone I have not yet released, and give me grace to cancel the debt today (Ephesians 4:32)
  • Father, let no unforgiveness block the flow of Your grace in my life or hinder my prayers
  • Teach me to pre-forgive — to begin each day having already forgiven, as Jesus taught (Matthew 6:12)
  • Heal my wounds fully, so I stop making innocent people pay for old scars
  • Give me wisdom to set boundaries that are reasonable and necessary — and no bigger (James 1:5)
  • Keep my heart open to the God of the impossible, who restored John Mark and transformed Moses — let me never say never
Declaration

My Declaration

I choose to forgive — not because they deserve it, but because God said it.

I cancel the debt. I take the case out of court. I turn it into my gift.

I will pray for those who hurt me, even before my feelings catch up.

I will set boundaries with wisdom — but I will keep loving people.

And I will never say never — because the God who changes anybody is still God.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

I Forgave, But Must I Reconcile? — Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview & Key Scriptures

Overview — Following the message on meekness, Bishop Johnson turned a member's text message into an interactive teaching on the difference between forgiveness and reconciliation. Forgiveness is a commanded, one-person decision to cancel the debt; reconciliation is a two-person process requiring repentance and rebuilt trust. Wise boundaries while wounds heal are not unforgiveness — but they must be reasonable, and the door must never be closed on what God can do.

Numbers 12:1-3 — Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses over his Ethiopian wife; Moses, the meekest man on earth, did not retaliate — he overlooked and forgave before they even asked, and God defended him. First biblical mention of meekness.

Ephesians 4:32 — "Forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" — the command that removes forgiveness from the realm of options.

Genesis 50:20 — Joseph forgave his brothers yet still named the wrong: "You thought evil against me." Forgiveness does not deny facts.

Matthew 5:44 — Pray for those who despitefully use you; the ability to genuinely pray for an offender is evidence forgiveness has happened.

Matthew 6:12 — The Lord's Prayer teaches pre-forgiveness: proclaim forgiveness at the start of the day, before the offense comes.

Acts 15:37-39 & 2 Timothy 4:11 — Paul and John Mark's breakup and restoration: the betrayer became "profitable for the ministry."

Ephesians 4:3 — God's perfect will is "the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace," not boundaries everywhere.

Summary 1 of 4

Main Teachings

Forgiveness defined — "A decision I make to no longer require you to repay me for what you took from me." Like canceling a $1,000 debt: you release it, take it out of court, and turn it into a gift. The offender doesn't have to acknowledge anything.

Forgiveness is an act, not a process — It can be done by one person, alone, on their own, without any help from the offender. Reconciliation, by contrast, cannot be done by one person — both must be willing.

Forgiveness is commanded — "God said it. That's enough." We don't audit God's commands for personal benefit before obeying — though when God says so, it is always for our good.

The scar metaphor — A healed wound leaves a scar: the pain is gone, but the memory remains. Remembering is not unforgiveness; the question is what you do with the memory.

The pain may outlast the decision — The presence of pain does not mean you have not forgiven. Severe wounds take longer; give yourself time to heal.

Reconciliation requires repentance and trust — Broken trust is like a broken bridge: it takes time to rebuild, and sometimes it never happens. You are not required to force a relationship.

Some relationships demand reconciliation — A husband and wife living in the same house cannot stop at forgiveness; that covenant relationship must be reconciled, with help if needed.

Summary 2 of 4

Principles & Warnings

Grace flows through forgiveness — Like electricity through copper, grace needs a conduit. Unforgiveness is rubber: it blocks grace, hinders prayers, and is "good ground for the devil."

Unforgiveness is self-victimization — The offender sips wine at the party while you rage. "It is not that person anymore that's victimizing you — you are victimizing yourself" by nursing the memory.

Boundaries are wisdom, not sin — If the knife-wielder has not repented and trust has not been rebuilt, reasonable boundaries are wise protection, not evidence of unforgiveness.

Warning: don't transfer the wound — When your unhealed wound is brushed accidentally, don't treat the innocent person as if they wielded the knife. Childhood wounds especially can make loving people pay for old offenses.

Warning: don't oversize the boundary — If three feet protects you, don't build a hundred — a wall so big that reconciliation becomes impossible forever.

Warning: don't make distance a policy — If every hurt triggers a permanent cutoff, you will end friendships with people who love you and would never harm you intentionally.

Warning: watch what you say behind backs — Miriam and Aaron's private words reached God's ears. Whether before the face or behind it, God is hearing.

Summary 3 of 4

Takeaways & Declarations

Forgive now — better yet, pre-forgive — "The best time to forgive is to forgive in advance. The next best time is to forgive now." Begin each day proclaiming forgiveness, because within 24 hours somebody will say something.

Acknowledge, release, pray — Like Joseph: name the wrong honestly, decide to release the debt, then pray genuinely for the person — even before your feelings align with your decision.

Keep saying hello — Boundaries don't require rudeness. Saying hello doesn't restore the relationship, and it can't hurt you. If you can pray for someone, you can greet them.

Say "for now, no" — never say "never" — Stay open to what God can do. Paul got John Mark back as his most dependable helper; Moses the murderer became the meekest man on earth. "Since God doesn't give up on people, we should not."

Continue the conversation — The discussion continues in weekly small groups — because for many, this is not theory but a present assignment: someone to forgive this week.

Closing prayer — "Cause us to be doers of the word… may we be quick to forgive, willing to forgive… and while we know it's wisdom to place boundaries, we allow You to be God even in this situation."

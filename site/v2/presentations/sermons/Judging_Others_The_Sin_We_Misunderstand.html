Rubber Meets the Road

Judging Others: The Sin We Misunderstand

“The greatest way sometimes to show love is not to comfort, but to correct.”

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Matthew 7:1-6 • John 7:24 • 1 Corinthians 5:1-13 • Galatians 6:1 • September 2026

Hear It for Yourself

"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."

James 1:22 (NKJV)

The tendency when hearing these messages is to think about someone else: the gossiper, the person who gossips about you. If you approach these messages that way, you're approaching them wrongly.

Each of us needs to examine our own selves and ask: In what way am I not doing what God wants me to do in this area?

A Blunder of My Own

A young man shared a decision with me privately. Assuming it had already happened, I asked him about it publicly so we could celebrate. The answer was “not yet.” I had violated his confidence. So I called him aside: “You told me that privately. I made some assumptions. I was wrong. I'm sorry.”

“Bishop, You're Trying”

God asks us to be perfect, and He knows we won't be. Thank God for grace and mercy. But we must take responsibility, and where we can make correction, make it. Often all it takes is to sincerely say, “I did wrong. I'm sorry.”

Part 1

“Judge Not”

One of the most misunderstood passages in the Bible

“Stop Judging Me”

Many people don't know John 3:16, Genesis, or Revelation. But the minute you address sin, or suggest that something in their life needs correction, Matthew 7:1 comes out: “Judge not. The Bible says you shouldn't judge.”

“You have no right to say anything about my life.” • “Take care of your own problems.” • “Who are you to tell me what I'm doing is wrong?” • “If you love me, you must accept whatever I do.” • “You should be glad that I'm happy.”

Love Redefined

We have adopted and appropriated the world's definition of love.

Love now means

Acceptance, affirmation, and approval. “You never try to correct me.”

Correction now means

Rejection and hatred. “If you really loved me, you would not be saying that.”

Does that change the Bible's definition? Certainly not. Yet a large section, perhaps a majority, of the body of Christ has embraced it.

I Love You, But I Can't Celebrate This

What We Seldom Say

“Listen, this behavior is just wrong. In fact, this thing is sin. Don't expect me to celebrate what happened because of your refusal to obey the Word. I love you, but I can't celebrate this. This needs to change.”

Because we misunderstand this verse, we are failing to walk in the kind of obedience Christ has called us to.

What Did Jesus Mean?

  • Shut our eyes and pretend sin among believers isn't happening?
  • Never evaluate behavior and say, “This is not acceptable”?
  • Never call sin, sin, anymore?
  • Never confront unrighteousness when we see it?

That is not, and cannot be, what Jesus is teaching.

Part 2

Jesus Did Not Say “Never Judge”

Three passages that should convince you

The Speck and the Plank

"Judge not, that you be not judged. For with what judgment you judge, you will be judged... And why do you look at the speck in your brother's eye, but do not consider the plank in your own eye?"

Matthew 7:1-3 (NKJV)

Hyperbole With a Point

Nobody has ever had a plank in their eye. Jesus is exaggerating, with a little humor, so you remember it. Then He says, “Hypocrite!” Whatever He is saying here is within the context of hypocrisy.

Then Remove the Speck

"Hypocrite! First remove the plank from your own eye, and then you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother's eye."

Matthew 7:5 (NKJV)

He didn't say leave it because it's small, or pretend it's not there. The speck, though small, needs to be removed. Left alone, it can infect the eye, take the sight, and spread until what started as a little speck destroys the entire body.

The problem is the one trying to remove it with a light pole in his own eye. How will he ever see? He will only cause more harm.

Pearls Before Swine

"Do not give what is holy to the dogs; nor cast your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn and tear you in pieces."

Matthew 7:6 (NKJV)

How Can You Obey Verse 6 Without Judging?

How will you know who the dogs and the swine are, or who you can trust with your pearls? If verse 1 forbade all discernment, verse 6 would be impossible to obey. The question is not whether we judge, but whether we judge rightly.

Don't Put a Period Where Jesus Didn't

"Do not judge according to appearance, but judge with righteous judgment."

John 7:24 (NKJV)

When you quote Jesus as saying “Do not judge,” you're putting a period where He didn't. The sentence continues: according to appearance. Don't rush to judgment. Don't be too quick or too eager to judge.

And then He makes it even more clear: but judge, with righteous judgment.

Two Ways to Judge

Judging wrongly

We rush to judgment. We're too quick to conclude and too eager to render a verdict. We make a lot of mistakes along the way.

Righteous judgment

We have a responsibility to judge. When we do, we are careful to judge rightly.

Judging wrongly can actually cause more harm than good. So Jesus is not ending all judging; He is telling us not to judge wrongly.

Discipline Is Love

"For whom the LORD loves He chastens, and scourges every son whom He receives... But if you are without chastening, of which all have become partakers, then you are illegitimate and not sons."

Hebrews 12:6, 8 (NKJV)

How Love Looks

One of the most powerful ways God shows us He loves us is by disciplining us. And one of the greatest ways we show love to one another is not to overlook the speck, but to say, “Brother, there's a speck in your eye. How can I help you remove it?”

The One Who Loved Me Most

“My mother was the one who disciplined me a lot, and I'm convinced she is the one who loved me most.” Had she lived, she would have turned 100 the day before this sermon.

“The thing I thank God for most about my mother is the fact that she did discipline me. She didn't just let me have my way. She loved me enough to say, not in this house, not in this family.”

So why think it's different in the body of Christ? Affirming people in their sin, or calling church discipline legalism and not loving, is not correct.

Part 3

A Church That Tolerated Sin

1 Corinthians 5: Paul obeys Jesus' instruction

Gifts, Power, and Tolerated Sin

"It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you... that a man has his father's wife! And you are puffed up, and have not rather mourned."

1 Corinthians 5:1-2 (NKJV)

Corinth had the Word, the Holy Spirit, tongues, and prophecy. They boasted about the power of God and how loving and accepting their church was.

Yet something outrageous was being tolerated, and nobody was confronting it. Nobody said, “This cannot be tolerated in the church.” They went on like nothing was wrong.

A Little Leaven

"Your glorying is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump?"

1 Corinthians 5:6 (NKJV)

Paul said: you haven't acted, so I will. Gather together, investigate, and decide. If the man will not repent, put him out of the church.

Was Paul obeying Jesus?

Yes.

Was it an act of love?

Yes, to the couple and to the church.

Do You Not Judge Those Inside?

"For what have I to do with judging those also who are outside? Do you not judge those who are inside? But those who are outside God judges. Therefore 'put away from yourselves the evil person.'"

1 Corinthians 5:12-13 (NKJV)

Hold One Another Accountable

Unbelievers are unbelievers; God judges them. We are not called to stand in the kingdom shouting at those outside. But inside, we help each other obey the Scriptures, resist sin, and live holy, not excusing sin for the sake of so-called love.

The Purpose of Discipline Is to Restore

1. Make a genuine effort to help

Putting someone out is not the first option.

2. Set a personal boundary

“I don't hate you, but for now I've got to put some distance. If I can help you deal with this, I will.”

3. God forbid, the church acts

Because a little leaven leavens the whole lump.

When the Corinthian man repented, Paul said, “That's enough. Bring him back” (2 Corinthians 2:6-8). Discipline is not meant to destroy a person, but to restore him.

Part 4

What Jesus Forbids

The plank is self-righteousness

Who Jesus Called Hypocrites

Almost every time, He was addressing the Pharisees, the Sadducees, the religious leaders, the legalists, the holier-than-thou and the self-righteous: people always searching for someone to expose, embarrass, shame, condemn, and punish so they could look superior.

  • They dragged the woman caught in adultery to Jesus to get Him to join their condemnation (John 8:3-5)
  • They criticized Him for healing people on the Sabbath
  • They prayed in the temple, “Look at that sinner. I'm not like that. I pray, I fast, I give my tithes” (Luke 18:11-12)

A Judgmental Spirit

Their Problem

Their problem was not that they judged behavior for the purpose of correction. It was that they had a judgmental spirit: self-righteousness that takes delight in exposing, embarrassing, and shaming people so you can look good.

If your intention in talking to someone about their sin is not to help or restore them, but to look good and enjoy their shame, Jesus says: don't do that. With what judgment you judge, you will be judged.

The Plank in Your Eye

  • As long as you are self-righteous, you have a plank in your eye
  • As long as you are proud and holier-than-thou, you have a plank in your eye
  • As long as your motive is to expose, shame, embarrass, and condemn, you have a plank in your eye

“If you're self-righteous, you cannot help me. You can only hurt me.”

Remove the Plank

Jesus does not condemn the attitude that identifies the speck, nor the desire to have the speck removed. He forbids the self-righteous, judgmental spirit that was present in the Pharisees and too often shows up among us.

Then You Can Help

Take it out of your eye. When you remove it, you will be in a position to really help someone with a speck, because you will approach it differently. Your judgment will be right, because pride no longer affects your sight.

Part 5

What Righteous Judgment Looks Like

Guidelines for judging rightly

Restore Such a One

"Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted."

Galatians 6:1 (NKJV)

1. The motive

Restore

2. The manner

Gentleness

3. The posture

Humility

Any Trespass, One Motive

Any trespass, not just the big ones. A habit of lying. Friends who are boyfriend and girlfriend living in fornication. We can't just ignore it and be what Christ has called us to be.

Whatever we know is contrary to Scripture, we lovingly confront so that the saint is restored. Not to expose them, embarrass them, or shame them. Not to feel holier-than-thou. Not to retaliate.

A famous evangelist was once caught and exposed by someone he had exposed, who trapped him to get back at him. That motive was never restoration. The only acceptable motive is restoration.

Setting a Broken Bone

The Word “Restore”

The Greek word here is the same word used for what a doctor does when a person breaks a bone: he sets the bone so it can heal. Something is broken in that person's life spiritually, and left alone it could get worse, for him, his family, and the church.

You cannot help if you don't first acknowledge something is wrong. Motivated by love and compassion, you step in to heal what is broken.

“The purpose for discipline, for correction, for reproof, is to restore.”

Gentle, But Still the Truth

"But, speaking the truth in love, may grow up in all things into Him who is the head, Christ."

Ephesians 4:15 (NKJV)

The way you say something influences how it is received. Don't approach people from a holier-than-thou, self-righteous spirit. Be as gentle as possible.

That doesn't mean you compromise the truth. You still say it clearly, but motivated by love, in a way that makes it easier to receive and helps the person believe you are really seeking their interest.

But for the Grace of God

"Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall."

1 Corinthians 10:12 (NKJV)

“Considering yourself.” Before correcting anyone, remind yourself: but for the grace of God, there go I. I'm not above the thing I'm correcting. “I'm Bishop Johnson, I'm not susceptible to that”? Maybe not that sin, but there is something else he won't fall for that I would.

To restore, gently, mindful that I am but a man: with that attitude I can help remove the speck from your eye, because I have removed the plank from mine.

Part 6

Be Open to Receive Correction

One last thing you must not forget

The Wounds of a Friend

"Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful."

Proverbs 27:6 (NKJV)

“If you love me, you won't correct me.” • “If you correct me, you're hating on me.” • “You're not being sensitive to my needs.” • “You're being intolerant.” • “You're not really my friend.”

A friend who is never willing to tell you where you're wrong is not your friend. The one who comes from a place of love and risks the friendship to tell you the truth is God's gift to you.

The Surgeon and the Child

The surgeon with a scalpel

You can call him an executioner, or see him as the friend he is. If the tumor remains, it grows, and what is manageable becomes unmanageable.

The child running toward traffic

You don't respect his sense of direction. You chase, you yell louder, you grab whatever you can. You might break his arm, but you save his life.

“Some of the people who are yelling the loudest in your life to tell you stop it are the people who are loving you the most.”

Keep the Balance

Not the Pharisees

Always searching for people to expose, embarrass, and condemn so we can look superior. Christ doesn't want that spirit.

Not “anything goes”

Where nothing matters anymore and all that matters is that we are, quote-unquote, showing love.

Living in fornication is sin. A pregnancy out of wedlock is sin: love the baby, love the couple, and know God does not discriminate against that child, but the process was sinful. Doing business unethically is sin. In the church, the loving thing is to say, “This is wrong. There needs to be genuine repentance.”

The Charge

Not to Comfort, But to Correct

We don't want to be hearers of the Word only, but doers. We learned not to gossip. We learned the importance of forgiving and what meekness looks like. Today we learned what it means to judge not.

Yes, let's show love. “But the greatest way sometimes to show love is not to comfort, but to correct. May God give you and me the grace to do so.”

Prayer Points

  • Father, make me a doer of Your Word and not a hearer only; let me hear this message for myself and not on behalf of someone else (James 1:22)
  • Thank You for loving me enough to discipline me; help me receive Your correction as proof that I belong to You (Hebrews 12:6-8)
  • Forgive me for using “judge not” to shield my life from correction, and for confusing love with approval and correction with hatred
  • Remove every plank of self-righteousness, pride, and holier-than-thou attitude from my eye, so that I can see clearly (Matthew 7:5)
  • Keep me from judging by appearance or rushing to a verdict, and give me righteous judgment and discernment (John 7:24; Matthew 7:6)

Prayer Points

  • Give our church the courage to deal with sin while it is small, before a little leaven leavens the whole lump (1 Corinthians 5:6)
  • When a brother or sister is overtaken in a trespass, let my only motive be restoration, never exposure, shame, or retaliation (Galatians 6:1)
  • Teach me to speak the truth in love, gently and without compromise, remembering that but for Your grace, there go I (Ephesians 4:15; 1 Corinthians 10:12)
  • Give me a heart that values the faithful wounds of a friend; thank You for the friends, family, and pastors who love me enough to correct me (Proverbs 27:6)
  • Make me Your instrument of restoration and healing, and when someone repents, help me forgive, comfort, and reaffirm my love (2 Corinthians 2:7-8)
Declaration

I Will Judge Rightly

Father, I will not hide behind “judge not” to avoid correction, and I will not call it love to approve what You call sin. Remove the plank of self-righteousness and pride from my eye, so that I see clearly. I will not judge by appearance or rush to a verdict. When my brother or sister is overtaken in a trespass, I will go to restore, not to expose; in a spirit of gentleness, speaking the truth in love; considering myself, because but for Your grace, there go I. I receive the faithful wounds of a friend as Your gift. Make me Your instrument of restoration and healing. Not only to comfort, but to correct, in love. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

Judging Others: The Sin We Misunderstand - Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Central Message - Following “Gossip: The Sin We Tolerate,” this rubber-meets-the-road message takes on one of the most misunderstood and most quoted verses in the Bible. “Judge not” has become a shield against all correction, because the world, and much of the church, has redefined love as acceptance, affirmation, and approval, and now hears correction as rejection and hatred.

The Proof - Three passages show that Jesus never forbade judging. Matthew 7:6 cannot be obeyed without discernment. John 7:24 commands, “judge with righteous judgment.” And in 1 Corinthians 5, Paul orders a Spirit-filled church to confront and discipline tolerated sin: “Do you not judge those who are inside?”

What Jesus Forbids - The hypocrite with the plank is the Pharisee: self-righteous, proud, holier-than-thou, always searching for someone to expose, embarrass, shame, and condemn. The plank is self-righteousness, and it makes you unable to help anyone.

What Jesus Commands - Righteous judgment, as Galatians 6:1 describes it: the motive is restoration, like a doctor setting a broken bone; the manner is gentleness; the posture is humility. And we must be open to receive correction, because the people yelling loudest in our lives may be the ones loving us most. “The greatest way sometimes to show love is not to comfort, but to correct.”

Summary 1 of 5

Key Scriptures

Matthew 7:1-5 - The anchor text. “Judge not, that you be not judged.” Jesus uses humorous hyperbole (nobody has ever had a plank in the eye) and the word “Hypocrite!” to set the context. He never says leave the speck; He says remove the plank first so you can see clearly to remove the speck.

Matthew 7:6 - “Do not give what is holy to the dogs; nor cast your pearls before swine.” Impossible to obey without discernment, so verse 1 cannot forbid all judgment.

John 7:24 - “Do not judge according to appearance, but judge with righteous judgment.” Don't put a period where Jesus didn't. Don't rush to judgment, but do judge, and judge rightly.

Hebrews 12:6-8 - “For whom the LORD loves He chastens.” Discipline is one of the most powerful ways God shows His love; a child who is never corrected is not a son.

1 Corinthians 5:1-2 - “It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you... And you are puffed up.” A church full of gifts and prophecy boasted of its love while tolerating a man with his father's wife, and nobody confronted it.

1 Corinthians 5:6 - “A little leaven leavens the whole lump.” Sin that is small, overlooked, and tolerated spreads until the whole body is affected.

1 Corinthians 5:11-13 - “Do you not judge those who are inside? But those who are outside God judges.” Unbelievers will act like unbelievers, and God judges them. Believers must hold one another accountable, and put away the one who will not repent.

2 Corinthians 2:6-8 - When the disciplined man repented, Paul told the church to forgive, comfort, and reaffirm their love. The purpose of discipline is not to destroy but to restore.

John 8:3-5; Luke 18:11-12 - The Pharisees bringing the woman caught in adultery to get Jesus to join their condemnation, and the Pharisee in the temple thanking God he was not like that sinner: portraits of the judgmental spirit Jesus condemned.

Galatians 6:1 - “Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted.” The blueprint for righteous judgment: restore, gently, humbly. The Greek word for restore (katartizo) describes a doctor setting a broken bone.

Ephesians 4:15 - Speaking the truth in love: never compromise the truth, but say it gently.

1 Corinthians 10:12 - “Let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall.” But for the grace of God, there go I.

Proverbs 27:6 - “Faithful are the wounds of a friend.” A friend who never tells you where you're wrong is not your friend.

James 1:22 - Be doers of the Word, and not hearers only. Hear the message for yourself, not for someone else.

Summary 2 of 5

Main Points & Teachings

Doers, not hearers - Apply the message to yourself. The Bishop confessed that two days earlier he had publicly asked a young man about a private decision that had not yet happened, then called him aside and apologized. “We're asking you to be perfect; we just know that you won't be.” Take responsibility and make correction.

The favorite misquoted verse - People who know little Scripture know Matthew 7:1 the moment their sin is addressed: “Stop judging me. Take care of your own problems. If you love me, you must accept whatever I do.”

Love redefined - We confuse love with acceptance, affirmation, and approval, and correction with rejection and hatred. A large section of the church has embraced this, so we seldom say, “I love you, but I can't celebrate this. This needs to change.”

The speck must go - Jesus did not say ignore the speck because it is small. Left alone, it can infect the eye, take the sight, and spread through the body. The problem is trying to remove it with a light pole in your own eye.

Verse 6 requires judgment - You cannot know who the dogs and swine are without discernment. The question is not whether to judge, but whether we judge rightly.

Two ways to judge - Wrongly, by rushing to judgment and being too quick and eager to render a verdict; or rightly, with righteous judgment, which is our responsibility.

Discipline is love - God shows His love by chastening (Hebrews 12). The Bishop's mother, who would have turned 100 the day before, disciplined him most and loved him most: “Not in this house.” Church discipline is not legalism.

Corinth tolerated sin - The church boasted of gifts, power, and how loving and accepting it was, while tolerating sexual immorality no one confronted. Paul commanded them to gather, investigate, decide, and put out the unrepentant man. That was obedience to Jesus, and an act of love toward the couple and the church.

Inside, not outside - Paul's instruction not to keep company with a sexually immoral, covetous, idolatrous, reviling, drunken, or extortionate “brother” concerned believers, not unbelievers. We are not called to stand in the kingdom shouting at those outside; we are called to hold each other accountable.

Discipline in stages, aimed at restoration - Genuine effort to help comes first. If someone persists, a personal boundary follows: “I don't hate you, but for now I've got to put some distance.” And, God forbid, the church acts. When the Corinthian man repented, Paul said, “That's enough. Bring him back.”

The hypocrites Jesus addressed - Pharisees, Sadducees, legalists, and the holier-than-thou, always searching for someone to expose, embarrass, shame, condemn, and punish so they could look superior: the accusers of the woman caught in adultery, the critics of healing on the Sabbath, the Pharisee in the temple.

The plank is self-righteousness - Jesus does not condemn identifying the speck or wanting it removed. He condemns judgmentalism. “If you're self-righteous, you cannot help me. You can only hurt me.” Remove the plank, and pride no longer distorts your sight.

Righteous judgment - Any trespass, not just the big ones: a habit of lying, friends living in fornication. The motive is restoration, never exposure or retaliation, unlike the person who trapped a famous evangelist to pay him back. The manner is gentleness, speaking the truth in love without compromise. The posture is humility: “but for the grace of God, there go I.”

Be open to receive correction - “If you correct me, you're hating on me” is a lie of the world system. The surgeon with a scalpel is a friend, not an executioner. The parent chasing a child toward traffic yells louder because they care, and might break an arm to save a life.

Keep the balance - Don't be Pharisees seeking to expose, but don't go to the other extreme where anything goes. Fornication, pregnancy out of wedlock (love the baby, and know God does not discriminate against the child, but the process was sinful), and unethical business in the church must still be named and lovingly confronted.

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

Principle - Judging is not forbidden; judging wrongly is. The issue is motive and manner, not whether to judge at all.

Principle - Discipline is one of the clearest expressions of love, from God, from parents, from pastors, and from friends.

Principle - Tolerated sin spreads. A little leaven leavens the whole lump.

Principle - The church judges those inside and leaves those outside to God.

Principle - The purpose of discipline is to restore, never to destroy.

Principle - Self-righteousness disqualifies a person from helping anyone else with a fault.

Principle - Speak the truth without compromise, and speak it gently.

Principle - The people who correct you may be the people who love you most.

Warning - Beware using “judge not” to silence legitimate correction in your own life.

Warning - Beware rushing to judgment or judging by appearance.

Warning - Beware confronting sin in order to expose, embarrass, shame, retaliate, or feel superior.

Warning - Beware tolerating sin in the name of so-called love.

Warning - Beware thinking you are not susceptible. Let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall.

Warning - Beware treating the friends and pastors who correct you as enemies.

Warning - Beware hearing this message on behalf of someone else. Examine yourself first.

Summary 4 of 5

Takeaways & Declarations

1. Hear it for yourself - Ask where you are not doing what God wants, rather than thinking of someone else.

2. When you get it wrong, make it right - Go to the person and sincerely say, “I did wrong. I'm sorry.”

3. Stop hiding behind Matthew 7:1 - Let the people who love you speak into your life.

4. Slow down - Don't judge by appearance or rush to a verdict.

5. Check for the plank - Before correcting anyone, examine your heart for pride, self-righteousness, and the desire to expose.

6. Aim to restore - Go to a brother or sister overtaken in a fault the way a doctor sets a bone: to heal what is broken.

7. Be gentle and humble - Speak the truth in love, remembering that but for the grace of God, there go I.

8. Say it with love - “I love you, but I can't celebrate this. This needs to change.”

9. Give thanks for correction - Thank God for the friends, church, and pastors bold and faithful enough to tell you the truth.

Congregational confession - “Bishop, that's all right. You're trying.” “I am trying myself to be obedient to the Word of God.”

Closing prayer - “May the Lord give you the grace that you need to really remove self-righteousness, remove pride, remove that spirit of judgmentalism that may be blinding you... May the Lord help you to become His instrument of restoration and healing.”

The charge - “Let's show love. But the greatest way sometimes to show love is not to comfort, but to correct. May God give you and me the grace to do so.”

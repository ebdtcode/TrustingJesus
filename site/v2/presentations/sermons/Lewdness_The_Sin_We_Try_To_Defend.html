Rubber Meets the Road

Lewdness: The Sin We Try To Defend

“The culture determines what's popular. The culture does not determine what's holy. The Word does.”

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Galatians 5:19-21 • Romans 12:2 • 1 Thessalonians 4:3-5 • 1 Corinthians 6:19-20 • September 2026

Doers of the Word

"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."

James 1:22 (NKJV)

For several weeks we have dealt with rubber-meets-the-road issues: practices or sins that exist within the church that we don't treat as seriously as we ought to. First unforgiveness, then gossip.

People came back and said it was practical, it was helpful, and as they searched themselves they realized they had to repent of certain things they took for granted. That's what we want to see.

Say “Lewdness”

L-E-W-D-N-E-S-S

What is that? Well, that's lasciviousness. That doesn't help me. That's licentiousness. Some Bibles say sensuality. For most of us, we don't know what those words mean.

But guess what? That word is used a lot in the Scriptures. And lewdness is one of those sins we need to talk about: the sin we have learned to defend.

Part 1

A Work of the Flesh

Lewdness is a sin, and it is broader than you think

Named Among the Works of the Flesh

"Now the works of the flesh are evident, which are: adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lewdness, idolatry, sorcery, hatred, contentions, jealousies, outbursts of wrath, selfish ambitions, dissensions, heresies..."

Galatians 5:19-20 (NKJV)

Up Front

Lewdness is mentioned among all of these other sins. So it lets you know: lewdness is a work of the flesh. Lewdness is a sin. It has to do with sexual sin, but it is not limited to the physical acts of adultery and fornication.

Lewdness Extends Beyond the Act

Not just sexual relationships outside of marriage, which fall under sexual immorality. It's a broader application.

Our eyes

What we look at, what we watch, what we see

Our speech

What we say and how we joke

Our minds

Our thoughts and imaginations

Our entertainment

What we choose to laugh at and be amused by

Our dress

How we present our bodies

And we want to take it seriously

Individually, and as a congregation

Part 2

Conformed or Transformed

How sin gets renamed, redefined, and defended

Don't Let the World Squeeze You

"And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God."

Romans 12:2 (NKJV)

Intentionally or Unintentionally

The culture has its own values and standards, and we live surrounded by it. If we do not intentionally allow our minds to be renewed by the Word of God, we will unintentionally find ourselves being conformed to the world.

When Sin Stops Bothering Us

  • What once shocked us no longer does
  • What once embarrassed us now becomes a form of entertainment
  • What we once wouldn't watch, we now watch. What we once wouldn't say, we now say. What we once wouldn't wear, we now wear
  • And eventually, what we once called sin, we begin to call normal

When sin stops bothering Christians, that's really bad. But it becomes worse when not only doesn't it bother us, we find ourselves defending it.

“Times Have Changed”

“When the Bible was written, people got married as teenagers, so the command to abstain before marriage made sense. Now they go to college, get their master's, start a career. It's unreasonable to tell fully developed adults in their 20s and 30s to abstain. What matters is that it's two consenting adults being responsible.”

I have actually heard a pastor say this. Once, hearing that an unmarried couple lived together shocked you. Now the conversation doesn't even come up. “They're adults. They're going to get married anyway. What's the big deal?”

So we begin not only to normalize sin, we start to defend sin.

“You Need a Test Drive”

“They're engaged. What's wrong with them sleeping together? After all, you need to make sure.” A minister on social media was literally, strongly advocating that this is necessary before you make a vow.

Struggling Versus Defending

It's one thing to struggle with sin. It's another thing to defend the sin you're struggling with. Because you cannot repent of something that you insist on defending.

Three Excuses, Three Answers

“Everybody does it.”

“Everybody does it” is not our standard. Christ Jesus is.

“It's just culture.”

Romans 12:2 says don't be conformed to the culture. The culture determines what's popular. It does not determine what's holy. The Word does.

“All that talk about holiness is legalism.”

Legalism is trying to earn acceptance by keeping rules. Holiness is responding to the One who has already accepted you, with a desire to please Him in all things.

Part 3

What Is Lewdness?

A definition, and where it shows up

The Definition

Lewdness Is

Expressing, displaying, or stimulating sexuality in a way that violates God's boundaries: in a way that violates purity, violates modesty, and violates the restraints God has put in place to govern our behavior.

Expressing
Displaying
Stimulating

God has established boundaries for our sexuality. When we go beyond them, we have entered the area of lewdness.

Lewd Speech

"Let no corrupt word proceed out of your mouth, but what is good for necessary edification, that it may impart grace to the hearers."

Ephesians 4:29 (NKJV)

Vulgar jokes. Sexually suggestive comments in conversation. “I didn't mean it, I was just joking.” But sin doesn't become holy just because people laughed.

Even presidents and senators now use the F word publicly, and it's considered normal. That's the culture. But the culture may determine what's popular; it doesn't determine what's holy. God has called us to holiness.

Salt and Light, More Than Ever

"You are the salt of the earth... You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden."

Matthew 5:13-14 (NKJV)

There was a time you could watch the news with your children, because society embraced certain standards even where it wasn't born again. Those standards have gone out the window. Christians could once count on our values being reinforced by non-Christians. You can forget about that now.

If we don't shine the light, nobody else is giving any light. If we're not the salt, nobody else is preserving anything. Are you willing to accept the rejection that comes with it? Are you willing to be different?

Lewdness in Dress

An area we have forgotten to talk about, and don't want to touch. But we're going to have to. You may never get into somebody's bed, but you can get into their imagination. Men and women: the way I dress, why do I dress the way I do?

Holiness Doesn't Have to Be Ugly

Sexuality is not evil; it is God's gift. Beauty is not sinful. God loves beauty. Nothing I'm saying says you should not look attractive. But you do not glorify God when you dress in a way that draws attention to the intimate parts of your body that ought to be properly covered. At that point, it's not God you're displaying.

The Principle Is Modesty

We don't have a bunch of rules where we'll be measuring. God doesn't provide that. But there is a principle: dress, but don't draw attention to what you know are intimate parts of your body. Once you start doing that, you cross the line of modesty.

You can be attractive without being sexually provocative. Just yesterday I saw someone whose blouse left no way to look at her without your eyes being pulled to what only her husband should see. I didn't need to see that. And I couldn't see her for beauty.

After Jesus preached about eating His flesh and drinking His blood, everybody left, and He asked, “Do you also want to go away?” (John 6:66-67). I don't know what my congregation will look like after today. But we've got to teach the Word.

Entertainment

"A merry heart does good, like medicine, but a broken spirit dries the bones."

Proverbs 17:22 (NKJV)

I like to laugh. When I leave y'all I just want to lie down, laugh, and fall asleep. But it's becoming harder and harder. Those words contribute nothing to the joke. They just have to cuss.

We cannot call those things sinful and then choose to be entertained by them. What I hear and what I see are things I need to really govern.

Part 4

God's Will Is Your Sanctification

1 Thessalonians 4:3-5

Fire in the Fireplace

Fire where it belongs

Fire is a wonderful blessing. In the fireplace or on the stove, it can bless you.

Fire on the carpet

Put it on the sofa, or in the back of the car, and you're going to get a house burned down.

Sexuality is a gift from God that we should value, within the boundaries God placed. Holiness is not anti-beauty, anti-attraction, or anti-sex. Christianity is pro-holiness.

One Area You Don't Have to Pray About

"For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you should abstain from sexual immorality."

1 Thessalonians 4:3 (NKJV)

God's Will Is Already Written

We pray to know God's will for our career, our business. Here is one area you don't need to pray about; God put it in Scripture so you always know. Sanctification: set apart from that which is sinful, set apart unto God for His use and His glory. And the first thing He says: abstain from sexual immorality.

Possess Your Own Vessel

"...that each of you should know how to possess his own vessel in sanctification and honor, not in passion of lust, like the Gentiles who do not know God."

1 Thessalonians 4:4-5 (NKJV)

Your vessel is your body. Your body has its own needs, its own voice, appetites, emotions. It will tell you to do things that are sexually immoral. Unless you know how to bring it into subjection, you will keep surrendering to the desires of your flesh.

Set the boundaries: in your behavior, your entertainment, your speech. Take the body and tell the body to sit down. You've got to be able to say no.

Whose Body Is It?

The culture says

“It's your body. Do whatever you want with it, as long as you don't bother anybody else. You have a right to please yourself.”

God says

“No. Your body is Mine. I purchased you. I own you. Your body exists to bring Me pleasure, to honor Me.”

Here's the good thing: whatever He calls you to do, He gives you the power to do it. If you're saying, “Oh, God,” that's a good place to be. Admit your weakness, and God can help you.

Call It What It Is

The Holy Spirit is in your body to help you. But He cannot help you overcome lewdness until you first recognize what it is, and stop defending it. Stop trying to legitimize it.

  • Don't call holiness “legalism”
  • Don't call the exposure of your body “fashion”
  • Don't call what you watch “just a movie”

Let's call it what it is: lewdness.

Parents, Tell Your Children

Because the culture isn't. We've got to have the conversations, with our boys and our girls. “Just because you're in your 20s, it's still sin. You've got sexual needs? Get married. If you're choosing to wait, then you need to abstain.”

If they say it's not reasonable: “We're not talking about what's reasonable. We're talking about what the Word of God says. We're talking about holiness.” Everybody will say you're crazy. Some will call you a fanatic.

“The Word of God doesn't change. Sin cannot be redefined. Holiness is not redefined.”

Your Body Is Too Sacred

Not Because It's Dirty

The reason you should be concerned about how you dress and carry yourself is not because your body is dirty. Your body is beautiful. It's because your body is so valuable, so sacred. It belongs to God. It is God's temple. Don't reduce it to a sexual toy for the entertainment of some man. Protect it. Cover it.

I used to look at Muslim women all covered and say, “What is that?” Now, seeing where some of our women have gone, I'm beginning to say, “Wow.” We have gone to the extreme. Men, we want to look good too, but we also have a responsibility not to dress in a way that unnecessarily draws attention.

Part 5

You Are the Temple

1 Corinthians 6:19-20: the power to say no

Do You Not Know?

"Or do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own? For you were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's."

1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (NKJV)

Recognize Who You Are

The most important thing for possessing your body in holiness is to recognize who you are. If you try to restrain your body without a revelation of who you are, you will struggle and struggle, and you will lose every time.

Renew and Reinforce Your Identity

This is not something you do once, because the temptation is always there. Don't see yourself as just an attractive person or another human being. Say it: “I am the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is inside of me.”

The more this is realized in your life, the stronger you will be in resisting. Not without challenges, but so much easier to walk away from sin, even sexual sin. That's where you get the power to say no.

Every Morning

You're not trying to become a temple. You are the temple. The Holy Ghost already lives in you. Before you face the world and its temptations, reinforce in your mind that wherever you go, the Holy Spirit is living inside of you.

Your Body Exists for Worship

What is a temple for? In the Old Testament the presence of God was in the temple, and people came there to worship. So when God says your body is the temple, He is saying the purpose of your body is worship. Not for the worship of man, but of God.

Your body exists not to bring you pleasure, but to bring God pleasure. The pleasure you get is a fringe benefit. Living for the glory of God means His love, His holiness, His goodness, and His power can be revealed through me.

When people leave your presence they should say, “What a godly man. What a godly woman. What a good, loving, holy God.” Not have to pray in tongues to get the image out of their minds.

Eyes and Ears Are Doors

"I will set nothing wicked before my eyes; I hate the work of those who fall away; it shall not cling to me."

Psalm 101:3 (NKJV)

Your eyes and ears are windows through which you see and hear. But they are also doors that allow the world to get on the inside of you. The more you are entertained by what comes through them, the more these things become normal.

Two women holding hands would have shocked me before. The more you see it, the more normal it seems. If you don't remind yourself that this is sin, you'll get to the place where you simply join the culture.

This Is Warfare

We have to intentionally renew our minds and allow them to be transformed by the Word of God regularly, or we will become conformed to this world. Two people living together on TV, adultery on the screen: there was a time you would have been shocked. Now we're entertained by it.

Consumed or Changing

If we don't intentionally, I keep saying that word, keep transforming our minds with the truth of Christ and His Word, all of us will be consumed by the culture, and we will become like the world rather than changing the world. This is warfare, and we've got to take it seriously.

I Am Not My Own

Your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is in you, whom you have from God. Say it: “I am not my own.” Your body is for God's glory. Your body is to worship God. Your body is for God to be revealed through you.

You do not need a body to be alive. Darlingston Johnson is a spirit. One of these days I won't have a body, and I'll still be Darlingston Johnson. God didn't give me a body so I can be me. God gave me a body so that through this body, He can be revealed.

“Let your body be used for the purpose for which God gave it to you.”

Grace and Mercy

There Is No Condemnation Here

Some of you are struggling in a particular area. Pornography, for some, is a major one, and the percentage keeps increasing every day. If it has become an addiction, there's help for you. Jesus paid the price for your sins. But He didn't just come to provide forgiveness. He came to give you power over sin.

Don't leave feeling condemned. Come to the throne of grace to receive help in time of need (Hebrews 4:16). You don't have to be strong in yourself. Just say, “Holy Spirit, I depend upon You in this area, and I thank You for the victory.”

Prayer Points

  • Father, make me a doer of Your Word; search me, and show me where I have taken lewdness for granted so I can repent (James 1:22; Psalm 139:23-24)
  • Forgive me for renaming, redefining, and defending what You call sin; give me the honesty to call it what it is (Galatians 5:19-21)
  • Renew my mind by Your Word every day, so that I am transformed and not conformed to the culture around me (Romans 12:2)
  • Let sin never stop bothering me; keep my conscience tender to what once shocked me, so I never call sin normal (Ephesians 4:19)
  • Set a guard over my mouth; let no corrupt word or suggestive joke come out of my lips, only what edifies (Ephesians 4:29; Psalm 141:3)

Prayer Points

  • Teach me to possess my body in sanctification and honor, to tell my body to sit down, and to say no to its appetites (1 Thessalonians 4:3-5)
  • Let my dress and my beauty glorify You and not draw attention to what only You intended for marriage; make modesty my principle (1 Timothy 2:9)
  • I will set nothing wicked before my eyes; guard the doors of my eyes and ears and govern what entertains me (Psalm 101:3)
  • Give parents courage to have the conversations the culture will not have, and keep our children pure (Deuteronomy 6:6-7)
  • For everyone struggling with pornography or sexual sin, thank You that there is no condemnation and there is power over sin; Holy Spirit, we depend on You for victory (Romans 8:1; Hebrews 4:16)
Declaration

I Am Not My Own

Father, I will not be a person who defends sin or justifies lewdness. Even though the culture embraces it more and more, I am the temple of the living God, and I will not embrace the culture. The culture is not my standard; Christ is. I am the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is inside of me. I was bought at a price, and I am not my own. My body exists to worship You and to reveal You. I will possess my vessel in sanctification and honor. I will set nothing wicked before my eyes. I will call sin what it is, and I will depend on the Holy Spirit for victory. May my sexuality bring glory to You. May my beauty bring glory to You. May my body bring glory to You. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

Lewdness: The Sin We Try To Defend - Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Central Message - Third in the rubber-meets-the-road series, after unforgiveness and gossip, this message takes on lewdness: a work of the flesh the culture has renamed, redefined, and reclassified as normal, and that even believers have learned to defend. Lewdness is expressing, displaying, or stimulating sexuality beyond the boundaries God has set, and it reaches far beyond adultery and fornication into our speech, our dress, our entertainment, our thoughts, and our imaginations.

The Diagnosis - Romans 12:2 is the hinge. If we do not intentionally renew our minds by the Word, we will unintentionally be conformed to the world: what once shocked us stops bothering us, then becomes our entertainment, then gets called normal, and finally gets defended. And you cannot repent of something you insist on defending. “Everybody does it” is not our standard; Christ is. The culture determines what's popular, not what's holy.

The Way Out - God's will is not a mystery here: your sanctification, abstaining from sexual immorality, and knowing how to possess your own vessel (1 Thessalonians 4:3-5). The power to say no comes from a revelation of identity: you are the temple of the Holy Spirit, bought at a price, not your own, and your body exists for the worship of God (1 Corinthians 6:19-20). Sexuality is God's gift, like fire in the fireplace, and holiness is not ugly; Christianity is pro-holiness. Guard the doors of your eyes and ears, tell your children what the culture won't, call sin what it is, and depend on the Holy Spirit, because Jesus came not only to forgive sin but to give power over it.

Summary 1 of 7

Key Scriptures

Galatians 5:19-21 - “Now the works of the flesh are evident, which are: adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lewdness...” The anchor text. Lewdness (lasciviousness, sensuality) sits in the list of the works of the flesh, so it is a sin, and it is sexual in nature but broader than the physical act.

Romans 12:2 - “Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.” The culture has its own values and tries to squeeze us into its mold. Intentional renewal is the only defense against unintentional conformity.

Sanctification means set apart from sin and set apart for God's glory; possessing your vessel means bringing your body's appetites into subjection.

Romans 8:1; Hebrews 4:16 - No condemnation, and a throne of grace where mercy and help are found in time of need. The altar call for those struggling with pornography and sexual sin: Jesus came to provide forgiveness and power over sin.

Summary 2 of 7

Main Teachings

Lewdness Is a Sin, and It Is Broad - Listed among the works of the flesh, lewdness concerns sexual sin but is not limited to adultery and fornication. It reaches our eyes (what we look at and watch), our speech, our thoughts and imaginations, our entertainment, and our dress. We must take it seriously as individuals and as a congregation.

The Slide From Shock to Defense - Surrounded by a culture that does not recognize sin as sin, Christians who are not careful reach the place where sin stops bothering them. What once shocked us no longer does; what once embarrassed us becomes entertainment; what we wouldn't watch, say, or wear, we now do; and eventually what we called sin, we call normal. Worse than sin not bothering us is finding ourselves defending it.

Struggling Versus Defending - It is one thing to struggle with sin and another to defend the sin you struggle with, because you cannot repent of something you insist on defending. The “times have changed” argument (people marry later, so abstinence in your 20s and 30s is unreasonable) and the “test drive” argument for engaged couples are defenses of sin, even when preached by ministers.

The Definition - Lewdness is expressing, displaying, or stimulating sexuality in a way that violates God's boundaries: violating purity, modesty, and the restraints God put in place to govern our behavior. Three words to remember: expressing, displaying, stimulating.

Lewd Speech - Vulgar jokes and sexually suggestive comments are lewdness, and “I was just joking” is no defense. Sin doesn't become holy because people laughed. Leaders cursing publicly shows how the culture has shifted; the standard for our speech is Ephesians 4:29.

Salt and Light More Than Ever - Society once reinforced Christian values even where it wasn't Christian; you could watch the news with your children. That is gone. Believers are now the only ones who can set a standard, and must be ready for the rejection and persecution that come with being different.

Summary 4 of 7

Principles

Intentional renewal or unintentional conformity - There is no neutral ground. If the mind is not deliberately renewed by the Word, it drifts into the world's mold (Romans 12:2).

You cannot repent of what you defend - Repentance requires first agreeing with God about what a thing is. Defense of sin blocks deliverance from it.

Popular is not holy - The culture determines what is popular; the Word determines what is holy. “Everybody does it” is not our standard; Christ is.

Holiness is a response, not a ransom - Legalism earns acceptance by rules; holiness responds to acceptance already given with a desire to please God in all things.

Boundaries make the gift a blessing - Sexuality is God's good gift, and, like fire, it blesses in its place and destroys outside of it. Christianity is pro-holiness, not anti-sex.

Sin is not sanctified by laughter - A joke, a movie, or a fashion does not change the category of what God calls lewdness.

Modesty is a principle, not a measurement - Dress attractively, but do not draw attention to what should be covered.

Identity precedes victory - Restraint without revelation loses every time. The power to say no flows from knowing you are the temple of the Holy Spirit, bought at a price.

Ownership settles the argument - “It's my body” is answered by “You are not your own.” The body exists to worship God and reveal Him, and every command He gives comes with power to obey.

What enters becomes normal - Eyes and ears are doors. Repeated exposure numbs the conscience, so guard the doors (Psalm 101:3).

Summary 5 of 7

Warnings and Cautions

Against sin that stops bothering you - When sin stops bothering Christians, that is really bad; when they begin defending it, that is worse. Watch for the progression from shock, to entertainment, to normal, to defended.

Against ministers who defend sin - Pastors and social-media ministers are teaching that abstinence is unreasonable for modern adults and that engaged couples need a “test drive.” Times change; the Word of God does not. Sin cannot be redefined.

Against renaming sin - Calling holiness legalism, exposure fashion, and what you watch just a movie keeps the Holy Spirit from helping you, because He cannot help you overcome what you refuse to name.

Against counting on the culture - Society no longer reinforces Christian values, even on the news. If believers do not set the standard, nobody will.

Against provocative dress - Dressing to draw attention to what should be covered displays yourself, not God, and pulls others into imagination even when there is no physical act. Men are not exempt.

Against entertaining what you call sin - Being amused by cursing, adultery, or unmarried couples on screen quietly makes them normal to you.

Against fighting without identity - Trying to restrain the body without a revelation of who you are leads to struggling and losing every time.

Against expecting reasonableness - The world will call you crazy or a fanatic for teaching abstinence. Holiness is not measured by what seems reasonable.

Against condemnation - For those already struggling, including with pornography, there is no condemnation. Grace and mercy are available, but you must ask, and you must come to the throne of grace.

Summary 6 of 7

Takeaways and Declarations

Examine yourself, not others - Ask where you have renamed, normalized, or defended lewdness, and repent of what you have taken for granted. Be a doer, not a hearer only.

Renew your mind on purpose - Every day, before facing the world, reinforce: “I am the temple of the Holy Spirit, and He lives inside me.” This is not a one-time act.

Govern your speech - No vulgar jokes or suggestive comments; measure every conversation by whether it edifies.

Forgiveness & Reconciliation • Part 2

Living The Father's Dream

Cancel the Debt — But Never Cancel the Person

Bishop Darlingston Johnson • Harvest Intercontinental Church-Olney

Hebrews 12:14-15 • Luke 6:27-28 • Romans 12:18 • July 27, 2026

Why We Are Going Deeper

Last week's teaching on forgiveness and reconciliation flowed out of the teaching on meekness. Many were blessed — but questions remained, and some things were not communicated as clearly as they should have been.

A Letter From Africa

A WhatsApp message came all the way from Africa — thanking God for the message, but asking a question about Hebrews 12:14-15. So today we take more time to reinforce and clarify.

The Text

"Pursue peace with all people, and holiness, without which no one will see the Lord: looking carefully lest anyone fall short of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by this many become defiled."

Hebrews 12:14-15 (NKJV)

How we relate to one another in the body is a serious issue — it carries consequences, spiritually and beyond.

The Root of Bitterness

It starts small — you become offended, you don't deal with it properly, and the offense becomes bitterness.
It opens a door — "cause trouble" means it opens the door to the devil: trouble for you, trouble in your family, trouble in the church.
It spreads — what began between two people spreads; sides get taken, relationships fall, people act funny, and "many become defiled."
It costs the work — the work of God is hindered. We give Satan an opening.
Part 1

What Is God's Dream?

When God looks at His church, what does He long to see?

What God Does Not Long to See

  • Division and discord in the body
  • Husbands and wives living as strangers
  • Brothers and sisters refusing to speak — or avoiding one another
  • A church fractured by bitterness and offense
  • The silent treatment: "I just won't talk to him. I just won't talk to her."
  • Keeping score, listening to and spreading gossip
  • False accusations built on incomplete information — one side heard, the other never asked

The Dream Itself

God dreams of a family where there is no war between members, no bitterness, no desire for revenge — a family of love, genuine caring, no blame, no suspicion, no selfishness, no broken trust, all living in harmony.

Sons and Daughters

A family who would love one another, care for one another, be gracious to one another, be good to one another, enjoy one another — and enjoy Him as a family forever.

"Lord, Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven."

It Was Real in Eden

When God created man, none of those things were present in Eden. That was the Father's will — and for a time, the dream was a reality. When Jesus came, He did not change the Father's vision. He embraced it.

"A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another."

John 13:34 (NKJV)

Not just as much as you love yourself — go beyond that. Love one another as much as I love you. And He loved us enough to die for us. That's a lot of love.

Imagine Yourself Living It

A family member who is not waiting — just waiting — for someone to do something so they can be offended.

A family member to whom gossip comes to die — because you never entertain it and you never repeat it.

Quick to forgive, because you yourself have been forgiven, and because love covers a multitude of sins.

Addressing conflict humbly — not to defend yourself or prove yourself right, but to seek reconciliation.

Always assuming the best instead of the worst.

Now imagine an entire church of such people — challenged, encouraged, and inspired by the life you are living before them. What we can only imagine now will one day be reality, because the Father is still dreaming.

Part 2

Then Genesis 3 Happened

Adam and Eve betrayed the Father's trust

Sin's First Casualty

Before the fall, Adam and Eve and God were family. They were close. They trusted one another. They fellowshipped and communed in the cool of the day — joy, laughter, happiness. That was the Father's dream, and it was happening.

When Adam sinned, the first thing and the greatest thing sin impacted was the relationship — first between God and man, then between Adam and Eve.

It Hurts

It really hurts when someone you trusted with your life, your secrets, your well-being undermines your trust, connives behind your back, and partners with those who want to do you harm. All of us have been wounded — and if we are honest, all of us have wounded others. All of us need grace.

Adam Gave God the Silent Treatment

Adam did exactly what we often do when a relationship fractures. Hiding, he was saying: "I don't want to be in God's presence anymore. I don't want to talk to God. I don't want to deal with God."

Our Pattern

Right after we've given the person a piece of our mind, we stop dealing with them. We get away from them. We limit our dealings with them as much as possible.

"You've given away so many pieces of your mind, you don't have much left. So be careful how much you give away."

Nobody Could Have Blamed God

God was the offended One. God was the One wronged. God was the One whose trust was betrayed — Adam went behind His back, talked behind His back, and connived with His enemies. All the things you complain about.

You would think God would wait for Adam to apologize. Some of you are waiting: "I'm going to remain angry. I'm not dealing with that person until they at least acknowledge they did me wrong."

What the World Says

"You've been hurt — don't deal with that person anymore. Cut him off. Stop speaking to him. Create your boundaries. Don't give him any more access to your life. And you're right, because you did him right and he did you wrong." If anyone had the right not to seek reconciliation, it was God.

But God Came Looking

"Then the LORD God called to Adam and said to him, 'Where are you?'"

Genesis 3:9 (NKJV)

Wait a minute — it should be the other way around. At least that's how we think. But the world does it one way; in the kingdom we do it another way. In the world leaders want to be served; in the kingdom, you who are great, you serve. It is upside down.

The offended One comes looking. The One who is right comes looking for the one who is wrong. The One who was hurt is the One trying to fix it.

He Who Was Hurt Committed to Reconciling

"God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself, not imputing their trespasses to them."

2 Corinthians 5:19 (NKJV)

And Notice What Jesus Said

"If you bring your gift to the altar, and there remember that your brother has something against you… go your way. First be reconciled to your brother." (Matthew 5:23-24) He did not say you have something against them. You are to be the one seeking reconciliation.

The Story of a Pursuing God

Since the fall, the story of God in the Bible has become the story of a God who pursues those who betrayed Him, undermined Him, and broke trust with Him.

He pursues Adam, then Israel, then we Gentiles — that's why we are here.
He pursues Peter — who denied Him three times, abandoning Him in the very hour of His crisis.
He pursues Thomas. He pursues Paul.
Thank God He pursued me. Did He pursue you?
Part 3

So What Does He Require?

What does a pursuing God expect from us in a broken world?

First: Forgive

"And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you."

Ephesians 4:32 (NKJV)

When?

The first and best time to forgive is in advance. The second best time is now. If there is someone you have not yet forgiven — forgive them now.

You need no other motivation than this: God said it. You will benefit — but whether you benefit is irrelevant. It is a matter of obedience.

Forgiveness Defined

To Forgive Is To…

Cancel the debt that you believe the person owes you for something they said or did to you.

When someone offends you, you believe they took something away from you — your peace, your well-being, your dignity. Sometimes physical, most often emotional. And so you feel: they ought to pay.

Forgiveness happens in the heart: "I no longer require, demand, or even want you to pay me back for what you took from me. I cancel the debt. You are free."

The pain may still be there for a while. But the decision has been made.

He Canceled Before She Repented

They brought Him the woman caught in adultery. She was guilty. She had broken His law. He could have insisted on justice. Instead He said: "Neither do I condemn you."

He Did Not Wait

Jesus did not wait for her to repent. He did not even wait for her to acknowledge the wrong. He just canceled the debt.

And that is exactly what you do: "Listen — that's okay. You don't have to pay me back. That's fine."

Cancel It Now — And Free Yourself

Money is tangible. Someone borrows from you, and it stays on your mind. Every time you see them you want to say something. It is eating at you. Then one day you say: "You know what — you can have it." You did not say they didn't do you wrong. You just canceled the debt.

Don't Carry Emotional Debts

Most of those debts they can't pay back anyway. Cancel it right now. Don't go think about it. Don't say, "When they acknowledge it" — that person may never acknowledge it. You free them, but you also free yourself.

The Clarification

Cancel the Debt — Not the Person

"I'm not just canceling the debt — I'm canceling him."

Where We Got It Wrong

We said truthfully that you cannot make reconciliation happen, because it takes two. But some of us used that truth to justify canceling the person too: "God isn't requiring me to reconcile, so I can cancel him. The debt is canceled — but that person is canceled. I won't talk to him. I won't even greet her."

Is that what the God who pursues those who betray Him shows us to do? Is that part of His dream? What if all of us did that — a church full of people not talking to each other?

Say to Your Neighbor

"Neighbor, I remove you from my cancel list. I have canceled your debt, but I will never cancel you — because you are my family, created in the image of God. Christ died for you. I see the image of Jesus in you. I will not cancel him whom God loves."

So What Are We to Do?

"Pursue peace with all people…"

Hebrews 12:14 (NKJV)

Not canceling the debt alone — canceling the debt and pursuing peace. Not waiting for the person to pursue you.

With whom? All people. One version says "with everyone." Last time I checked, "everyone" includes the person you plan to cancel — or the person you have already canceled. So go back and reverse that action.

Two Different Things

Forgiveness — the heart, the past

Canceling the debt is done in the heart, and it deals with the person's past behavior.

Reconciliation — the behavior, the future

Pursuing peace is not something you do in the heart — it is something you actually do. It is your actions, and how you will treat that person going forward.

"In my heart I've canceled it. Great. Now: how am I going to behave?"

Declare a Unilateral Ceasefire

Simply speaking: treat people kindly. Treat the people you have forgiven the way you want to be treated.

A president can declare "no more bombs." The other side doesn't have to respond in kind — but they cannot prevent him from declaring the ceasefire.

Say It

Declare. Unilateral. Personal. Ceasefire. That means from now on — no more bombs.

Stop Lobbing Bombs

Sometimes you hide, so no one sees that you are the one who lobbed the bomb.

You went behind that person's back and said something to somebody about them because you wanted to hurt them.

You decided to walk past them and not say hello.

You heard something not too good about them — and instead of praying or keeping your mouth shut: "Ah, this is juicy. That's what I always believed about them. This is my opportunity."

Some of you have your own homemade bombs. It's unique to you. You know how to hurt. You know how to get back.

Once you have canceled the debt, you declare ceasefire: you stop the things you did and said because you were angry — and you replace them with things that are good.

What Pursuing Peace Looks Like

"But I say to you who hear: Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, bless those who curse you, and pray for those who spitefully use you."

Luke 6:27-28 (NKJV)

Love is not a feeling. Love is a decision to treat that person kindly. When He says "love your enemies," He tells you how: do good to them.

Bless — "Lord, help him. Lord, help his children. May his business prosper." Blessing those who curse you is far better than cursing them — for them, and for you.

Pray for them, not against them. There are imprecatory prayers — praying against the father, the mother, the children, the whole generation. But He did not say pray against. And notice the tense: pray for those who are spitefully using you right now.

He Said Pursue — Not Guarantee

God does not ask you to do what you cannot do. You cannot guarantee reconciliation, because you cannot guarantee the other person's behavior.

You Are Responsible for You

He is not holding you responsible for the other person's behavior — He is holding you responsible for yours. The other person may not apologize. That does not stop you from apologizing if you need to apologize.

If you are not living peaceably with somebody, let it not be because you are the source of the problem.

Peace, Reconciliation, Restoration

1. Peace

You cannot guarantee it, but you can pursue it — by being purposefully kind, loving, and helpful, even to those who wounded you.

2. Reconciliation

He did not say pursue closeness, or even friendship. Reconciliation is not closeness and it is not friendship.

3. Restoration

The restoring of trust and friendship. It becomes a possibility — not a guarantee — only when peace is real on both sides. God's first desire is to reach the place where restoration can happen.

Mark and Paul

Mark proved untrustworthy and lost Paul's trust. Paul was not mean-spirited and did not try to hurt Mark — he simply concluded, "I can't trust Mark because of his past behavior." The relationship was broken.

But later, that same Mark became Paul's most trusted helper.

"Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me for ministry."

2 Timothy 4:11 (NKJV)

If we pursue peace, act kindly, do good, and love the person, God can work a total transformation — the one who broke your heart becoming your most trusted friend. It's possible. It's not guaranteed.

Boundaries, Used Wisely

Do not hear this as "go restore trust immediately," before the person has demonstrated repentance and earned it. If someone is stealing from me, I can forgive them and still pursue peace — but on money there is a boundary. "I'm not canceling you out of my life; I'm canceling you out of my finances."

The boundary does not have to surround your whole life — only where trust was actually lost. In every other area we can laugh, we can eat together, we can talk about sports and church and prayer. Just not that area, for now. We leave room for the future.

But use them wisely. Some of the boundaries we establish are actually our way of punishing the person. That is not pursuing peace. That is not the example Christ showed us.

Put boundaries where you genuinely sense threat, danger, or real harm. Say it plainly and kindly: "I love you, and we're good — but in this area right now, I don't want us to go there." If they respect it, you can still have a good relationship. Given time, trust may be rebuilt. If it isn't, that is not your responsibility.

The Border Illustration

Two countries exist next to each other — Liberia and Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone. There is always a border. That border is the boundary.

When trust is good

The border is still there — but nobody bothers to enforce it. "I don't have to enforce it, because I trust you."

When trust becomes questionable

The greater the danger and the distrust, the more the border is defended and enforced — until you sign a peace treaty.

As Much As Depends On You

"If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men."

Romans 12:18 (NKJV)

God Himself Says It Is Not Always Possible

But as much as it depends upon you, you make it your goal to live peaceably with all men. If you are not living peaceably with somebody, let it not be because you are the source of the problem.

Response

Prayer Points

Living the Father's Dream

1. Pull Up Every Root

Prayer Focus

Father, search me. Where an offense I never handled has become a root of bitterness, I pull it up today. I will not let it cause trouble in me, in my home, or in Your church, and I will not let many be defiled through me. (Hebrews 12:15)

2. I Cancel the Debt

Prayer Focus

Lord, I stop carrying emotional debts I want people to pay. Right now, not later, not when they acknowledge it — I cancel the debt. I no longer require, demand, or want repayment for what was taken from me. They are free, and so am I. (Ephesians 4:32)

3. Off My Cancel List

Prayer Focus

Father, I have canceled the debt — but I will never cancel the person. I remove every name from my cancel list. I will not cancel one whom You love, one created in Your image, one for whom Christ died. (Genesis 1:27; Romans 5:8)

4. A Unilateral Ceasefire

Prayer Focus

Lord, I declare a personal, unilateral ceasefire. No more bombs — no more words behind backs, no more walking past without a greeting, no more feeding on what is juicy. I lay down every homemade weapon I have learned to use. (Hebrews 12:14)

5. Bless and Pray, Not Curse

Prayer Focus

Teach me to love my enemies by doing good to them, to bless those who curse me, and to pray for — never against — those who are spitefully using me right now. Let my mouth carry blessing where it used to carry ammunition. (Luke 6:27-28)

6. Wise Boundaries

Prayer Focus

Give me wisdom with boundaries. Let me place them only where trust was genuinely broken and harm is real — never as a way of punishing someone. And where You are able, restore what was destroyed, as You restored Mark to Paul. (2 Timothy 4:11; Romans 12:18)

Declaration

I Will Live the Father's Dream

"I cancel the debt — and I will never cancel the person.
I am the one to whom gossip comes to die.
I do not wait to be pursued; like my Father, I pursue.
I declare a unilateral ceasefire: from today, no more bombs.
I cannot guarantee peace — but I can pursue it,
and as much as it depends on me, I will live peaceably with all men.
The Father is still dreaming — and I am living His dream."

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

Living The Father's Dream — Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

This is Part 2 of a message on forgiveness and reconciliation that grew out of the teaching on meekness. Bishop Johnson returns to the subject deliberately, because after the first message some people were still confused — including a listener in Africa who wrote on WhatsApp asking about Hebrews 12:14-15. The confusion was serious enough to correct, because a root of bitterness left unhandled does not stay private: it causes trouble, opens a door to the enemy, spreads through a congregation, and defiles many.

The message answers a single question in two movements. First: what is God's dream for His family? A family with no war between members, no bitterness, no revenge, no silent treatment, no score-keeping, no gossip, no verdicts reached on one side of a story — the Eden reality Jesus embraced and commanded when He said "love one another as I have loved you." Second: what does that God require of us in a broken world? Forgive — cancel the debt. But then the correction that gives the message its edge: canceling the debt never means canceling the person. Hebrews 12:14 does not stop at forgiveness; it says pursue peace with all people — and "everyone" includes the person you have already written off.

Summary 1 of 7

Key Scriptures (I)

Hebrews 12:14-15 — "Pursue peace with all people, and holiness… lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by this many become defiled." The anchor text, carrying both halves of the message: the danger of unhandled offense, and the positive command that goes beyond forgiveness to the active pursuit of peace.

Genesis 3:9 — "Adam, where are you?" The offended One comes looking. God, who had every right to wait for an apology, is the one who initiates.

John 13:34 — "Love one another; as I have loved you." Jesus did not revise the Father's vision for the family — He embraced it and raised the standard past loving your neighbor as yourself.

2 Corinthians 5:19 — "God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself." He who was hurt was the One committed to reconciling.

Romans 5:8 — God commends His love in that while we were still sinners Christ died for us. God does not wait for the offender to make the first move.

Summary 2 of 7

Key Scriptures (II)

Matthew 5:23-24 — leave your gift at the altar when you remember that your brother has something against you. Note the direction: the text does not say you have something against him. You are to be the one seeking reconciliation.

Ephesians 4:32 — "Forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you." The command needs no supporting motive beyond the fact that God said it.

Luke 6:27-28 — love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, bless those who curse you, pray for those who spitefully use you. This is what pursuing peace actually looks like in behavior, and the present tense matters: pray for those using you right now.

John 8:1-11 — the woman caught in adultery. She was guilty; He could have insisted on justice. He canceled the debt before she repented and before she acknowledged the wrong.

2 Timothy 4:11 — "Get Mark… he is useful to me for ministry." The man who lost Paul's trust became his most trusted helper. Restoration is possible.

Romans 12:18 — "If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men." The closing text: God Himself admits it is not always possible, and still fixes the responsibility on you.

Summary 3 of 7

Main Teachings (I)

Bitterness is never a private matter — an offense mishandled becomes bitterness, bitterness causes trouble, and trouble spreads. Two people in a local church can defile many: sides get taken, relationships fall, the work of God is hindered, and Satan is given an opening.

God has a dream for His family — not division, not spouses living as strangers, not siblings avoiding each other, not a church fractured by offense; but love, genuine care, no suspicion, no broken trust, all living in harmony. That dream was real in Eden and is still what the Father is dreaming.

Jesus embraced the Father's vision rather than replacing it — and raised the bar: love one another not merely as you love yourself, but as much as I have loved you, which is love enough to die.

Sin's first casualty was relationship — before the fall, God and Adam and Eve were family, close, trusting, walking in the cool of the day. The greatest thing sin damaged was not the environment but the relationship: first with God, then between Adam and Eve, and by the second generation the first family had become the first battleground.

Hiding is the silent treatment — Adam's response to the fracture was our response: get away, stay away, limit dealings as much as possible. We give someone a piece of our mind and then withdraw entirely.

Summary 4 of 7

Main Teachings (II)

The kingdom reverses the world's order — the world says cut him off, stop speaking, build walls, deny access, and you are within your rights. In the kingdom the offended One comes looking, the one who is right seeks the one who is wrong, and the one who was hurt is the one trying to fix it. "Adam, where are you?"

The Bible is the story of a pursuing God — He pursues Adam, Israel, the Gentiles, Peter who denied Him in His hour of crisis, Thomas, Paul. "Thank God He pursued me. Did He pursue you?"

Forgiveness is canceling a debt — an offense is experienced as something taken from you: your peace, your well-being, your dignity. To forgive is to decide in your heart, "I no longer require, demand, or even want you to pay me back." The pain may remain for a while; the decision has still been made.

Forgive in advance, or forgive now — the best time is before the offense; the second best is immediately. Do not wait for acknowledgement; that person may never acknowledge it. Canceling frees them, but it also frees you.

Forgiveness and reconciliation operate differently — forgiveness happens in the heart and deals with the past; reconciliation is behavior and deals with the future. "In my heart I've canceled it. Great. Now — how am I going to behave?"

Summary 5 of 7

Principles & Warnings

Cancel the debt — never the person. The central correction of the message. "It takes two to reconcile" is true, but it has been misused to justify writing people off. You may not cancel one whom God loves, who bears His image, and for whom Christ died.

"Everyone" includes the one you excluded. Hebrews 12:14 says pursue peace with all people — which means going back and reversing the cancellation you already made.

Pursue, don't guarantee. God never asks you to do what you cannot do. You cannot control the other person's response; you are answerable only for your own behavior. Their refusal to apologize does not excuse you from apologizing.

Peace, reconciliation, and restoration are three different things. Reconciliation is not closeness or friendship; restoration — the rebuilding of trust — is a further step, a possibility rather than a guarantee, and only after peace is real on both sides.

Warning: boundaries used as punishment. "Some of the boundaries we establish are actually our ways of punishing the person. That is not pursuing peace. That is not the example Christ showed us." A wise boundary is placed only where trust was genuinely broken or real harm threatens — never around the whole of a person's life unless they are dangerous in every area.

Warning: the silent treatment and gossip. Refusing to greet, refusing to speak, repeating what is "juicy," reaching verdicts after hearing one side — these are the bombs, and some are homemade and unique to you.

Summary 6 of 7

Takeaways & Declarations

Cancel the debt today, not when they admit it. "Don't go think about it. Cancel it." Waiting for acknowledgement is waiting for something that may never come.

Remove the names from your cancel list. The congregation was told to say it to a neighbor out loud: "I remove you from my cancel list. I have canceled your debt, but I will never cancel you."

Declare a unilateral, personal ceasefire. The other side does not have to agree; they cannot stop you from declaring it. From today, no more bombs — and replace the harmful things with things that are good.

Do good, bless, pray. Love is not a feeling; it is a decision to treat someone kindly. Pray for those spitefully using you — not the imprecatory prayers aimed at a person's parents, children, and whole generation.

Enforce borders only when trust requires it. As with neighboring countries — Liberia and Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone — the border always exists, but where trust is good, nobody bothers to enforce it. Distrust is what makes a boundary defended, and only until a peace treaty is signed.

The altar call. Come if you have been unable to cancel the debt; come if you canceled it but still cannot do good and pursue peace; come if you are unsure whether you are using boundaries rightly. This is not to remain a sermon — it is to be acted upon.

Prophetic word. "By prophetic revelation, I know some of you are not speaking to some of you." And: the Father is still dreaming — what we can only imagine now will one day be reality.

Lukewarmness: The Sin That Causes Jesus To Spit

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Revelation 3:15-20

Sins We Are Not To Tolerate

Continuing the Series

"We want to become aware of them, and we want to take action against them, because God is not glorified by sin."

"Lukewarmness, the sin which causes Jesus to spit. Now, that sounds kind of gross, but that's what Jesus himself said."

The Principal Text

"I know your works, that you are neither cold nor hot. I could wish you were cold or hot. So then, because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will vomit you out of My mouth."

Revelation 3:15-16 (NKJV)

Jesus speaking to the church at Laodicea

Rich, Yet Wretched

"Because you say, 'I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing,' and do not know that you are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked... As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten. Therefore be zealous and repent. Behold, I stand at the door and knock."

Revelation 3:17, 19-20 (NKJV)

Part 1

Replace It With Zeal

Zeal for the things of God is the opposite of being lukewarm

The Phone at 3%

  • Imagine your telephone has only 3% power and is about to die.
  • "You don't yell at the phone. You don't shake it hard. You don't speak to it and demand that it has power."
  • You find a charger and plug it in, and 3% becomes 100%, "because power outside of the phone flowed into the phone."
  • Hold that thought. You plugged it into a power source other than itself.

What Zeal Means

An old religious word, but it simply means to be burning with passion: great energy and enthusiasm in the pursuit of God, His purpose, and His things.

All in, not half-hearted.

You care deeply about God, the work of God, the church, souls, and the kingdom.

Never passive.

"We cannot be zealous and at the same time be passive." Zeal moves you to action.

Two Things Today

1. Lukewarm is not okay.

"When God's people are lukewarm, Jesus isn't okay with it... So I'm here to challenge you, and I'm here to challenge myself, to exhort us from the Word of God to burn."

2. The good news: God supplies the fire.

"He who commands zeal will supply us with the zeal. He who commands the fire will give us the fire. So you don't have to try to manufacture it."

Hear the Word of the Lord

At Friday's prayer meeting the leader independently focused on not being lukewarm. "In the mouth of two or three witnesses, every word shall be established." This is the word for this weekend.

A Word to the Church

"God does not want a church that is lukewarm. God does not want a people that are lukewarm. God does not want believers that are lukewarm... Don't be a lukewarm Christian. And our church must not become a lukewarm church. Hear that as the word of the Lord to you."

Part 2

The Disease Itself

Let's be honest about lukewarmness

Not a Threat of Hell

  • "I will spit you out of my mouth" is not about losing your salvation. These are born-again believers, and there is no threat of hell here.
  • Jesus is describing how He feels: "I find lukewarmness very disgusting. I take no pleasure in lukewarmness."
  • "I don't swallow lukewarmness, and I don't want you to swallow it either."
  • He will not excuse it, He will not tolerate it, and His whole attitude is: it needs to go.

Make His Attitude Yours

"Find it disgusting. Find it unacceptable. Find it intolerable. Decide, 'I don't want to have anything to do with it, and I'm going to reject it and make no excuses for it.'"

Say It

"I reject lukewarmness. I make no excuses for it. I do not tolerate it. And I will not accept it in my life. And I will not accept it in my church."

What It Means To Be Lukewarm

  • Spiritually passive. Complacent, satisfied.
  • Double-minded. "One day you're here, the other day you're there."
  • Indifferent. "Unmoved by what moves God, undisturbed by what disturbs Him."
  • Without boldness. You can no longer pray, love, give, or obey boldly. "Your convictions aren't strong enough to move your feet."

The Middle Is Not Safe

Hot or Cold

"I want you to have a stand. I want you to be extreme in your commitment and in your passion in the things of God. I don't want you to just kind of serve me. I don't want you to just kind of love me. I want you to go all the way."

Being lukewarm deceives you because it feels comfortable. It lulls you into a spiritual slumber.

"You Kind Of Believe"

Holiness

You believe in holiness, but you excuse sin: "Nobody's perfect. After all, we're all human."

Grace

"It's mostly about grace, but I still got to earn my keep."

Giving

"You got so many expenses... God understands I got all these other needs."

God hasn't called us to be a kind-of people. Real caring and commitment translate into action.

Part 3

The Cause: Jesus at the Door

"Behold, I stand at the door and knock"

Where Is Jesus Standing?

  • Look at verse 20. Jesus is outside, at the door, while the people sit inside at the table having supper.
  • You knock to get the attention of the people inside. Christ is present, but they are not paying attention.
  • He who should be at the head of the table is outside, trying to get their attention, and they are ignoring Him.
  • "This is perhaps the number one reason for lukewarmness."

Why Would You Leave Him There?

"What disrespect for the presence of the Lord, that I would allow him to be present but really, in essence, ignore him." There is only one reason you would:

"I've Got Things Under Control"

"At this point in my life, I really don't think I need to be that close to him... It's good to have him there, where I can access him when I need to, but he doesn't really need to be part of this conversation."

If you felt you really needed Him, He would be at the head of the table with your undivided attention.

"I Am Rich, I Have Need of Nothing"

  • You feel okay because you are self-deceived into thinking you are managing things quite well. That is exactly what the Laodiceans were saying.
  • Many born-again Christians believe the only time they need to be close to Him is in crisis. "Otherwise, Jesus, we are doing quite well, thank you."
  • A nice home, nice clothes, nice cars, reasonable health: "We'll treat you as a visitor for now, and when we really have a crisis, then we'll bring you in."
  • "If that is your approach in your relationship with Christ, you will end up being lukewarm."

Common Grace Is Not Closeness

"He makes His sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust."

Matthew 5:45 (NKJV)

Application

Even unbelievers can have food, clothes, a nice home, and good doctors. All of that is possible with Christ at the door. But leave the phone unplugged long enough and all its power drains. "Jesus is the source of passion. Jesus is the source of the zeal of God."

Your Position and Your Condition

Your position never changes

In Christ. Forgiven. Blessed with all spiritual blessings. A child of God.

Your condition can be quite different

"Wretched, miserable, poor, naked, and blind": the condition of every believer who treats Jesus as a visitor rather than as Lord.

The Tip of the Iceberg

"One's life does not consist in the abundance of the things he possesses."

Luke 12:15 (NKJV)

"The greater part of you, and the greater part of your existence, is not seen. It's spiritual. You're far more a spiritual person than you are a physical person. And if you neglect your spiritual life in favor of your physical life, you're neglecting the greater part of you."

Spiritually, There Is Always a Crisis

  • "You need him far more than you realize. You don't need him just in times of crisis. You need him now, because spiritually there's always a crisis."
  • "Every decision ought to begin with a consultation with Christ. Every plan ought to begin with him."
  • Days, weeks, or only Sunday mornings without Him reveal that you don't sense how much you need Him every day.
  • His patience is His love: the Son of God stands outside and keeps knocking while we ignore Him.
Part 4

Zeal Is God's Will

Two scriptures that reveal what He wants for your life

Purchased To Be Zealous

"Who gave Himself for us, that He might redeem us from every lawless deed and purify for Himself His own special people, zealous for good works."

Titus 2:14 (NKJV)

Application

"Jesus didn't just die to save us from hell. He didn't just die to forgive our sins. He died in order to obtain, to purchase with his own blood, a people who are zealous for good works." Zealous to see souls saved, the work of God done, and the kingdom expand to the nations.

Fervent in Spirit

"Not lagging in diligence, fervent in spirit, serving the Lord."

Romans 12:11 (NKJV)

  • Fervent is the Greek word for zeal: boiling. Present tense. "Be continually boiling."
  • Not lagging means no dragging your feet. "I'll serve tomorrow. I'll pray tomorrow. I'll give." Stop procrastinating.
  • Serving is the test. "You cannot possess the zeal of the Lord and not be serving."

While It Is Day

"I must work the works of Him who sent Me while it is day; the night is coming when no one can work."

John 9:4 (NKJV)

"You only have so much time to do what God has called you to do. You're alive today. You don't know if you'll be alive tomorrow." While you have breath in your lungs, today is the day to be about the Father's business. That is the zeal of the Lord manifested in Christ.

Part 5

Two Examples of Zeal

Phinehas and Jesus in the temple

Phinehas: God's Zeal in a Man

Numbers 25. Israel fell into idolatry with the Midianites, with no shame left. While the nation mourned and fasted, one man brought a Midianite woman into his tent. Phinehas rose up, took a javelin, and the judgment stopped.

"He was zealous with My zeal among them, so that I did not consume the children of Israel in My zeal."

Numbers 25:11 (NKJV)

"Phinehas felt what I felt. Phinehas was moved by what I was moved by. Phinehas rejected what I rejected."

You Have a Decision To Make

When the World and the Church Embrace What Dishonors God

"Will we change our convictions because the world says it's okay, or because everybody in the church says it's okay? Or will we, like Phinehas, rise up with zeal, caring for the things that God cares about?"

In the new covenant we fight with no sword or gun. You take the Word of God, you take prayer, and you wage war on behalf of God's will in the church and in the nation.

Enough Is Enough. It Stops Now.

"Begin with yourself. Some of the things that need to be killed and put to death are the things that you are tolerating." A holy anger needs to rise up inside us:

  • "There's enough fornication. It stops now."
  • "There's enough making of excuses. It stops now."
  • "There's enough not speaking to my neighbor. It stops now."
  • "There's enough worldliness. It stops now."

Jesus Overturns the Tables

John 2. The place set aside for God to be honored had become a place of business. Jesus did not lose His temper; He prepared a cord, drove out the money changers, overturned the tables, and then healed the sick and the maimed.

"Zeal for Your house has eaten Me up."

Psalm 69:9; John 2:17 (NKJV)

"There are some tables of lukewarmness in our lives that need to be overturned." Spit it out in your own life first.

Part 6

How Do I Overcome Lukewarmness?

"I cannot manufacture the zeal. I've tried that."

Step 1: Recognize It

You Cannot Change What You Deny

"If you keep pretending like you're okay spiritually, you're not going to make the changes that need to be made." Say it honestly: "I'm lukewarm with regard to prayer. I'm lukewarm with regard to the Word. I'm lukewarm with regard to my involvement in the church."

  • Don't blame other people. Don't say it's the church's fault.
  • Don't make excuses. Don't say you're too busy. No condemnation, but recognize it.

Step 2: Repent

  • To repent is to turn, to change the way you're thinking, especially about self-sufficiency.
  • "You're thinking that you're fine. You're thinking that you're managing everything. That is a lie that you need to repent of."
  • Say: "I need Him. Every day. Every moment of every day. I cannot do without Him. There's always a crisis in the spirit if Christ is not at the table."

Step 3: Get Plugged Back In

"And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus."

Philippians 4:19 (NKJV)

Jesus Is the Source of Your Zeal

"Just like that cell phone doesn't fire up itself, it receives power from a source outside of itself. The zeal of the Lord is in Christ... What do you do with a believer who lacks zeal? You plug him into the source of zeal. The source of zeal is Jesus Christ. Not your willpower. Jesus."

How Do You Plug In? With Faith.

Put your hand on your belly and say it

"Jesus, I plug into your zeal. I make your zeal my own. Jesus in me, you are the zeal of God working in me now. You are the fire of God setting me on fire. You are the zeal of God making me zealous. You are the power of God. You are the fire of God working in me now."

Step 4: Act As Though It Is True

"Jesus said to him, 'Rise, take up your bed and walk.' And immediately the man was made well, took up his bed, and walked."

John 5:8-9 (NKJV)

The Command Carries the Power

Thirty-eight years, never walked, and still could not walk when he met Jesus. "The very command possessed the power to make him do what he couldn't do." If he had sat there, he never would have walked.

Take Up Your Bible By Faith

  • "Stop looking at yourself for zeal. Stop looking at man for zeal. Stop looking at your church for zeal. You look to Jesus."
  • Decide: "Because the zeal of God is working in me in Christ Jesus, I'm going to take up my Bible." As you open it in faith, the zeal you need for the Scriptures starts to come to you.
  • Do the same with prayer: "Even though you have no zeal, act like you have some zeal," believing the One who is zealous for prayer is operating in you.
  • You will find yourself developing a love for the Word, for prayer, and for fellowship.

By Faith, Not By Law

Law

"Okay, I'm going to make up my mind. I'm going to start reading my Bible." That's law.

Faith

"Jesus, you are the zeal of God in me. You are working this in me. As you are working in me, you are making me zealous with your zeal. I trust you."

"I labored more abundantly than they all, yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me." 1 Corinthians 15:10

The Power of Christ Is Available Right Now

"What you could not do in your own strength, you are now empowered to do"

If you realize you are lukewarm and have been for some time, come and let the elders and pastors agree with you. If you have never received Jesus as your Savior, come and receive the gift of salvation now. "Christ is here. The word has been spoken. The power of God is working now."

Sermon Summary

Lukewarmness: The Sin That Causes Jesus To Spit - Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

Continuing the series on sins the church must not tolerate, Bishop Johnson preaches from Jesus' letter to Laodicea. Lukewarmness, the half-hearted and self-satisfied spiritual state, is so distasteful to Jesus that He says He will spit it out. That is not a threat to anyone's salvation but a description of how Jesus feels, and He wants His people to feel the same way about it.

The diagnosis - Believers become lukewarm when Jesus is present in their lives but left standing at the door, treated as a visitor to call on in a crisis, while they manage life "quite well" at the table without Him.

The cure - Not willpower. Like a phone at 3% plugged into a charger, the believer is re-plugged into Christ, the source of zeal, by faith, and then acts on that faith before the feeling arrives.

Context - Preached at Harvest Intercontinental Church, Olney, the Sunday after the Bishop returned from travel, and confirmed by an independent word on the same theme at the Friday prayer meeting.

Key Scriptures (1)

Revelation 3:15-20 - "Because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will vomit you out of My mouth... Therefore be zealous and repent. Behold, I stand at the door and knock." The foundation text: Jesus' revulsion, the self-deception behind it ("I am rich... have need of nothing"), and the remedy and invitation in the same passage.

Titus 2:14 - "Purify for Himself His own special people, zealous for good works." Christ died to purchase a zealous people, not a passive one.

Romans 12:11 - "Not lagging in diligence, fervent in spirit, serving the Lord." Fervent means boiling, present tense; service is the evidence of zeal.

2 Corinthians 6:2 - "Now is the accepted time; now is the day of salvation." Stop procrastinating on prayer, giving, and obedience.

Summary 3 of 10

Main Teachings (1)

What lukewarmness is - Neither cold nor hot: spiritually passive, complacent, double-minded, indifferent, "unmoved by what moves God, undisturbed by what disturbs Him." It feels safe because it sits in the middle, but "being in the middle is not a good place when it comes to the things of God." Jesus wants people hot or cold, with a stand, extreme in commitment, not "kind of" serving and "kind of" loving Him.

How it shows up - Half-believing. You believe in holiness but excuse sin ("nobody's perfect"). You believe in grace but feel you still have to earn your keep. You believe in honoring God with tithes and offerings but let expenses override it. Lukewarmness dulls boldness: you can no longer pray, love, give, or obey boldly, because conviction is too weak to move your feet, hands, or mouth.

Jesus' attitude - Vomiting or spitting is not a loss of salvation for born-again believers; there is no threat of hell in the text. It is His revulsion. He finds lukewarmness disgusting, will not swallow it, excuse it, or tolerate it, and He wants His people to adopt the same attitude toward it in their own lives and in the church: "I reject lukewarmness. I make no excuses for it."

Zeal defined - Burning with passion, great energy and enthusiasm in pursuing God and His purpose. All in, not half-hearted. Caring deeply about God, His work, the church, souls, and the kingdom. "We cannot be zealous and at the same time be passive."

Summary 4 of 10

Main Teachings (2)

The root cause: Jesus at the door - In verse 20 Jesus stands outside knocking while the believers sit inside at the table. He is present in their lives but not given their attention. "This is perhaps the number one reason for lukewarmness." Leaving Him at the door reveals a belief that He is not needed closely right now: "I've got things under control."

The self-deception of sufficiency - "I am rich, I have need of nothing." Many born-again Christians feel they only need to be close to Him in a crisis. A nice home, nice clothes, nice cars, and reasonable health make Him a visitor to be brought in when trouble comes. Because of common grace, even unbelievers can be provided for, so physical comfort is no evidence of spiritual closeness. Leave the phone unplugged long enough and its power drains.

Position and condition - Your position in Christ never changes: forgiven, blessed with all spiritual blessings, a child of God. But your condition can be "wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked" when Jesus is kept at the door instead of at the head of the table, involved in every decision, conversation, and business.

Always a crisis in the spirit - "You need him far more than you realize... spiritually there's always a crisis." Every decision and every plan should begin with a consultation with Christ. His patient knocking shows how much He loves you and wants to be close.

Summary 5 of 10

Main Teachings (3)

Zeal is God's will - Titus 2:14: Jesus did not die only to save from hell or forgive sins; He purchased a special people zealous for good works, zealous to see souls saved and the kingdom expand to the nations. Romans 12:11: be fervent (boiling, continually) in spirit; not lagging in diligence means stop dragging your feet and procrastinating. "You can be very busy and not possess the zeal of the Lord, but you cannot possess the zeal of the Lord and not be serving." Jesus' own zeal: "I must work the works of Him who sent Me while it is day."

Phinehas - When Israel openly embraced Midianite idolatry and immorality, one man refused to accept what everyone else tolerated and the judgment stopped. God said, "My zeal was in him": Phinehas felt what God felt, rejected what God rejected. When the world and even the church embrace what dishonors God, we must decide whether to conform or rise up.

Enough is enough - In the new covenant the weapons are the Word and prayer, not a sword. But "begin with yourself": a holy anger must rise up against what you tolerate. "There's enough fornication. It stops now. There's enough making of excuses. It stops now. There's enough not speaking to my neighbor. It stops now. There's enough worldliness. It stops now."

Jesus in the temple - He did not lose His temper; He prepared a cord, drove out the money changers, overturned the tables, then healed the sick and the maimed, fulfilling "zeal for Your house has eaten Me up." There are tables of lukewarmness in our lives that need to be overturned so we can be the house of prayer God called us to be.

Summary 6 of 10

The Remedy: Four Steps

1. Recognize - "You cannot change what you deny." Look honestly and name the areas: prayer, the Word, involvement in the church. Don't blame others, don't make excuses, don't say you're too busy. No condemnation, but recognize it.

2. Repent - Change the way you think about self-sufficiency. "You're thinking that you're managing everything... That is a lie that you need to repent of." Replace it with "I need Him every day, every moment of every day."

3. Get re-plugged in - Zeal is not produced by your effort. Jesus is the source of your zeal, and you plug in with faith, not willpower. Release your faith daily by confession: "Jesus, you are the fire of God burning in me now. You are the zeal of God working in me now." Then "you end up receiving the Lord's zeal as your own."

4. Act as though it is true - The man at Bethesda could not walk until Jesus commanded it, and the command carried the power, but he had to attempt to rise. Take up your Bible by faith and the zeal for the Scriptures will come. Get on your knees, "and even though you have no zeal, act like you have some zeal." Do it by faith, not as an act of law ("I'm going to make up my mind to read my Bible"), and "a power that is not inherent in you begins to flow into you," as Paul said: "I labored, yet not I, but the grace of God."

Summary 7 of 10

Principles

The middle is not neutral - God values wholehearted devotion; sitting between hot and cold is not a safe place.

Presence is not centrality - Christ can be present in a believer's life and still be left at the door while something else sits at the head of the table.

Prosperity proves nothing - Common grace provides for the just and the unjust, so comfort and health are not evidence of closeness to Christ.

Position is fixed, condition drifts - A believer's standing in Christ never changes, but their condition can be wretched, poor, blind, and naked.

Zeal is supplied, not manufactured - "He who commands zeal will supply us with the zeal." It flows from Christ into the believer who is plugged in by faith.

Recognition precedes change - "You cannot change what you deny."

Faith acts before it feels - A concrete step of obedience releases the power already given; the feeling follows the step.

Effort and grace work together - "I make the effort, but it's the grace of God working in me that produces it."

Zeal always serves - True zeal results in action and service; it cannot remain internal or emotional.

Summary 8 of 10

Warnings and Cautions

Do not hear a threat of hell - "I will spit you out" expresses Jesus' intense displeasure, not a loss of salvation. No guilt, no condemnation, but repentance.

Beware "I have need of nothing" - Self-sufficiency is self-deception. Do not mistake managing well for being spiritually well.

Do not make Christ a crisis resource - Treating Him as a visitor to bring in when trouble comes creates the conditions for lukewarmness.

Do not excuse sin or complacency - "Nobody's perfect," "we're all human," and "I have too many expenses" are the language of a kind-of believer.

Do not try to manufacture zeal - Willpower produces one week of effort followed by a crash back into old habits.

Do not procrastinate - "I'll pray tomorrow. I'll give tomorrow." You do not know if you will be alive tomorrow.

Do not let the crowd reset your convictions - When the world, or everybody in the church, says something is okay, that is not a safe standard.

Comfort is the trap - Lukewarmness feels fine, which is exactly what makes it deceptive and able to lull you to sleep.

Summary 9 of 10

Takeaways and Declarations

Examine yourself - Name specifically where you are lukewarm: prayer, the Word, giving, church involvement, relationships.

Decide like Jesus - Reject lukewarmness, refuse to excuse it, refuse to tolerate it in your life and in the church.

Move Him to the table - Begin every decision and plan with a consultation with Christ instead of calling on Him only in emergencies.

Take the step before the feeling - Open your Bible, get on your knees, show up to serve, and trust that the zeal follows the act of faith.

Confess daily - "Jesus, I plug into your zeal. I make your zeal my own." Depend on Christ, not on motivation.

Overturn your tables - Identify the compromise, excuses, unresolved offense, or worldliness you tolerate, and say "it stops now."

Respond - If you recognize lukewarmness, come for prayer with the elders and pastors. If you have never received Jesus, come and receive the gift of salvation.

Word of the Lord to the church - "God does not want a church that is lukewarm... Don't be a lukewarm Christian. And our church must not become a lukewarm church." Confirmed by two witnesses.

Closing declaration - "Father, I repent of self-sufficiency. I repent of believing that I only need you in times of crisis... Jesus, your fire is working in me now. Your zeal is working in me now... I take up my Bible. I take up prayer... the zeal that I need shall be supplied, is being supplied, by you."

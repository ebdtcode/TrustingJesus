The Great Commission • Climax of the Month

Partnering With God

“Don't donate to the Great Commission. Own it.”

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Philippians 4:10-20 • August 2026

Our Reason for Being

"But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."

Acts 1:8 (NKJV)

The Vision

One of the first things the Spirit does is give you power to be His witness. Our vision: win the lost at all costs, and make as many disciples for Christ as we possibly can in our generation. We live and work to see a world where no one will live or die without Christ. May that be more than words on a banner.

A Thank-You Letter From Prison

Paul writes to the Philippians from a Roman prison: chained, in distress, lacking the things that would make life bearable. The Philippian church had an opportunity to send relief, and they seized it.

So Paul writes, in part, to say thank you. But in thanking them, what the Spirit gives us is a theological treatise: one of the most powerful passages on what it means to be partners together with the Lord.

At Harvest we endeavor not just to be hearers of the Word, but doers of the Word (James 1:22).

Rejoicing Greatly in Chains

"But I rejoice in the Lord greatly that now at last your care for me has flourished again; though you surely did care, but you lacked opportunity."

Philippians 4:10 (NKJV)

Extremely Happy

Not merely “I rejoice,” but “I rejoice greatly.” Many of us cannot rejoice when something small doesn't go our way. Here is a man in prison, chained, possibly facing death, and he is extremely happy. Why was Paul rejoicing so greatly?

Once and Again

"Nevertheless you have done well that you shared in my distress. Now you Philippians know also that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church shared with me concerning giving and receiving but you only. For even in Thessalonica you sent aid once and again for my necessities."

Philippians 4:14-16 (NKJV)

When few were willing to support the mission, this one church took a personal interest in Paul the missionary. Not once, but over and over: whenever they had opportunity, they gave. It was a habit.

“I Have All and Abound”

"Indeed I have all and abound. I am full, having received from Epaphroditus the things sent from you, a sweet-smelling aroma, an acceptable sacrifice, well pleasing to God."

Philippians 4:18 (NKJV)

If You Stop There, You Miss the Point

They gave so generously that a man in chains could say “I have more than I need.” But Paul is not saying, “I'm so happy I received money.” He is grateful for that. It is not the reason for his great joy.

The Key

What Your Giving Reveals

“The reason I am so grateful is not so much because of what your giving will do for me. I am rejoicing because of what your giving reveals about you, and what your giving will do for you.”

Your generosity will not only support God's work and help souls be saved. The level of your support for God's work will impact you. You stand personally to profit from your giving.

“Thank You, Lord, for the opportunity You give me to be a part of what You are doing.”

Part 1

The Secret of Contentment

Why Paul's joy was not tied to his circumstances

“I Have Learned”

"Not that I speak in regard to need, for I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content: I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound. Everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need."

Philippians 4:11-12 (NKJV)

Not Feeling Sorry for Himself

Paul was not in prison depressed, feeling forsaken by God. “My rejoicing is not because you met my need.” He had learned to be so satisfied in Jesus that his joy did not depend on what was happening externally.

Have You Learned to Be Content?

Is your emotional state tied to your circumstances?

  • Are you happy when everything is going great, and miserable when things change?
  • Can you witness after a powerful answer to prayer, but when disappointment comes, your mouth shuts?
  • Can you serve when the business, the job, and the children are doing well, but not when things shift?
  • In a “Roman prison with chains,” facing possible death, are you able to rejoice?

“Lord, teach me the secret of being able to rejoice regardless of my circumstances.”

The Verse in Its Context

"I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me."

Philippians 4:13 (NKJV)

Not About Achieving Goals

We confess this facing a hard job, business, or goal, and that is fine, because it is true. But the context has nothing to do with achieving goals. Paul is saying: I can abound, I can be abased, I can go through difficult things and make it through, because Christ gives me strength. The secret of his contentment was the sufficiency of Jesus Christ.

Jesus Is Better

"For He Himself has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.'"

Hebrews 13:5 (NKJV)

In this world you will have tribulation (John 16:33). You are either just getting out of trouble or headed for one. But Christ in you is stronger: greater is He who is in me than he who is in the world (1 John 4:4).

Paul's one constant, in good times or bad: Jesus was with him. Thank God for the million dollars in the bank. Thank God for the ten million. But Jesus is better.

“I Can Also Say”

"Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil; for You are with me; Your rod and Your staff, they comfort me."

Psalm 23:4 (NKJV)

Not as a Sermon, but as a Prophecy to You

Yes, there's no money, no job, a physical challenge, children not behaving, a ministry not where we want it. You don't have to deny it. But I can also say: the Lord is with me. He was with Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego in the fire; with Daniel in the lions' den; with David facing Goliath. He is the same yesterday, today, and forever (Hebrews 13:8). So go tomorrow and meet the person you are afraid of meeting.

Part 2

Caring Leads to Sharing

What their generosity revealed about them

Do You Care?

“Your care for me has flourished again.” Their giving revealed hearts that cared: about the missionary, the minister, and most of all the mission. Ask yourself, and you have permission to ask me:

  • Do you care about the state of God's work, here and abroad?
  • Do you care whether the gospel is being preached and people are getting saved?
  • Do you care that there are needs on the mission field not yet supplied?
  • Do you care whether disciples are being made and churches are being built?
  • Do you care whether the poor are helped, the hungry fed, the naked clothed, in the name of Jesus?

You Can Give a Million Dollars and Not Care

"And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing."

1 Corinthians 13:3 (NKJV)

The First Question Is Not “How Much?”

Jesus looks at the heart. Before “how much am I going to give?” comes the more important question: do I even care? And if I care, how much? The things you care about, you think about, like your children or your business. If you care a lot, you will be asking about the needs of the kingdom, and nobody will have to ask you.

Their Caring Led to Their Sharing

When you care, it goes deeply. It goes beyond feelings and leads to actions. Because the Philippians cared deeply, they shared.

We cannot care deeply about the gospel and the mission of the church and not be motivated to share. It is a natural progression: when you care deeply, you naturally share your time, your talent, your treasure, to care for that which you care about.

“My prayer is that my caring will lead to a sense of responsibility: what has God given me? How can I participate? How can I contribute so that the mission of Christ in the world is carried out effectively?”

Part 3

Partners, Not Donors

The Greek word for “share” carries the idea of partnership

“You Partnered With Me”

In some translations Paul says, “you did well because you partnered with me.” Not “you donated to my mission.”

The attitude of a donor is different from the attitude of a partner, and it is not about the amount. A donor can give far more than a partner. The difference is your relationship to the work.

How do you see your personal relationship to the Great Commission? As simply one who donates? Or as a partner?

Donor or Partner?

The Donor

Sees the gift as a favor done for somebody else. No skin in the game. Nothing to gain or lose by giving or not giving, except maybe a tax break. Hopes they do well with it, but is not really concerned about the mission. A donor must be asked.

The Partner

Owns the work as a co-owner. When a partner gives, he is giving to himself. The success of the enterprise benefits me; its loss causes me to lose. So I have a personal interest in helping it succeed. A partner doesn't wait to be asked.

God Is Not Looking for Donors

There are many good causes we donate to: feeding the hungry, helping animals. Continue if it brings you joy. But when it comes to the mission of the church, God is not asking for your donation.

If you relate to God's work simply as a donor, you are going about it wrong, and you are cheating yourself of the benefits found in partnership.

God doesn't need anybody to do Him a favor. What He has done is give us an opportunity to co-labor together with Him, so that what we do in fulfilling the mission directly profits us, and not just Him.

Part 4

Your Eternal Account

Paul is using the language of an accountant

An Account With Your Name on It

"Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that abounds to your account."

Philippians 4:17 (NKJV)

Not in Chase Bank, but in Heaven's Bank

You have a personal, eternal account. God gives no details of His banking system, but enough to know the account exists and that it can grow. It grows in proportion to your generosity: your commitment of time, talent, and treasure to His work. Your generosity affects you. Your lack of generosity affects you. You have skin in the game.

Where the Risk Is Zero

"Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal; but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven... For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."

Matthew 6:19-21 (NKJV)

Invest your best in your heavenly account, because it will increase and the risk is zero. Whatever happens with tariffs or the economy, earthly accounts can disappear tomorrow. You don't have to choose between the two. But don't neglect your eternal account.

The Future That Was Far Away Is Here

Twenty-five, thirty years ago I opened a Roth IRA and never touched it. I had enough sense to know I had a future and would not always be young, so I forewent some gratification: pleasure, clothes, fast cars. That future has arrived, and it is better because I was willing to wait.

Think in those terms, but not only for earth. You have a future beyond this world. Don't give your best to a world that lasts for however long and neglect the one that is eternal.

My IRA will close when I die. Don't give your best to what death will close. Invest your best in the account that opens at death and is available to you for eternity.

“Lord, Improve My Caring”

Paul is not asking you to give. He is asking you to be a partner. Partner with God first, then with your local church. The question is not “how much will I give?” but “how much do I care?” The prayer is not “increase my giving” but “improve my caring.” When caring increases, giving follows.

A young adult heard that the media department needed equipment. Nobody asked him. He heard, he cared, and he gave $7,000. That's an owner, not a donor.

Don't donate to the Great Commission. Own it. Don't donate to God's work. Own it.

Three Motivations for Faithful Partners

1. You have a stake

Through giving, your heavenly account increases. You profit eternally. When the account here closes, another one you invested in benefits you forever. When you give, it is not going to my account; it is going to yours. (Philippians 4:17)

2. God will supply all your need

A promise from a God who cannot lie. Generosity does not leave you uncared for. (Philippians 4:19)

3. The glory of God

The number one reason, above all others: that my life would bring glory and honor to God my Father forever. (Philippians 4:20)

“I Cannot Out-Give God”

"And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus."

Philippians 4:19 (NKJV)

A Personal Commitment

“I give a lot. I'm not asking you to do anything I don't do.” Knowing this promise is true frees you to be generous. Plan wisely, but you don't have to be so tight, so stingy, because you fear your needs won't be met. He supplies not according to your riches, but His. Seek first the kingdom, and these things shall be added (Matthew 6:33).

The Greatest Motivation

Glory Forever and Ever

"Now to our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen."

Philippians 4:20 (NKJV)

If There Were No Skin in the Game

“I would still give just as much. I would still want to do even more.” The greatest reward is knowing my life, my words, my time, talent, treasure, and breath bring glory to God. Let that be your motivation for serving, for using your gifts, for giving.

Changing the Currency

Travel to France or China and you must change your dollars, because the dollar does not spend on their streets. The U.S. dollar by itself has no value in heaven. But you can change the currency.

When you invest dollars in God's work, you are literally transforming earthly currency into heavenly currency. Souls have value in heaven. Disciples have value. Preaching the gospel, witnessing, giving to missions and missionaries: all of it has value there.

God is glorified when the gospel is preached. Your giving, which lets this church gather and do what it does in the world, abounds to your account.

The Charge

Rich Toward God

The Philippians were looking for the opportunity, and when they had it, they seized it. At Harvest you have many opportunities to invest in the work of God. Determine which one you will seize, and make the most of it.

Don't wait to be asked. This is your business. This is God's business. You are a co-owner. You are a partner.

I want you to be rich on earth. But most importantly, I want you to be rich toward God. Whether or not that happens, let this prayer be answered: may God make you rich in heaven.

Prayer Points

  • Lord, teach me the secret Paul learned: to be content in whatever state I am, abased or abounding, hungry or full, because my joy rests in You and not in my circumstances (Philippians 4:11-12)
  • Thank You that You will never, no never, leave me nor forsake me; I can walk through the valley of the shadow of death and fear no evil, for You are with me (Hebrews 13:5; Psalm 23:4)
  • Christ in me is stronger than any pressure the enemy brings: greater is He who is in me than he who is in the world, and I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me (1 John 4:4; Philippians 4:13)
  • Lord, don't just increase my giving; improve my caring. Give me a heart that cares about souls, disciples, the mission field, and the poor, so deeply that it moves me to action (Philippians 4:10)
  • Forgive me for relating to Your work as a donor doing You a favor; make me a partner and co-owner of the mission who does not wait to be asked (Philippians 4:14-16)

Prayer Points

  • Let my time, talent, and treasure bear fruit that abounds to my eternal account, and keep me from giving my best to what death will close (Philippians 4:17; Matthew 6:19-21)
  • I trust Your promise to supply all my need according to Your riches in glory, so I refuse to be tight or stingy; I will seek first Your kingdom and give without fear (Philippians 4:19; Matthew 6:33)
  • Open my eyes to the opportunities in front of me, and give me the readiness of the Philippians to seize them once and again (Philippians 4:10, 16)
  • Take what I give and change it into heavenly currency: souls saved, disciples made, the gospel preached, missionaries supported (Philippians 4:18)
  • Above every other reason, let my life, my words, and my giving bring glory to You, my God and Father, forever and ever; make me a doer of the Word and not a hearer only (Philippians 4:20; James 1:22)
Declaration

I Am a Partner, Not a Donor

Lord, today I choose to be a partner with You, not a donor to Your work. I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be content, because You are with me and You will never leave me nor forsake me. I care about the state of Your work, the salvation of souls, and the making of disciples, and my caring will overflow into how I give my time, my talent, and my treasure. I will not wait to be asked. This is God's business, and I am a co-owner. Every seed I sow into Your kingdom is changed from earthly currency into heavenly currency, and it abounds to an account that will never close. I will not give my best to what death will take from me. Whether or not You make me rich on earth, make me rich in heaven. To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sermon Summary

Partnering With God - Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Central Message - Preached as the climax of Harvest's Great Commission month, the sermon reads Paul's thank-you note from a Roman prison (Philippians 4:10-20) as a theological treatise on partnership. Paul rejoiced greatly in chains, not because the Philippians' gift made him comfortable, but because of what their giving revealed about them (they cared) and what it would do for them (fruit abounding to their eternal account).

The Secret - Paul's joy was not tied to his circumstances. He had learned to be content, abased or abounding, because of the sufficiency of Christ and the certainty that the Lord would never leave him nor forsake him. “Thank God for the million dollars in the bank, but Jesus is better.”

The Corrective - God is not looking for donors. A donor does a favor and has no skin in the game; a partner owns the work and gives to himself. Relating to God's work as a donor is going about it wrong and cheats you of the benefits of partnership. “Don't donate to the Great Commission. Own it.”

The Motivation - Three reasons to be a faithful partner: you have a stake (your heavenly account grows), God will supply all your need, and above all, the glory of God. The sermon closes with a prayer: rich on earth perhaps, but above all, rich in heaven.

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

Principle - Contentment is learned, and it is anchored in the sufficiency and presence of Christ, not in circumstances.

Principle - Giving reveals the heart. God evaluates the care behind the gift, not the size of the gift.

Principle - Caring precedes sharing. Deep care always produces action; if the sharing is absent, the caring is the deficiency.

Principle - Partnership, not donation, is God's design for the mission. A partner owns the work and does not wait to be asked.

Principle - Generosity toward God's work is an eternal investment that profits the giver, not a favor done for God, the pastor, or the church.

Principle - God's supply is measured by His riches, not yours; that promise is the ground of fearless generosity.

Principle - The glory of God is the highest motivation; every other blessing is real but secondary.

Warning - Beware an emotional life tied to circumstances: happy in breakthrough, silent and unserving in disappointment.

Warning - Beware giving without caring: you can give a million dollars, even sell everything for the poor, and have not love (1 Corinthians 13:3).

Warning - Beware relating to God's work as a donor. You are going about it wrong and cheating yourself of the benefits of partnership.

Warning - Beware giving your best to what death will close. Earthly accounts vanish; don't neglect the eternal account that opens at death.

Warning - Beware stinginess born of fear. Plan wisely, but do not be so tight and mean because you doubt God will take care of you.

Summary 4 of 5

Takeaways & Declarations

1. Learn the secret - Pray, “Lord, teach me the secret of being able to rejoice regardless of my circumstances,” and practice saying “I can also say: the Lord is with me” alongside every hard fact.

2. Go tomorrow - Meet the person you are afraid of meeting, confidently, knowing the Lord is with you.

3. Ask the first question - Not “how much will I give?” but “do I care, and how much?” Pray “Lord, improve my caring,” and let the giving follow.

4. Own the mission - Stop relating to God's work as a donor. Partner with God first, then with your local church, in fulfilling the gospel here and around the world.

5. Ask about the state of the work - When did you last ask what is happening on the mission field, what the needs are, what you can pray for, give to, or support with your time and resources?

6. Seize an opportunity - Harvest offers many opportunities to invest in God's work. Determine which one you will seize and make the most of it. Don't wait to be asked.

7. Invest for eternity - Take care of earthly needs, but give your best to the account that opens at death, not the one that closes.

8. Give for God's glory - Let the glory of God, above the eternal account and the supply of needs, be your motivation for serving, using your gifts, and giving.

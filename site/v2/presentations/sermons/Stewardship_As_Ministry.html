36th Anniversary • Guest Ministry

Stewardship As Ministry

“Let a man so account of us, as of the ministers of Christ, and stewards of the mysteries of God”

Dr. Shawn Smith

1 Corinthians 4:1-2 • Harvest Intercontinental Church, Olney • August 24, 2026

The Text

"Let a man so account of us, as of the ministers of Christ, and stewards of the mysteries of God. Moreover it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful."

1 Corinthians 4:1-2 (KJV)

Two words carry the whole sermon: hypēretēs — the under-rower — and oikonomos — the steward of the house.

Every Church Has an Assignment

In Revelation, when the Lord Jesus spoke to the Apostle John, He addressed seven churches and sent specific instructions to each one. Particular congregations receive from the Lord Jesus Christ a particular assignment.

This House’s Assignment

“We have been able to see beyond a shadow of a doubt the assignment for global discipleship that this ministry has so valiantly carried on throughout the nations in these past 36 years. Longevity is a proof of God’s sustaining power — God has not only done great things, He has sustained the things He has done through you.”

The Fear of the Call

When we speak of ministry, many believers imagine they must forsake their secular career, stop everything they are doing, and be sent to some far-flung region of the world. And so they fear to consecrate themselves to the call.

The Correction

Ministry begins as stewardship. Not somewhere else, not someday — here, now, with what is already in your hand.

Part 1

The Under-Rower and the Steward

What Paul actually called himself

Hypēretēs — The Under-Rower

Roman naval vessels travelled at speed because they did not rely on wind alone — the hull was filled with oarsmen. In Ben-Hur, the hero’s whole existence for years was rowing in the belly of the ship.

On deck, a captain navigates. Under the boards, a whole company rows.

Ministry Is Mostly Unseen

“What ministry consists of, most people would never get to see. It’s what happens behind the scenes that makes ministry, as God intends, go forward. If you want to think anything about us — we’re just attendants to Christ. We are oarsmen for Christ.”

Oikonomos — The Steward of the House

A steward is an administrator of the affairs of another. In the Greco-Roman world, the master of a great estate would take his most trusted servant and appoint him oikonomos — and hand him the purse of the house.

The Job Description

To know what everyone in the household needed. To know the diet habits of his masters. To know what had to be replenished. To ensure nothing was lacking in the entire household — and where a domain had yielded, to increase it further for the advancement of the house.

Pause and think of the responsibility — and of the entrustment.

Money Is the Least of Riches

When we hear “stewardship,” our minds go straight to money — “which is probably why you tensed up a little bit.”

But Jesus said that on the scale of God’s riches, mammon is the least. Many think faithfulness with finances is the epitome of the walk with God. It is the entry point — because most of the time money is simply what keeps people in survival mode.

"If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?"

Luke 16:11 (KJV)

You Will Never Be Given More Time

“Most people do not find the time to serve, not because they need more time. Everyone gets 24 hours a day. There is no scenario where any one of us is going to have more time.”

The Real Question

Not how do I get more? but how do I steward what I have been given, and prioritize what God has entrusted to me?

“It is written, ‘In God we trust’ — and the question is, which god are you talking about?”

The Requirement

Faithfulness Is Embodied

Faith is professed

It is spoken, confessed, declared.

Faithfulness is embodied

It is lived — and it is called faithfulness only because it has been tested.

“A minister is made.”

Part 2

Identity Leads to Vocation

The indicative and the imperative

Indicative → Imperative

The Indicative — who we are in Christ

“Now are we the sons of God.” “He who knew no sin became sin, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.” “Blessed with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ Jesus.”

The Imperative — what we are therefore to do

“Walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called.” (Ephesians 4:1)

One leads to the other. Identity leads to vocation.

Take Stock of Your Portfolio

When you awaken to sonship, it leads to vocation — where you take stock of who you are, what you have, and where you are situated — geographically, in time and space.

Where Are You Now?

There is a reason for that. And once you have taken stock of your stewardship portfolio, you begin to ask the only question left: what am I to do?

The Two Questions on the Damascus Road

1. “Who are You, Lord?”

You will be asking this your entire life — because the more you behold Him, the more He unfolds who He is. And when He reveals who He is, you see a reflection of who He has called you to be.

2. “What wilt Thou have me to do?”

These two questions summarize the entirety of the Christian life — and both unfold sequentially, over a lifetime.

Acts 9:5-6 • Paul had rabbinic training under Gamaliel and a fixed profile of the Messiah — and it was that profile that made him a persecutor.

“But Who Do You Say That I Am?”

"He saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am? And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God."

Matthew 16:15-16 (KJV)

They had been with Him two and a half years. To “who do men say” they had many answers — to “who do you say” they were all quiet. We can carry the narratives we heard from others without the knowledge of Christ ourselves.

Living Stones

In the reflection of Peter’s confession came his name: Thou art Cephas — Petros. And upon this rock of revelation, “I will build My church.”

A living stone is a stone that is not cut — untouched, unshaped. The Celtic church built cathedrals in Scotland and Ireland from living stones: they simply set them together, and they fit.

The Architect Knows

Within the body of Christ, God knows where to fit you — with your particularities and your peculiarities. You do not have to be cut into someone else’s shape to belong in the building.

God Cannot Be Discovered

"Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-jona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven."

Matthew 16:17 (KJV)

“Revelation is not a function of the human mind. As important as academics are, as important as study is — except God reveals Himself to you, you will not know Him. God cannot be discovered. God reveals Himself. And once you have beheld the revelation of Christ, and it awakens within your heart — from that, ministry is birthed.”

The Mystery

He Ascended Without Leaving a Budget

“Can you imagine conferring a global commission, transgenerationally, that will last for thousands of years — and not leaving a cent? How do you know that? Well, the treasurer was dead. There was no treasurer — they had to sort that out during the ten days of prayer before Pentecost.”

No budget. No building. Could it be He knows what we don’t?

The Deposit Is Already In You

“If you knew what God had already deposited within you, you would learn to trust the process.”

On a Need-to-Know Basis

"Arise, and go into the city, and it shall be told thee what thou must do."

Acts 9:6 (KJV)

“It’s almost like Mission: Impossible — your mission, should you choose to accept it… and we will remain in contact with you on a need-to-know basis. God keeps you updated on a need-to-know basis.”

The Dilemma of Silence

The silence can mean go on — you are doing what I asked. Or it can mean why would I add a new detail, when you have not done what I asked last time? Promotion in God is that He gives us another opportunity to be of service.

Shake Off the Dust

You cannot spend your entire Christian life saying “I know who I am.” It is wonderful to know who you are — but that security, that sense of God’s acceptance, is given to equip you. You will preach to people who try to cast you out, and you had better know who you are when it happens.

What It Means

“Don’t carry that rejection with you. Don’t let it define what you think of yourself. Maybe I shouldn’t have said that. Maybe I didn’t do this right. No — shake it off and keep on walking.” (Matthew 10:14)

Part 3

Your Stance and Your State

Why circumstances are called circumstances

Stance and State

Your Stance — immutable

What is true of you in Christ and cannot change. You are in Him by His initiative. Grace cannot be manufactured by any human. The Giver Himself is the gift — and what He has given, He sustains. Your sonship is unchanging. Eternal life is eternal.

Your State — changing

Your day-to-day natural life. Some days you feel down. Some days you are discouraged. Some days someone says “you are the righteousness of God” and you answer, “I’ve heard that before.”

Your State Is the Field of Your Mission

“That is the reason why circumstances are called circum-stances — they are the details that surround the stance, which never changes.”

Grocery prices, rumors of layoffs, jobs about to be lost — “did you know God is aware of all of that?” In the midst of all the changing occurrences in your state, your stance remains irrevocable.

The Consequence

“If I am aware of my stance within the circumstance, I can minister without being affected by the outward changes.”

A Prosperous Journey

The word Paul prays in Romans 1:10 — euodoō, “prosperous journey” — in Koine Greek refers to a journey where you arrive at your destination. It is a picture of the whole Christian vocation.

"Sirs, I perceive that this voyage will be with hurt and much damage, not only of the lading and ship, but also of our lives."

Acts 27:10 (KJV)

He was not in the position to decide. They sailed. Fourteen days without sun or stars. Then the angel: not one life lost — but the ship shall be lost. He had prayed for a prosperous journey; the prosperous journey cost him the ship and the cargo.

The Economy of God

Losing to Gain

“There is nothing that you have ever lost for the sake of Christ and of His kingdom that God will not cause you to regain exceedingly, abundantly beyond what you can ask or think.”

“The heart is the greatest asset in the economy of God. And God will not spare anything to train you, so that your heart has the right attitude. From our perspective it is loss — look at all the things I’m losing. From His perspective, His training is losing to gain.”

Malta: The Heart of the Man

Paul swims ashore on Malta. It is winter, everyone is shivering — and he goes out to gather wood so the others can be warmed. He is in the same condition as everyone else, and he is thinking of others beyond himself.

That Is What Leadership Is

“There is no sense of entitlement when it comes to leading in this upside-down kingdom. It is found when Jesus, the Master and Lord of all, comes washing feet. He was calling Himself the least.”

He Shook It Off Into the Fire

A viper springs from the fire and fastens on his hand. He does not panic. He calmly shakes it off. When things try to stick on you and inject venom while you are on your prosperous journey, it is to derail you from the journey. He knew the One who sent him.

Don’t Live by the Polls

First they said, “he escaped the sea, but justice has caught him.” Minutes later: “he is a god.” If you build your understanding of yourself on what people think of you and how they react to you, you are going to be in a very confused place.

The Chief’s Son and the Better Ship

They carry him to the chief of the island, whose son is dying of dysentery — life-threatening, because when you drink from a poisoned source it threatens your life. “I’m speaking at many different levels. There is danger in mixture. You need to know where your source is and where you feed from — because there might be death in the pot.”

Paul lays hands on the son; he is healed; the whole island brings its sick. And the chieftain says: we will build you another ship to go back to Rome.

The Prisoners’ Ship Never Arrived

In Rome, his detractors waited for the ship designated for prisoners. It never came — because God was giving Paul a prosperous journey. There may be snakes. There may be storms. There may be false accusations. There will be miracles. But you will arrive.

Part 4

God Relies Upon Us

The mystery hidden inside stewardship

The Miracle of Participation

Jesus could have manifested wine in all six waterpots at once. Instead the first miracle was a miracle of participation: “Fill the waterpots with water” — and they filled them to the brim.

There was no running water 2,000 years ago. They had to go to the stream, to the well, and carry it. “I’m sure many of them were grumbling.”

Some Things He Will Not Do Alone

“It will require your participation. It will require you doing things beyond your comfort. It is called sacrifice — sacrificial love. And when the miracle happened, the servants were the first to know: we were taking part in a miracle.” (John 2:1-11)

The Frightening Verse

"Who now rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up that which is behind of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh for his body's sake, which is the church."

Colossians 1:24 (KJV)

Is something lacking in Christ’s afflictions? Paul says there is — and that is the scary part. By what audacity does he say this?

The Answer

What Is Lacking Is Our Understanding of It

All that Christ has done on behalf of humanity — for humanity to benefit from it, they have to hear it and they have to understand it. Which means someone must take up the charge of making Christ known — of which suffering is unavoidable, and loving sacrifice for this message is unavoidable.

“But isn’t it wonderful to spend and be spent for this glorious gospel?”

When we look at the 10/40 Window, as you have been praying this morning — something is lacking in the afflictions of Christ, and someone must fill up what is lacking. That is where the son awakened to his vocation steps in.

Make My Purpose Prosper

Stewardship means God has given you charge of His belongings — remember the oikonomos. And He says: you now have to seek what I want in this situation.

  • Make My purpose prosper in your field
  • Make My purpose prosper in your sphere of influence
  • Make My purpose prosper wherever I have placed you

Jesus Never Called the Idle

“Did you know that Jesus never called someone who was unemployed to ministry? He never came to someone who was just idling around and said, follow Me and be My disciple. Everyone whom Jesus chose was already actively involved in a particular sphere.”

Jesus called fishermen and businessmen. He called successful entrepreneurs. He called a tax collector — an agent of the IRS. “You know how scandalous that is today.”

First Steps

“There are so many young men who want to reach the world for Christ, but have never given a sacrificial offering in church for those who are already reaching the world for Christ. Awakening to stewardship is taking the first steps in your vocation — because stewardship is ministry.”

It Must Reach All Worlds

“For this message to reach the world, it must reach all worlds — and He has already positioned you in your world. Yes, we need the gospel to go into every nation under heaven, even into the 10/40 Window. But there are people in your world that are yet unreached, whereas you are there among them.”

The Question to Take Home

“Why has God placed me where I am?” You are to answer that question in communion and conversation with the heavenly Father.

The Charge

Everything Given to You Is an Entrustment

Go and look at what He has entrusted to you — your possessions, your person, your influence, your relationships, your opportunities — and allow the sense of identity you have received in Christ to lead you into an awareness of vocation.

“Everything given to you is a gift of entrustment for which you will have to render account.”

Grace makes us gracious. Grace begins as God’s initiative — but it awakens our response.

Prayer Points

  • Lord, deliver me from the fear of consecration — show me that ministry begins as stewardship, right where You have already placed me (1 Corinthians 4:1-2)
  • Make me content to be an under-rower — faithful in what no one sees, because it is the unseen rowing that moves the ship (1 Corinthians 4:1)
  • Give me the heart of the steward: to know what the household needs, to let nothing be lacking, and to increase what You have entrusted for the advancement of Your house (Luke 16:10-11)
  • Free me from survival mode — I refuse to let money be the ceiling of my obedience when You have called it the least of riches (Luke 16:11)
  • Teach me to steward the 24 hours You have given rather than wait for hours You will never give (Ephesians 5:16)
  • Let my faith not merely be professed but embodied — and where You must test it to make it faithfulness, give me grace to stand (1 Corinthians 4:2)

Prayer Points

  • Let my identity lead me into vocation — I will not stop at knowing who I am; I will walk worthy of the calling (Ephesians 4:1)
  • Reveal Yourself to me, Lord — not through the narratives of others, but by the Father who alone unveils the Son (Matthew 16:17)
  • Fit me as a living stone exactly where You know I belong, with all my particularities and peculiarities (1 Peter 2:5)
  • Help me trust the process — You left no budget because You had already deposited what the commission requires inside Your people (2 Corinthians 4:7)
  • When You are silent, keep me obeying the last thing You said, and count every new assignment as promotion (Acts 9:6)
  • Anchor my stance so firmly that circumstances become the field of my mission, not the measure of my life (Colossians 3:3)
  • Grant me a prosperous journey — through storms, snakes and false accusations — until I arrive where You sent me (Romans 1:10; Acts 28:1-10)
  • Show me the unreached people in my own world, and make Your purpose prosper wherever You have placed me (Colossians 1:24-25)
Declaration

I Am a Steward of the Mysteries of God

Father, I am an under-rower for Christ and a steward of the mysteries of God. I stop waiting for a call that looks like someone else’s. I take stock of my portfolio — my possessions, my person, my influence, my relationships, my opportunities — and I present all of it to You as entrustment. My stance in Christ is immutable; my circumstances are simply the field of my mission. I will be faithful where I am, behind the scenes, without applause. I will shake off rejection and keep walking. I will fill up what is lacking in the understanding of Christ’s afflictions among the people of my world. Make Your purpose prosper wherever You have placed me — for everything given to me is a gift of entrustment for which I will render account. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

Stewardship As Ministry — Dr. Shawn Smith

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Occasion — Preached at Harvest Intercontinental Church, Olney, during the church's 36th anniversary month by Dr. Shawn Smith of Cameroon, who came with his wife Dr. Annie Smith and their six children. He opens by honoring Bishop Darlingston Johnson and Lady Chrys, and by naming what he sees in the house: an assignment of global discipleship, "worthy of being studied and emulated."

The Problem Addressed — Believers fear consecration because they imagine the call means abandoning their career and being shipped to a far-flung region. That fear keeps them from ever saying yes.

The Central Message — Ministry begins as stewardship. From 1 Corinthians 4:1-2, Paul calls himself two things: an hypēretēs (under-rower, hidden in the hull) and an oikonomos (steward given the purse of the house). What ministry consists of is mostly unseen, and it starts with what has already been entrusted to you — your possessions, person, influence, relationships and opportunities.

The Movement — Identity leads to vocation (indicative to imperative). Revelation of Christ births ministry, ministry is directed on a need-to-know basis, and it is carried out in a state full of change while resting on a stance that never changes. The journey may cost a ship and still be a prosperous journey.

The Closing Charge — Something is lacking in the world's understanding of Christ's afflictions, and someone must fill it up. Jesus never called an idle person; He calls people already active in a sphere. "Everything given to you is a gift of entrustment for which you will have to render account."

Summary 1 of 5

Key Scriptures

1 Corinthians 4:1-2 — "Let a man so account of us, as of the ministers of Christ, and stewards of the mysteries of God. Moreover it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful." The text of the whole sermon: two Greek words, hypēretēs and oikonomos, and one requirement, faithfulness.

Revelation 2-3 — The Lord sends specific instruction to each of seven churches: particular congregations receive particular assignments. Used to name this church's assignment of global discipleship.

Luke 16:11 — Mammon is the least of riches in God's economy; faithfulness with money is the entry point, not the summit.

Ephesians 4:1 — "Walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called" — the imperative that flows from the indicative of sonship.

Acts 9:5-6 — "Who art thou, Lord?" and "what wilt thou have me to do?" — the two questions that summarize the Christian life; answered in Damascus on a need-to-know basis.

Matthew 16:15-18 — "But whom say ye that I am?" Peter's confession, the naming of Cephas, and the church built of living stones on the rock of revelation.

Matthew 10:14 — Shake off the dust: do not carry rejection with you, and do not let it define what you think of yourself.

Romans 1:10; Acts 27-28 — Paul's prayer for a prosperous journey (euodoō — a journey where you arrive), fulfilled through shipwreck, viper, and a better ship built by a grateful chieftain.

Colossians 1:23-24 — "Fill up that which is behind of the afflictions of Christ" — the frightening verse that closes the message, and the charge to make His purpose prosper where you are placed.

Summary 2 of 5

Main Teachings

1. Ministry begins as stewardship — not with relocation or resignation, but with the faithful administration of what God has already put in your hand.

2. The minister is an under-rower — hypēretēs, the oarsman below deck. The captain is seen; the rowers are not. "What ministry consists of, most people would never get to see."

3. The minister is a steward — oikonomos, the trusted servant handed the purse of the house, responsible that nothing in the household lacks, and required to increase what yields.

4. Faithfulness is embodied, not professed — faith is spoken; faithfulness is lived, and it earns its name only by being tested. "A minister is made."

5. Identity leads to vocation — the indicative (who you are in Christ) exists to produce the imperative (walk worthy). Security in God's acceptance is given to equip you for rejection, not to insulate you from it.

6. Revelation births ministry — God cannot be discovered; He reveals Himself. And when He reveals who He is, you see a reflection of who He has called you to be.

7. God guides on a need-to-know basis — "Go into the city, and it shall be told thee." His silence either confirms your obedience or waits on it. Promotion in God is another opportunity to serve.

8. Stance vs. state — your stance in Christ is immutable; your state is your changing day-to-day life, and it is the field of your mission. Aware of your stance within the circumstance, you can minister unaffected by outward change.

9. Losing to gain — the heart is the greatest asset in God's economy, and He will not spare anything to train it. Nothing lost for Christ goes unrestored.

10. God relies upon us — He has sovereignly chosen to advance His purposes through participation: fill the waterpots, and the servants share the miracle.

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

Longevity is a proof of God's sustaining power — He has not only done great things, He has sustained the things He has done.

The Giver Himself is the gift — grace cannot be manufactured by any human, and what He has given, He sustains. Your sonship is immutable.

Circumstances surround the stance — that is why they are called circum-stances. They change; the stance does not.

There is no entitlement in this upside-down kingdom — Paul gathers firewood for shivering strangers; Jesus, Master of all, washes feet and calls Himself the least.

Warning — the survival-mode ceiling: money is the least of riches, yet it is precisely what keeps most people from ever serving. Do not mistake financial faithfulness for the summit of discipleship.

Warning — waiting for more time: everyone gets 24 hours. There is no future scenario with more. The issue is stewardship and priority, not scarcity.

Warning — living by the polls: on Malta they called Paul a murderer, then a god, within minutes. Build your self-understanding on what people think and you will live in a very confused place.

Warning — danger in mixture: the chief's son was dying of dysentery, and "when you drink from a poisoned source it threatens your life." Know your source and where you feed from — there may be death in the pot.

Warning — carrying rejection: shake off the dust. Do not let the region that refused the gospel define what you think of yourself.

Warning — wanting to reach the world while supporting no one: many young men long to reach nations but have never given a sacrificial offering toward those already going.

Summary 4 of 5

Practical Takeaways

Take stock of your portfolio — write down what He has entrusted to you: your possessions, your person, your influence, your relationships, your opportunities. That inventory is your ministry brief.

Answer the question at home — "Why has God placed me where I am?" is to be answered in communion and conversation with the Father, not in a seminar.

Prioritize rather than wait — stop asking for time you will never receive; steward the 24 hours you have.

Serve where you cannot be seen — accept assignments below deck, where the rowing happens and nobody applauds.

Give sacrificially toward those already sent — support the ones reaching the world before claiming you want to reach it.

Obey the last instruction during the silence — do not wait for new detail while the previous word is unfinished.

Shake it off and keep walking — treat rejection as dust on the sandal, not as a verdict on your identity.

Find the unreached in your own world — the 10/40 Window matters, and so does the world you are already standing in.

Live ready to render account — everything given to you is entrustment, and entrustment is audited.

Prophetic word over the house: "There are so many more of the sons and daughters in this house that You are calling to participate in faithful stewardship for the next 36 years" — and to those living in survival mode, in fear of what is to come, in the red: "He's calling you today to awaken to vocation. He gave you something to reach your world for the gospel."

Prayer Points

Praying the mindset of a champion into your life

Prayer Points (1 of 2)

  • Off cruise control. Thank God that salvation is the beginning and ask for grace to keep pressing into the process of transformation. (Romans 8:29)
  • A clear vision. Ask God to make the vision of knowing Christ crystal clear, so you run not with uncertainty. (1 Corinthians 9:26)
  • Discipline of the body. Pray for strength to bring your body, eye gate, ear gate, and speech under your spirit. (1 Corinthians 9:27)
  • Discernment. Ask the Holy Spirit to guard you from legalistic and mixed teaching. (Galatians 3:1, Revelation 3:15-16)

Prayer Points (2 of 2)

  • A life of prayer. Pray for the discipline to go beyond rushed minutes and to study the Word rather than scan it. (2 Timothy 2:15)
  • Perseverance. Ask for strength to keep pressing when the journey gets difficult, and not to quit.
  • Surrender to the Coach. Invite the Holy Spirit to work within you and correct you gently when you veer off course. (John 14:16)
  • A strong finish. Pray to one day say with Paul, "I have fought the good fight," and receive an imperishable crown. (2 Timothy 4:7-8)

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

The Mindset of a Champion - Pastor Chrys Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

Pastor Chrys Johnson preaches that salvation is not a finish line but the start of a lifelong transformation, and he confronts believers who have grown complacent, coasting through their spiritual lives "on cruise control." Using the image of the believer as an athlete from 1 Corinthians 9:24-27, he draws out five observations about championship athletes and applies each one to the believer's pursuit of being conformed to the image of Christ.

Context - Preached at the second service at Harvest Intercontinental Church, Olney, and closing with an altar call reminding listeners that no one is born saved.

Central claim - "God did not call us to be comfortable. He called us to take us through a process, a growth process."

Summary 1 of 9

Key Scriptures (1)

1 Corinthians 9:24-27 - "Run in such a way that you may obtain it... Now they do it to obtain a perishable crown, but we for an imperishable crown... But I discipline my body and bring it into subjection." The principal text. The whole athlete parallel is built here, and verse 27 returns under discipline and sacrifice.

Genesis 1:26-27 - "Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness." Establishes that man was made to bear God's image, that sin marred it, and that God refused to leave humanity there.

Romans 8:29 - "For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son." The believer's divine vision, already given, not invented.

Philippians 2:13 - "For it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure." Your desire matters, but the Holy Spirit does the work.

Matthew 27:51 - The veil torn from top to bottom. Cited in passing for the cost of our access: "God had to tear it. Man could not do it."

Summary 2 of 9

Key Scriptures (2)

Galatians 3:1 - "O foolish Galatians! Who has bewitched you?" Against teaching that pulls the delivered believer back under law.

Revelation 3:15-16 - "Because you are lukewarm... I will vomit you out of My mouth." The mixed spiritual diet is more dangerous than being clearly cold.

1 John 4:4 - "He who is in you is greater than he who is in the world." Against fear and superstition.

2 Timothy 2:15 - "Be diligent to present yourself approved to God." A call to real study, not a scan.

John 14:16 - "He will give you another Helper." Introduces the Holy Spirit as the Paraclete, the believer's coach.

Jeremiah 1:5 - "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you." The Coach knew you before you existed.

1 John 3:2 - "When He is revealed, we shall be like Him." The outcome of the vision.

2 Timothy 4:7-8 - "I have fought the good fight... there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness." The finish line.

Isaiah 64:6 - "All our righteousnesses are like filthy rags." In the altar call: you have nothing to give Christ but your brokenness.

Summary 3 of 9

Main Teachings (1)

1. Vision - A champion begins with a clear vision of medals, money, or name recognition, and it drives every choice. Serena Williams can "show up and make a million dollars because of name recognition," and "no wonder these people don't sleep." But Paul says they do it for a perishable crown. The believer also needs a vision and does not have to invent one: God has given it, to know Jesus and be conformed to His image. It must be crystal clear, embraced, and never put on the back burner, so that like Paul we are "not boxing the air."

2. Discipline - "Without discipline, that desire will only remain a desire. It's going nowhere." The natural athlete watches the diet and gets sleep. The believer-athlete watches a spiritual diet: the eye gate, the ear gate, the mouth, and what is believed. Guard against teaching that reintroduces legalism after grace, and against a lukewarm mixture of law and grace. Bishop Benson Idahosa's buffet story is the picture: piling the plate high until the voice of the Lord says, "If you eat all of this, you're going to die."

Summary 4 of 9

Main Teachings (2)

3. Sacrifice - Athletes give up time, fellowship, social activity, and social media to stay focused. Believers sacrifice time for prayer and the Word. "You cannot live on 15 minutes of prayer. You have to cultivate a life of prayer." Prayer is a place of isolation, just you and God doing business, so leave the phone behind. And study the Word, do not scan it.

4. Perseverance - The athlete presses through grueling training because the goal is bigger than the discomfort. The believer perseveres "when the journey gets difficult, when all hell breaks loose," refusing self-pity and distraction, pressing on come hell or high water.

5. A Coach - "The athlete cannot see what a coach sees." Potential is locked up until a coach draws it out. Human coaches are well paid but limited to one sport and only ever external. The believer's coach is the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, who is unlimited, who knew you before you were formed in the womb, and who works on the inside, bringing you back gently when you veer. "Yield to the Holy Spirit. He knows what He's doing."

Summary 5 of 9

Principles

Salvation is not the finish line. Salvation is just the beginning.

God did not call us to be comfortable. He called us to take us through a process, a growth process.

Without discipline, desire stays a desire. It is going nowhere if you do not discipline yourself.

Your spirit rules your body. God did not design it the other way around.

Lukewarm is the most dangerous. Mixture is worse than cold.

God did not make this thing hard. He made it easy, and it is by way of Jesus.

Agreement moves heaven. "Every time we come into agreement with God, things happen in the spirit."

The coach sees what the athlete cannot. That is why you need one.

When you know Jesus, you will find yourself. Because you are in Him and He is in you.

Summary 6 of 9

Warnings

Spiritual complacency - Too many believers put their spiritual lives on cruise control and coast, assuming salvation means they have arrived.

An unguarded eye gate and ear gate - What you watch, including pornography, and what you listen to shapes your spirit. Not everything carrying the name of Jesus is sound teaching.

False doctrine and legalism - The example given was a teaching that only river baptism counts. Such teaching puts believers "right back under the law."

Lukewarmness and mixture - A faith that blends law and grace is more dangerous than being clearly cold or clearly hot.

Uncontrolled spiritual buffets - Piling up every teaching without discernment will harm you.

Fear and superstition - Losing a night of sleep over an owl. "It's just a bird." Greater is He who is in you.

Distracted prayer - Do not bring a phone, or any distraction, into your set-apart time with God.

Summary 7 of 9

Practical Takeaways

Get off cruise control - Actively pursue growth rather than coasting on a past salvation experience.

Set the vision daily - Keep being conformed to the image of Christ crystal clear in front of you.

Guard the eye gate - Be deliberate about what you allow yourself to watch, and seek help if you need it.

Guard the ear gate - Test teaching against the Word rather than accepting it because it uses the name of Jesus.

Build a real prayer life - Go beyond a token 15 minutes and remove distractions from that time.

Study, do not scan - Consistent study of Scripture, in line with 2 Timothy 2:15.

Sacrifice deliberately - Time, social media, and resources, to stay focused on growth.

Persevere - Press through hardship instead of quitting or indulging self-pity.

Yield to the Coach - Invite the Holy Spirit's correction rather than resisting it.

Summary 8 of 9

Words Declared

Over the congregation, in the opening prayer - "Someone under the sound of my voice will be encouraged by this word, someone will be strengthened, someone will get up from where they are and continue to advance in the mighty name of Jesus."

Over every pending request - "Whatever you're dealing with and you are praying about, know that God has already done it. And all we have to do is to align our words with His words."

The closing benediction - "May God give all of us the grace to finish, and I like to always add, to finish strong, in the name of Jesus."

Over everyone who prayed the altar call - "I want to be the first to announce to you, you are born again. Welcome to the family of God."

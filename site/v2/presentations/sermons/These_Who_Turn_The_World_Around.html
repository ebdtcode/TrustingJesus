Anniversary Month • Our Reason for Being

These Who Turn The World Around

“These who have turned the world upside down have come here also”

Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Acts 17:1-6 • August 2026

Paul's Custom

"Now when they had passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews. Then Paul, as his custom was, went in to them, and for three Sabbaths reasoned with them from the Scriptures, explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus whom I preach to you is the Christ.'"

Acts 17:1-3 (NKJV)

It was Paul's custom — his practice, his habit — to share the gospel with people who did not know it, and to preach Jesus to those who did not know Him.

Some Were Persuaded

"And some of them were persuaded; and a great multitude of the devout Greeks, and not a few of the leading women, joined Paul and Silas."

Acts 17:4 (NKJV)

Not Everyone Responds the Same

Some of the Jews were persuaded — not all. The devout Greeks converted in far greater numbers: far more open, far more responsive. Not everybody you share Christ with will have the same openness. Some will reject it. But there is a great multitude waiting to hear someone preach Jesus to them — and that's why we're here.

“Not a Few of the Leading Women”

The Holy Ghost was impressed by the response of the women — He could simply have said “a great multitude,” but He specifically noted the leading women: women of influence.

God Uses Women Powerfully

God puts women in positions of leadership where they influence not just other women, but society. Women, we honor you because the Holy Ghost honors you. You're leading in your homes, your families, your workplaces, your communities. Use that influence for Christ.

The Opposition

"But the Jews who were not persuaded, becoming envious, took some of the evil men from the marketplace, and gathering a mob, set all the city in an uproar and attacked the house of Jason."

Acts 17:5 (NKJV)

Some will not just decline the gospel — they will actively oppose it. Doing what God has called you to do will make some enemies, because the gospel challenges people and their idols. And sometimes, like Jason, you pay a price not for what you did, but for what God is doing in His kingdom.

The Reputation

The Key Verse

"These who have turned the world upside down have come here too."

Acts 17:6 (NKJV)

Really Right Side Up

May that be our reputation! The people saying this were unbelievers — and the unbelieving world is already upside down. When you are living upside down, right side up looks like upside down to you. May our presence and influence be so powerful that everywhere we go, we turn people, systems, and cultures around.

Part 1

Not a Spa — a Military Base

Recovering the biblical picture of the church

The Church Is Not a Spa

God never intended the church to be a spiritual spa or a retreat center — a community where we come to relax, socialize, and focus on ourselves and our needs.

The Consumer Trap

Many people come to a church, choose a church, and stay in a church based on how much that church caters to me — how it makes me feel, how it attends to my needs. We do have needs, and the church does minister to them. But the church is not your personal retreat center.

A Spiritual Military Base

The far better metaphor from Jesus and the apostles — we gather to be:

1. Trained

In the art of war — spiritual war.

2. Equipped

With weapons for our warfare.

3. Strengthened

To endure the toughness of war.

4. Deployed

To the battlefield, to engage in the mission of the kingdom.

The Mission Is Clear

Wars fail when the mission is unclear — that was the complaint about Iraq. If you're going to war, the mission must be clear. The church is deployed to fight a war — or better, to enforce a victory — and our Commander, the Lord Jesus Christ, has made the mission very clear:

"Go into all the world and preach the gospel to every creature… make disciples of all nations… teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you."

Mark 16:15; Matthew 28:19-20 (NKJV)

Don't stay where you are. Go — everywhere your feet can take you: Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, the uttermost parts of the earth.

Jesus Speaks the Language of War

"The gates of hell shall not prevail" (Matthew 16:18)

Military language: the church meets the forces of darkness head-on — and they will not prevail against us.

"Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done on earth" (Matthew 6:10)

The language of invasion: go into what the enemy claims illegally, and enforce the King's will there.

"Put on the whole armor of God" (Ephesians 6:11)

Paul is talking about war — stand against the wiles of the devil.

Life on the Base

Every provision on the base exists for the battle — not instead of it:

The Hospital

Soldiers get wounded, and the wounded must be ministered to — but healing is for recovery, so you can return to battle. Don't let the enemy keep you forever in a hospital bed. Pray: “Heal me quickly. Restore me quickly. I'm ready to go.”

The Mess Hall

You are fed spiritual food to be strong for battle — not just to eat. If all you do is eat and eat, the food that should strengthen you makes you a heavier burden.

Recreation

Notice the word: re-create. Its purpose is to refresh and strengthen you — so you can go back and wage war.

Enforcing the Victory

The early church saw themselves as soldiers — not fighting to win the victory, but to enforce the victory Christ already won. Two ways:

1. Prayer — exercising the authority Jesus has given us.

2. Proclaiming Jesus — one of the most powerful weapons God has given us. In every city where they turned the world upside down, they prayed and they preached Jesus.

"For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for it is the power of God to salvation for everyone who believes… And how shall they hear without a preacher?"

Romans 1:16; 10:14 (NKJV)

Light and Salt

"You are the salt of the earth… You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden."

Matthew 5:13-14 (NKJV)

Presence That Changes the Environment

Everywhere light shows up, it changes the environment — exposing and challenging the works of darkness. That's warfare. Everywhere you place salt, it changes what it touches. We are called to change the world — and it takes prayer and the proclamation of the gospel.

All the Preachers, Stand Up

Preaching is not about being behind a pulpit. Every time you open your mouth to tell people about Jesus, you are preaching.

Say It

“I'm a preacher — and I need to start preaching.” You preach the gospel. You proclaim Jesus. You tell people who Jesus is. And that word you are preaching has the power of God to save, change, and transform the world.

Part 2

Who Are These?

Ordinary people, set on fire by being with Jesus

Ordinary People

Who are the “these” who changed the world? Fishermen. Tax collectors. Small business people. The great majority never stepped through the doors of a seminary. Ordinary people — just like you: teacher, nurse, custodian, chef, waiter, bus driver, carpenter.

"Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were uneducated and untrained men, they marveled. And they realized that they had been with Jesus."

Acts 4:13 (NKJV)

Because we are ordinary people, we qualify. They were the “these” of their generation. We are the “these” today.

Deliver Me From the Desire to Be Liked

Never be satisfied with being present and having no impact. Some will be drawn to you because of Jesus; some will hate you because of Jesus — but there has to be some reaction. The desire to be liked can grow so strong that we're unwilling to trouble anybody with the gospel.

Jesus said, “I did not come to bring peace but a sword” (Matthew 10:34). Not that we go looking for trouble — but the gospel calls people from idols to Christ, from self to a Savior. Its very nature is confrontational.

"For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind."

2 Timothy 1:7 (NKJV)

The Match

A match is very small — it hardly weighs anything. But set that match on fire, and it can burn down an entire house. An entire forest.

The Only Question

The question is not whether you are educated, naturally gifted, or rich. The question is: are you on fire? What distinguished these ordinary people from everyone else was that they had been with Jesus — and the presence of Jesus is what sets us on fire.

“These are they who set themselves on fire by being with Jesus.”

No Distance Between You and Him

But Bishop, Jesus isn't here physically! — Jesus said it is actually better for us that He went, because He sent the Holy Spirit: “I'm not just going to be with you, outside of you — I'm going to be inside of you” (John 16:7; 14:17).

The disciples couldn't be with Him all the time. We can — which means we can be on fire all the time, changing and impacting the world all the time.

"You are of God, little children, and have overcome them, because He who is in you is greater than he who is in the world."

1 John 4:4 (NKJV)

How to Stay On Fire

Begin your day with Him

Start alone: set your mind on Jesus. Praise Him, read His Word, and acknowledge out loud — “Lord, You are in me today. You are living through me today.”

Keep talking to Him all day

Short prayers, short praise. At work: “Jesus, You're in me now — loving this person through me, leading me to a solution.”

When distracted, just come back

Don't get angry with yourself. Open your mouth: “Jesus, I'm in Your presence. I may not feel You now, but You are here.” Feelings change; the fire remains.

The Unbelievers Named Them

Who said “these who have turned the world upside down”? Not the disciples — unbelievers said it. And the disciples didn't call themselves Christians first; it was unbelievers at Antioch who observed them and called them Christians (Acts 11:26).

When You're On Fire, You Don't Have to Announce It

You're so wrapped up, tied up, entangled up with Jesus that the unbeliever looks at you and says: “You're not one of us. You're different. You talk Jesus. Your mission is Jesus. Your ambition is Jesus.” By the way you live and talk and conduct yourself, it's evident — you are a Christian.

Part 3

“Have Come Here Also”

They went — and why they went

They Went

How did they change the world? They went to the world. They did not wait for Thessalonica to come to them on Sunday morning. The transformation took place in the world — they entered the cities, the towns, the homes, the lives of people, and proclaimed Jesus.

We cannot change the world while insisting we do so inside the church. On Sunday we gather to be trained, equipped, strengthened, and deployed — then we go.

Always Another

There's always another person. Another family. Another city. Another time. Make the most of every opportunity to preach, proclaim, publicize the good news — to trouble people with the news that God loves them, that Jesus died for them, that they can spend eternity with God.

Why Did They Go?

1. They believed

The gospel matters. Hell is real. Jesus is the only way. Unless people hear, they cannot be saved. They believed enough that they could not sit still.

2. They cared for people

“I've got to tell them — I've got the solution, the way of escape.” Too many of us are comfortable not sharing, as if we don't care whether they hear.

3. They obeyed

They did not own themselves — He owned them. If for no other reason: preach the gospel and make disciples because Jesus commanded it.

Raised Up Here, Deployed From Here

The pastors whose testimony we saw got saved in this church. The Lord used this church to bring them to Christ, train them in the art of war, equip them with the weapons they are using right now on the battlefront — and deploy them.

For months this church supported them financially to make that transition. You did it — you gave, you prayed, you made that testimony possible. The war you waged is now having impact in Ivory Coast and beyond — one story of many from this congregation.

The Charge

Back to the Battlefield

Yes, it's tough. Yes, war requires sacrifice. Yes, we can be wounded. But when we are wounded, let's get better as quickly as possible and get back on the battlefield.

Jesus came to turn us upside down — and having turned us upside down, to send us to turn the whole world right side up.

Stand to your feet. Take up your weapons. Go to the battlefront. Together, let's obey Jesus.

Prayer Points

  • Lord, may it be said of me, and of our church, that “these who have turned the world upside down have come here also” — make that our reputation (Acts 17:6)
  • Forgive me for treating the church as my spa and retreat center — restore to me the soldier's mindset: trained, equipped, strengthened, and deployed (2 Timothy 2:3-4)
  • Heal every wound I've received on the battlefield — heal me quickly, restore me quickly, so I can return to the fight and not stay in the hospital bed (Jeremiah 30:17)
  • Make me a preacher wherever I go — every time I open my mouth about Jesus, let Your power to save, change, and transform flow through the gospel (Romans 1:16)
  • Deliver me from the excessive desire to be liked — I refuse to let fear keep me off the battlefield, for You have given me power, love, and a sound mind (2 Timothy 1:7)

Prayer Points

  • Set me on fire, Lord — and keep me on fire — as I cultivate Your presence morning by morning and moment by moment (Acts 4:13)
  • Thank You that there is no distance between us: the Greater One lives inside of me, so I can commune with You all day, every day (1 John 4:4; John 14:17)
  • May my life be so wrapped up in Jesus that unbelievers conclude, without me saying a word, “You are different — you are a Christian” (Acts 11:26)
  • Give me the heart of those who went: believing the gospel matters, caring for the lost, and obeying simply because You commanded (Matthew 28:19-20)
  • Raise up world-turners from our congregation — saved, trained, equipped, and deployed to the nations — and let me have a part in sending them (Matthew 9:38)
Declaration

I Am One of the “These”

Lord, today I accept the call. I am a soldier, and there is a war being waged for the souls of men. I present myself — my time, my gifts, my substance — to You, for the war. I consecrate myself. I show up to be trained, equipped, and strengthened, because I am deployed on mission. I am one of the “these” today that God is using to turn my family, my work, my community, and my world around. God has not given me a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and a sound mind. I will set myself on fire by being with Jesus — and I will stay on fire. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Study Notes

Sermon Summary

These Who Turn The World Around — Bishop Darlingston Johnson

Key points, principles, and takeaways

Overview

The Central Message — Preached during the church's anniversary month as a reminder of “our reason for being,” the sermon takes the unbelievers' accusation in Acts 17:6 — “These who have turned the world upside down have come here too” — and turns it into a prayer: may that be our reputation as individuals and as a church.

The Corrective — God never intended the church to be a spiritual spa or retreat center chosen for how well it caters to me. The better biblical metaphor is a spiritual military base, where believers are trained in the art of war, equipped with weapons, strengthened for endurance, and deployed to the battlefield.

The Identity — The world-turners of Acts were ordinary, uneducated, common people whose one distinguishing mark was that “they had been with Jesus” (Acts 4:13). Because we are ordinary people, we qualify: “We are the 'these' today.”

The Method — They changed the world by going to it — praying, and proclaiming Jesus — because they believed the gospel matters, they cared for people, and they obeyed their Commander. The sermon ends in consecration: present yourself for the war.

Summary 1 of 5

Key Scriptures

Acts 17:1-6 — The anchor text: Paul's custom of preaching Jesus in Thessalonica; some Jews persuaded, a great multitude of devout Greeks, not a few leading women; the envious opposition attacking Jason's house; and the accusation that names the sermon — “these who have turned the world upside down have come here too.”

Matthew 16:18 — “Upon this rock I will build My church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail.” Military language: the church on offense against the forces of darkness.

Matthew 6:10 — “Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done on earth.” The prayer of invasion — enforcing the King's will in territory the enemy claims illegally.

Ephesians 6:11 — “Put on the whole armor of God” — Paul's explicit warfare picture of the believer's life.

Mark 16:15; Matthew 28:19-20 — The clear mission from our Commander: go into all the world, preach the gospel to every creature, make disciples, teach obedience.

Romans 1:16; 10:14 — The gospel is the power of God unto salvation — but it must be spoken: “How shall they hear without a preacher?”

Matthew 5:13-14 — Salt of the earth, light of the world: a presence that changes every environment it enters — that, too, is warfare.

Matthew 10:34 — “I did not come to bring peace but a sword.” The gospel's call from idols to Christ is confrontational by nature.

2 Timothy 1:7 — “God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind” — the antidote to the fear of being disliked.

Acts 4:13 — Uneducated, common men who astonished the rulers — because “they had been with Jesus.” The one essential credential.

John 16:7; 1 John 4:4 — It is better that He went: the Spirit now lives in us — no distance, the Greater One inside — so we can be on fire all the time.

Acts 11:26 — The disciples were first called Christians at Antioch — named by unbelievers who observed their Christ-likeness.

Summary 2 of 5

Main Points & Teachings

The church is a military base, not a spa — We gather for four purposes: to be trained in the art of spiritual war, equipped with weapons, strengthened to endure, and deployed to the battlefield. Everything on the base — hospital, mess hall, recreation — exists to return soldiers to the fight, not to replace it.

The mission is clear — Wars fail without a clear mission (the Iraq illustration). Ours could not be clearer: go into all the world, preach the gospel, make disciples, teach obedience — starting where you are: Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, the uttermost parts.

We enforce a victory already won — Believers don't fight for victory but from it: two weapons — the authority of prayer, and the proclamation of Jesus. In every city they turned upside down, they prayed and they preached Jesus.

Everyone is a preacher — Preaching is not the pulpit. Every time you open your mouth to tell people about Jesus, you are preaching — and that word carries the power of God to save.

Ordinary people qualify — The world-turners were fishermen, tax collectors, small business people who never saw a seminary. God doesn't require education or wealth; sometimes the more education, the less available we become. The one essential is being with Jesus.

The match principle — A match hardly weighs anything, but on fire it can burn down a forest. The question is never your size, gifting, or education — only: are you on fire?

Set yourself on fire by being with Jesus — And because the Spirit now lives inside us, we can be with Him — and therefore on fire — all the time. The fire is there because I'm in Christ; I find it by being conscious of His presence.

The world names real Christians — Unbelievers coined both descriptions: “world-turners” (Thessalonica) and “Christians” (Antioch). When you're on fire, you don't have to announce it — your life is so wrapped up in Jesus that the world concludes it for you.

They went — Transformation happened in the world, not in the meeting. They entered cities, homes, and lives; they didn't wait for Thessalonica to come to them on Sunday morning. There is always another person, another place, another time.

Three reasons they went — They believed (hell is real, Jesus is the only way, people cannot be saved unless they hear); they cared for people enough to trouble them with good news; and they obeyed — they did not own themselves; He owned them.

Summary 3 of 5

Principles & Warnings

Principle — Every provision of the church (healing, feeding, fellowship, recreation) is for the battle: it restores soldiers to deployment, never replaces the mission.

Principle — The gospel is inherently confrontational: it calls people from idols to the living God, so faithful witness always produces a reaction — attraction or opposition, but never neutrality.

Principle — Being with Jesus is the sole indispensable credential for world-changing impact; everything else (education, wealth, natural gifting) is optional.

Principle — The fire is maintained, not just caught: consciousness of Christ's indwelling presence — cultivated morning by morning and moment by moment — keeps a believer burning regardless of feelings.

Principle — Authentic Christianity is recognized, not announced: the world itself will name what it observes.

Warning — Beware consumer Christianity: choosing and staying in a church based on how much it caters to you turns a military base into a spa.

Warning — Beware the permanent hospital bed: some stay “wounded” forever as an excuse never to return to the battlefield. Be healed — quickly — and get back in the fight.

Warning — Beware over-feeding without deployment: spiritual food that is never spent in battle makes you a heavier burden, not a stronger soldier.

Warning — Beware the excessive desire to be liked: it will quietly persuade you never to trouble anyone with the gospel. Don't let fear keep you off the battlefield.

Warning — Beware seeking from the church what only heaven provides: “Anybody seeking comfort with no devil to fight — heaven is far more comfortable than here. God has you here for a purpose.”

Summary 4 of 5

Takeaways & Declarations

1. Change your metaphor — Stop treating church as your spa or retreat center. Show up as a soldier: to be trained, equipped, strengthened, and deployed.

2. Get healed to get back — If you're wounded, receive ministry — but pray to recover quickly, and return to the battlefield rather than settling into the hospital bed.

3. Start preaching — You are a preacher. Open your mouth about Jesus this week — at work, at home, in your community.

4. Set yourself on fire daily — Begin each day alone with Jesus: worship, the Word, spoken acknowledgment of His presence. Then keep short prayers and praise going all day, returning gently whenever you drift.

5. Go — Don't wait for the world to come to church. There is always another person, another family, another place. Make the most of every opportunity.

6. Give and pray for senders and sent ones — The pastors deployed from this congregation to Ivory Coast were saved, trained, and financially supported by this church: your giving and praying wage real war.

Prophetic prayer for the church — “Oh Lord, that we might be a church with the reputation that everywhere we go, we turn things around. We turn people around. We turn systems around. We turn cultures around. May our presence and our influence be that powerful.”

Corporate declaration — “God has not given me a spirit of fear, but has given to me a spirit of power, love, and a sound mind. The Holy Ghost has come upon me, and I have received power to be His witness.”

The consecration — “Lord, today I accept the call. I bring myself, I present myself — my time, my gifts, my substance — I present it to You, for the war. I consecrate myself for the war. I show up to be trained, to be equipped, to be strengthened, because I'm deployed on mission.”

### Relationships Are Worship

So let me answer the question about why I think Jesus would say to do what He did. And then I will give you what I call the five R's — five R's for a really complete, full apology, based upon what I see in Scripture.

Okay. So Jesus says to you: when you come to bring your offering to worship, He says leave the offering at the altar and go and try to fix it. Try to make peace. You did something. He said the person has something against you — you did something, or you said something that you know you should not have said or done, or you could have done something different. He's saying: listen, that is important. Don't just leave that.

So now, when He says "when you come to worship," what I believe He's saying is: we are to love God with all our heart, all our mind — and then to love our neighbor as ourselves. And I believe what Jesus is saying is that, as important as raising our hands and singing songs and giving our gifts is — and those are very important, we should do that — He is saying worship is more than that. He is saying that your relationship with people, how you treat people, how you talk to people, the way you deal with people, is also worship.

And in fact, that kind of worship is even *greater* than the worship we do when we come to church. Now, He didn't say don't come to the altar. He didn't say don't bring your offering. He didn't say don't come to church. But He's saying: you know what, as wonderful as this is — and you ought to do it — if you're really, really serious about worshiping God in spirit and in truth, you will not minimize your relationships with people. You will not allow relationships to be broken and just not bother to fix them if you can. You will not do wrong, speak wrong, talk to people wrong, and feel, "I don't have to say I'm sorry, I wronged you." He says no.

One of the highest forms of worship is loving and caring and treating one another right. So there's not a choice between worshiping God and doing that. It's recognizing that worship encompasses all we do. And one of the areas that He really wants us to pay attention to is how we treat one another. Remember, He said: if you can't love the person you can see, how will you love the God you cannot see?

So when you're saying "I'm sorry" — that's real worship. When you're saying "will you forgive me?" — man, that's worship at the next level. So let's worship. Let's worship in spirit and in truth. Let's come to church, let's bring our offerings, let's bring our tithes, let's lift up our holy hands, let's serve God in any way possible. But remember: one of the most important ways of worship is the way we treat one another. Each person is made in the image of God. And so how you treat a person who's made in the image of God is how you treat God.

### The Five R's of a Complete Apology

So quickly, let me give you five R's. Now, this is where — if you were to say, "Okay, a complete, full apology that has all the elements in it" — here they are. And all of us should try to grow and perfect this. We need to become experts at saying "I'm sorry, I was wrong." Here are the elements, five of them.

**One: express regret.** Genuine regret. Not "I'm sorry if I hurt you" — that's not genuine. "I'm sorry that I did this. I said this, and I'm genuinely sorry. I could have handled this differently. And because I did this or did that, it cost you this." Express real sorrow. Often it's more than simply "I'm sorry." Most people want a little bit more than that.

**Secondly: take or accept responsibility for what you did.** Don't say, "I was wrong, *but* you shouldn't have done this." "I was wrong, *but* the way I was feeling that day, you should not have provoked me." Whatever it is — once you put "but," it means you're about to raise a defense. You're setting up your argument against. You're about to prove why you're not as bad as she thinks. And usually "but" is strong enough to cancel everything that comes before it. So when you say "I'm sorry, but…" you just cancelled everything you said before. So accepting responsibility means you don't try to justify your actions. Determine what you said that was wrong, what you did that was wrong, and accept responsibility. Make no excuse.

**Third: offer restitution when possible.** Most of the time we don't get to do this, or we don't offer to do this. But when you look at the biblical narrative, the third thing that should be part of a full apology is for you to offer restitution when possible. I mean, if because of what you did you took something from someone — yeah, I say I'm sorry, take responsibility, but then ask them: "Is there anything I can do? How can I make this better, fix what I did? How can I give you back what I took from you, if it's possible?" Most people will appreciate the fact that you even want to make restitution. They may say, "Don't worry about it, that's fine." But the fact that you take responsibility enough that you want to make restitution…

I'm going to use money, because it's just easy to illustrate it with money. If I lend you money and you agreed, and you're not able to do it, you need to tell me. Don't just say, "Oh, I'm sorry," and then not tell me how you plan to — okay, you weren't able to do it, but this is what I'm working on, or this is why. In other words, unless the person releases you from the responsibility to give back what was given to you, you have to carry a sense of responsibility: it is my responsibility to make restitution. Talk to the person. Many people say, "That's okay, that's fine, I understand, you didn't mean it." But don't feel that you don't have a responsibility to do everything within your power to make right what you did that was wrong.

I use money just because it's easy, but it's anything. If you embarrass somebody because of the way you talked to them before people — restitution would mean that you've got to call the people before whom you did the embarrassment and say to them, "Listen, I said this, I said this, I was wrong. That lady didn't deserve it." You follow me? So you're trying to restore her dignity, because you took something from her by the way you talked.

**Fourth: commit to repentance or reformation.** Commit to change. "Okay, this behavior was wrong. The way I spoke was wrong. And God being my helper, I am going to change this type of behavior. I will not do this again, in Jesus' name." John the Baptist said you should bring forth fruits worthy of repentance. Hallelujah.

**And then lastly: request forgiveness.** Learn to say, "I did wrong. I take responsibility for it." But don't assume that just because you confess, the person is forced to forgive you. You go ahead and request it: "Will you forgive me?" or "Please forgive me." And then it's up to that person to say yes, I forgive you — or not. If they refuse to forgive you, and you know you have done everything you know to do to express it, then you've got to leave it with her and God, or with him and God. But just make sure you have gone through a true and sincere expression of your sorrow as a result of what you said or did.

### Go and Do It

So then, if we're willing to offer forgiveness, and we know how to receive forgiveness, then God's will can be done, and we can start operating in peace and in harmony.

This is not just a message to give you knowledge or information. This is something you're supposed to act upon. There may be someone, or people, who you know you said things to or did things to, and you have maybe justified it as a reaction to some wrong that they did. **The wrong they did doesn't justify any wrong you did.** If you need to go back and say, "Listen, I said this, I did this, I was angry, but I was wrong. And I want to say I'm sorry. Will you forgive me?" — go ahead and do it. You're doing the will of God.

And if somebody has said "I'm sorry" but you have refused to accept it — go ahead now and be gracious. Don't demand perfection. If you know that they're trying, go ahead and extend the forgiveness, make room for possible reconciliation. And where you need to talk to them to explain certain things, do it with the spirit of meekness and gentleness. But be merciful. Be gentle. And be peaceable. In Jesus' name.
